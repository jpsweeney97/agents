# Agent-Facing-Design Philosophy Audit

**Date:** 2026-07-02  
**Telos (yardstick):** Spend gating discipline, structure, and review weight in proportion to how expensive a mistake is to undo (blast radius, reversibility, real damage), rather than applying uniform process to every change.

Two-stage multi-agent audit (`philosophy-audit` workflow, Sonnet 5). Stage 1 drafted candidate teloi from your artifacts; you chose reversibility-weighted scrutiny. Stage 2 reconstructed the philosophy (espoused vs in-use), judged each principle with a 4-lens foreign panel plus a dedicated negation adversary, ran a parallel omission hunt, synthesized, and had two outside critics attack the synthesis.

## Run metadata

- **Principles judged:** 53
- **Agents:** 328 spawned · 324 completed · 4 errored (schema-validation retry-cap failures on lens judges — bounded impact: thinned panels on ~4 principles)
- **Subagent tokens:** ~10.3M · **Duration:** ~70 min · **Model:** claude-sonnet-5
- **Verdicts:** KEEP 10 · REWRITE 30 · GROUND 11 · MERGE 1 · RESOLVE 1
- **Negation survived (not defeated):** 11 of 53 principles

**Verdict legend:** KEEP = earns its place as written · REWRITE = right direction, needs blast-radius scaling/carve-outs · GROUND = right but asserted, needs its mechanism stated · MERGE = fold into existing text · RESOLVE = collision resolved into a scoped default. `negationDefeated:false` = a live counter-force survived; these needed the deepest restructuring.

## Evidence Boundary

This is a text/design-level audit, not a behavioral one: no finding cites an observed session where a skill actually misfired, drifted, or was misrouted. Claims about the corpus (file counts, duplication, "no skill states X") were produced by the sub-agents and were **not independently re-verified in this pass** — and at least one such claim (M43) was found overstated when checked. Citations are file-level, not `path:line`. The evidence base is skewed to the meta/governance layer (roughly 15–20 of ~76 skills read in depth; the rest largely unsampled). The fullest statement of limits is the Honesty Appendix and the two Critic sections below — read them before acting on any single finding. True at `reviewed_commit`; re-verify against live source.

## Headline

Only **9 of 53 principles survived in their original wording.** The dominant outcome is REWRITE (30) and GROUND (11). The single recurring defect: the philosophy's instincts are almost always directionally correct but are stated as **flat, uniform rules that themselves violate the telos** — which demands scaling by blast radius. 'Skills are low-gate', 'ambient is high-gate', 'third-party is always gated', 'structure only if it provokes thinking', 'net-new obligation → stricter gate' were all written categorically and had to be rewritten into blast-radius-scaled versions with explicit carve-outs.

## The critics' verdict (the honest limitation — read first)

Both outside critics independently caught the audit committing the circularity we set out to avoid: it graded the philosophy against a copy of itself.

### Critic 1

**Likeliest reviewer bias:** The dominant, telling verdict pattern is the near-total absence of clean KEEP or DELETE outcomes: of 53 ledger items, only 9 survived in their original wording, ~27 were REWRITE and ~10 GROUND, and the audit's own output adds a 15-section 'restructured philosophy' plus 10 brand-new proposed principles/machinery (single-sourcing scans, collision detectors, bloat audits, falsification channels, circularity-escape gates, independent-verification requirements, idempotency requirements, interface contracts, etc.). A reviewer genuinely applying 'spend structure only in proportion to blast radius' as a live constraint on itself, rather than only as a lens for judging the corpus, would be expected to find a non-trivial fraction of rules that should simply be cut, left alone, or judged not worth the ceremony to even discuss. Instead every tension resolves toward 'add a carve-out / add scaling language / add a new gate.' This is the signature of reviewers drawn from a sibling of the same reversibility-maximalist frame: elaboration reads as rigor, and simplification/deletion is never the satisfying move, so the ledger systematically converges on REWRITE-with-more-nuance even in cases (M6, M17, M27, M36, M41 — the ones explicitly marked negationDefeated:false) where the corrected version is still just an assertion dressed as a resolution.

**What the review missed:** The review never turns its own central test — 'machinery is earned only by itemized, real, cross-instance-evidenced damage, not generic caution' (M19, M27, M36) — back on itself. It rigorously demands that every rule in the corpus justify its existence against demonstrated failure, yet the audit's own prescribed remedy is a large net expansion of ambient machinery: a 53-item ledger, a fully rewritten 15-section philosophy, and a 10-item principlesToAdd list (nearest-neighbor collision detectors, scheduled bloat audits, falsification channels, circularity-escape requirements, independent-verification gates, idempotency requirements, interface contracts). By the honesty appendix's own admission, none of the underlying M-items in this ledger cite an actual observed session transcript where a skill misfired, drifted, or was silently misrouted — the entire audit is structural/source-level analysis judged against the corpus's own proof-boundary standard (M43), not behavioral proof. So the review builds a large, sticky, hard-to-undo addition to the ambient instruction surface (exactly the class it defines as maximally gated and requiring the heaviest admission bar, per M1/M5/M35/M40) while exempting its own recommendation set from that same admission bar. It also never asks whether the review process itself — the labor, the review-fatigue, the risk of the added machinery drifting or contradicting itself the way M39/M40 warn ambient documents can — is proportionate to the near-zero demonstrated incident rate it started from. Applying the telos reflexively to the artifact the telos itself produced is the missing step.

**Undefeated negation:** 'Reversibility/blast-radius is the correct master variable for gating weight' is presupposed as the organizing axis for all 53 adjudications, including several (M7, M12, M17, M20, M35) explicitly re-derived onto that axis when their original stated justification was something else entirely (authorship coherence, cognitive-routing-surface cost, generic caution). Its negation — that gating should instead be driven primarily by who bears the cost of a mistake and who has standing to accept the risk (a principal-agent framing), or by actual measured frequency×severity, or by how reliable the review step itself is (a review-time-cost framing that would sometimes recommend *less* structure even for irreversible actions if the review is unreliable or the reviewer lacks authority) — is named once, in the honesty appendix, as a rival framing 'never itself tested.' But naming a counter-frame as an unexamined caveat is not the same as attempting to defeat it: no M-item stages an actual test of blast-radius-primacy against principal-agent-primacy or frequency-primacy anywhere in the ledger; the rubric used to judge every item already assumes the conclusion. The negation therefore survives fully intact, not because it was weighed and found wanting, but because the adjudication apparatus was never capable of putting it on trial in the first place.

### Critic 2

**Likeliest reviewer bias:** The panel operates entirely inside the practitioner's own opening axiom: M3 ("reversibility is the currency... never subjective importance") was asserted at the start, and all 53 subsequent verdicts were produced by asking "does this scale with reversibility," never "is reversibility the right thing to scale by." Because the adjudicating apparatus (scrutinize-style challenge, GROUND/REWRITE verdicts, the charter itself) is drawn from the same corpus and already encodes reversibility-proportionality as its own organizing principle, the panel is grading the corpus against a copy of itself. That collapses "is this the correct master variable" into "is this master variable applied consistently" — a scope-narrowing that looks like rigor (uniform, non-arbitrary, resistant to vibes-based gating) but is actually the bias: reversibility survived not because it beat rival framings in a contest, but because it was never entered into one. The honestyAppendix names this gap explicitly yet the restructuredPhilosophy still crowns reversibility as "THE SPINE" — diagnosis without correction.

**What the review missed:** The review never asks who bears the cost of a mistake versus who decided to risk it, and that omission changes concrete verdicts, not just tone. Reversibility asks one question — "how cheaply can a bad state be detected and undone" — and that question is answerer-centric: it measures cost to the agent/operator's own system state. It is structurally blind to harms that leave the operator's rollback-cost untouched but land on someone else without their consent — e.g. a fully reversible action (delete your working notes, no trace, zero undo-cost) that nonetheless read private third-party data, or a third-party skill whose provenance is untrustworthy but whose blast radius is scope-local and cheap to prune (M6-corrected explicitly waives gating "merely by origin" — under an accountability telos, origin is exactly the relevant variable, since the question becomes who vouches for this code, not how easy is it to delete). Swap in a principal-agent/accountability telos and M6/M7's third-party gating inverts its trigger condition; swap in a frequency×severity actuarial telos and M25's mandatory defense-in-depth for catastrophic/irreversible cases gets reallocated toward high-frequency/moderate-severity failures instead, since expected-value weighting doesn't reserve redundancy for worst-case-only; swap in a reviewer-reliability telos (the one the honestyAppendix names but never runs through the corpus's own verdict machinery) and several M43/M47-style mandatory proof-disclosure and structured-packet requirements could reverse into *less* structure for high-blast-radius items specifically because verbose proof invites the rubber-stamping the appendix worries about in this very document. None of these alternate teloi are being proposed as correct — the point is each one relocates the gate in a materially different place than reversibility does, and the ledger's 53 items were never re-run against any of them.

**Undefeated negation:** "Cost-to-undo is the correct master variable for gating decisions" — its negation, "who bears the cost and who consented to risking it is prior to how cheaply the outcome can be undone," is explicitly raised in the honestyAppendix as an untested rival framing but is never actually subjected to the corpus's own adversarial-review standard for high-consequence self-amendment (pre-registration, blind/cross-model arms, external anchor — the exact bar the appendix itself invokes and says this document fails to clear). Naming a counter-force and running it are different acts; only the first happened. The negation therefore survives ungrounded and undefeated, which means every "GROUND" verdict in the ledger that re-derived a practitioner intuition strictly from the reversibility axis (M7, M12, M17, M20, M35) is only as sound as reversibility's unproven primacy — if the accountability or actuarial framing is closer to correct, those five "grounded" items were re-derived from the wrong axis and would need to be re-run, not merely re-worded.

### Honesty appendix (from the synthesis itself)

Inside-the-frame risk: this entire synthesis, including this appendix, adjudicates every finding through the philosophy's own master lens — blast radius / reversibility / cost-to-undo. That lens was never itself tested against a rival organizing principle (e.g., a principal-agent framing of who bears the cost of a mistake versus who decides to accept the risk, or a pure frequency×severity actuarial framing, or a review-time-cost framing that would sometimes recommend less structure even for irreversible actions if the review itself is unreliable). Multiple ledger entries (M7, M12, M17, M20, M35) were explicitly 'GROUND'-verdicted by re-deriving them from the reversibility axis when their original stated justification was something else (authorship coherence, cognitive-routing-surface, generic caution) — that move is only valid if reversibility really is the correct master variable and not merely the most available one, given that it's also this practitioner's own stated telos. The candidate-omission about a 'circularity-escape bar' for self-review that amends ambient contracts applies with full force to this document: it was produced single-pass, without blind arms, without an external cold judge, and without pre-registration, exactly the pattern the corpus's own framework-challenge review flagged as insufficient for a review that changes ambient instructions.

Findings that lack fresh evidence in this pass: I did not independently re-run any of the greps, file counts, or corpus-inspection claims the ledger reports (the 18-file proof-boundary duplication count, the 9/59 two-layer-scope fraction, the 15-file undefined-jargon count, the skill-cluster examples for plugin-family formation, the 76-skills/64-eras zero-pruning claim, the THROUGHLINE 40%-growth figure). All of these are taken at face value from the ledger's self-reported verification. M43 itself demonstrates why this matters: one ledger entry's factual claim ('near-verbatim boilerplate across a dozen-plus files') was checked against the live files during its own adjudication and found to be overstated (a shared skeleton, not verbatim duplication) — meaning at least one similar-sounding claim elsewhere in this same ledger could carry the same overstatement risk without having been caught, since not every entry that makes a corpus-count claim documents having actually run the check.

Sample and survivorship: the ledger's evidence base is concentrated on the meta/governance layer — charter.md, AGENTS.md, agent-facing-design, writing-principles, review-family, git-cycle — because that is where gating decisions live and where an adversarial audit naturally goes looking. Named as evidence across the entire 53-item ledger are perhaps 15-20 of the corpus's roughly 76 skills; the remaining 55-60 workhorse skills (dependency-upgrade, tdd, prototype, simplify-code, explain-codebase, and dozens more) are essentially unsampled — cited, if at all, only via a single grep hit rather than a full read. That skews every claim about 'the corpus does X uniformly' or 'no skill states Y' toward false confidence: a claim like 'no espoused principle governs skill-output presentation' (a divergence finding) is falsifiable by any workhorse skill this audit didn't read, and 'every skill closes with a field:value packet' was itself corrected mid-ledger (M47) precisely because a full-corpus check showed exploratory skills don't. This is a text/design audit, not a behavioral one: no entry cites an actual observed session transcript where a skill misfired, drifted, or was silently misrouted — the M4 unattended-invocation risk, the M8 advisory-lane misroute risk, and the M30 stateless-drift risk are all argued from tool-list existence and structural possibility, not from a recorded incident. Under the corpus's own proof-boundary standard (M43), this entire audit would have to disclose itself as structural/source-level analysis, not behavioral proof — an irony the audit should own rather than let pass silently.

## Espoused vs. in-use divergences

- CLAUDE.md's safety rule is narrow and shell-specific (never `rm`, use `trash`). Actual practice generalizes this into a pervasive reversibility law applied far beyond shell commands: staged-commit-only defaults, protected-branch detection, dirty-worktree checks, and 'stop and ask' gates before any consequential write, plus loop-termination logic that is licensed to be aggressive specifically because every stop is engineered to be safe. The corpus never states this generalized law anywhere — only the narrow shell rule and the narrow contract-gating reversibility axis (M3) are stated.
- CLAUDE.md states one blanket evidentiary rule for the whole conversation. In practice the skill corpus turned this into a formal, elaborately maintained taxonomy of proof classes (structural/source vs. behavior/runtime/install/cache/marketplace) repeated as near-verbatim boilerplate across a dozen-plus SKILL.md files, with every closing report required to name what was and wasn't verified.
- The stated communication rule targets chat replies to a low-context human. The actual skill artifacts do the opposite: they coin and reuse dense, undefined technical vocabulary across dozens of files, written for a peer agent assumed to already carry the whole library's shared terms.
- The charter declares skills/commands low-ceremony and disposable, with no admission test or gate required to build or prune one. The actual authoring discipline around any material skill edit routes through a multi-skill pre-flight (context-vs-machinery gate, obligation-by-obligation writing challenge, phased UX audit) that is procedurally heavy in practice, even though none of it is logged as a charter event.
- CLAUDE.md's error-format convention is explicitly scoped to Python production code. The corpus promotes the identical string shape into a general design law for any agent-facing tool return or error, well outside Python or exceptions.
- The espoused trust-skill bar explicitly demands machinery be 'single-sourced rather than copied.' The corpus's own proof-class-separation logic — arguably the single most load-bearing piece of machinery in the library — violates that exact bar: it is duplicated as near-verbatim boilerplate across a dozen-plus SKILL.md files rather than factored into one shared reference the rest could point to.
- No espoused principle anywhere governs how a skill should present its output. Yet the corpus enforces a strict, unstated convention — every skill closes with a compact labelled field:value packet in a fenced block, treated as important enough to be consumed both by a scanning human and by the next skill in a chain.
- No espoused principle addresses whether meta-tooling — the act of constructing skills themselves — should be automated. The practitioner nonetheless holds a considered, consistently-applied position: keep it manual and hand-authored on Claude specifically, while automating it on Codex, 'by design.' This deliberate asymmetry is acted on but never stated as a rule anywhere in the espoused material.
- No espoused principle states that verdict-rendering should be a deliberate partitioning axis for skill design. Yet the corpus consistently splits skills into 'renders no verdict' (exploratory/adversarial) versus 'renders exactly one verdict' (decision-owning) as an unstated but rigorously applied design law that goes beyond the stated 'one owner per job' principle.

- Add single-sourcing/canonical-pointer discipline for any obligation restated in more than one contract, plus a periodic corpus-wide scan for the drift this produces. The predicted failure has already happened: near-identical proof-boundary language is independently worded across 18+ files despite the corpus's own trust-skill bar explicitly naming 'the same gate hand-copied into four skills, drifting out of sync' as a defect, and despite one working counterexample already existing (AGENTS.md pointing to agent-facing-design as the canonical judgment/trust gate) that was never generalized into a rule.
- Add a nearest-neighbor collision check triggered on adding/editing a skill near an existing semantic cluster, since the only sanctioned collision detector (competing fires in a transcript) is structurally blind to a single plausible-but-suboptimal skill being chosen among overlapping neighbors (scrutinize/red-team/premortem/working-slice-review) with no second candidate ever surfacing.
- Add a scheduled aggregate bloat/usage audit for both the always-loaded instruction surface (AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md, which only ever grows by well-justified per-line admissions with no aggregate sweep) and build-and-prune inventories/append-only ledgers generally — evidenced concretely by 76 skills accumulated over 64 eras with zero pruning and no usage sensor, and THROUGHLINE.md growing 40% in two weeks before a size budget was retrofitted, both caught only by a one-off adversarial challenge rather than a standing threshold.
- Require a pre-committed, dated falsification or re-test channel at the moment any ambient value-theory belief is installed into always-loaded instructions — not deferred until a later adversarial challenge happens to surface the gap. Concrete precedent: a cognitive-offload claim was promoted to load-bearing, its metric declared unconstructible, then installed verbatim into AGENTS.md as an unfalsifiable prior discounting a specific counterargument, and remains unamended post-adjudication.
- Require a circularity-escape step (pre-registration, blind/cross-model arms, or an external human anchor) before any self-review's verdict is allowed to amend ambient instructions, size budgets, or ledger content — the corpus already owns a purpose-built methodology for this but a high-consequence self-review chose not to run it and no rule requires it to.
- Require independent (not self-authored) verification before admitting a contract into the one class explicitly declared unobservable ('no visible fire, you cannot watch it mis-fire') — currently Admission is answered entirely by the same party proposing the rule, which is the standard reliability-engineering gap (no second checker) for exactly the failure mode this class is defined by.
- Add an idempotency/safe-retry requirement for multi-step agent execution loops (execute-plan, migration-campaign, keep-green): whether a step, if interrupted and re-run from last known state, is safe to redo or silently double-applies. Nothing in the charter's reversibility analysis currently asks 'what happens if this exact step runs twice,' only 'can the outcome be undone.'
- Add an explicit minimal interface contract at skill-to-skill handoff boundaries — what a downstream skill is entitled to assume from an upstream skill's output — since the corpus is thorough about who owns a job but has no vocabulary for what crosses the seam, forcing re-derivation or drift at every handoff.
- State explicitly, as a general authoring rule (not five hand-copied instances), the baseline-comparison justification test already implicitly used by ideate/premortem/red-team/scope-cut/steelman: name what a capable agent already does when simply asked, then state what the skill guarantees the bare attempt drops under load, plus its one distinct product. Currently this exists only as unwritten convention despite the charter explicitly waiving any admission test for skills.
- Reconcile the auto-commit-vs-leave-for-review decision for Markdown-writing skills into one explicit rule rather than each skill deciding independently — CLAUDE.md's blanket auto-commit default is directly contradicted by grill-with-docs and improve-codebase-architecture ('do not commit, leave for user review'), with no document stating which behavior applies when.

## Load-bearing core (survived challenge)

- Reversibility is the currency: how gated a contract or action is turns on whether a bad version is caught cheaply and undone cleanly — never on subjective importance or how 'safety-sounding' it feels (M3).
- Skills/commands default to zero-gate build-and-prune (visible fire, trashable artifact); always-loaded ambient contracts, hooks, and third-party contract text default to charter-gated — but the skill exemption breaks for skills that are unattended-invokable (cron/remote-trigger/subagent-dispatched, no human reading the transcript) or that wield irreversible-effect tools (send/merge/force-push/delete), which must be gated by capability, not storage format (M1, M4-corrected).
- Ambient default-high-gate is grounded in detection latency (no invocation log, no single exercise point to confirm safe removal), not mere 'entanglement' asserted as fact; it is a floor, not a flat plateau — legible, self-contained, low-consequence ambient prose can discount toward lighter gating once checked (M5-corrected).
- Third-party material is always gated only when it becomes ambient/always-loaded contract text — because blast radius there is measured at the level of compounding, hard-to-detect cross-session influence, not artifact-deletion cost; scope-local or mechanically-checkable third-party material (a build-and-prune skill, a typo fix, a pinned-hash bump) is not swept in merely by origin (M6-corrected).
- Every running ambient contract must be first-party-authored or deliberately re-adopted; third-party material is source to mine, never infrastructure to run as-is, grounded in ambient auto-load's uncapped blast radius and only-partial reversibility (deletion doesn't retroactively un-shape sessions already steered) (M7-grounded).
- When mining third-party contract-text material, fold reusable discipline into a locally-authored contract and remove the original regardless of yield — an un-integrated leftover becomes a second, unvetted ambient source — with a narrow carve-out for attribution/license-obligated material (keep a citation stub outside any always-loaded surface, never the live instruction text) (M13-grounded).
- Consult the charter before authoring/retiring a gated surface stays a category trigger (never let an agent self-score severity to skip it) — but the weight of the consult scales with true reversibility of the specific edit: a narrow, fully-reversible tweak to an existing gated surface needs only a fast skim; authoring a new always-loaded rule or third-party import needs full Admission plus ledger entry (M2-corrected).
- Admission of a gated contract requires naming the specific unowned work and the specific misroute lighter context wouldn't catch (M10); absence-of-inbound-route alone is never sufficient evidence, but the size of the required observed-work pattern and review weight scale with reversibility — a cheap, prunable candidate needs one instance and light review; an ambient, hard-to-reverse candidate needs the fuller repeated-misroute pattern plus heavier review (M11-corrected).
- A contract that cannot name the work it owns gets no slot — grounded not in bare cognitive-routing-surface cost but in the fact that unscoped ambient contracts are exactly the expensive-to-undo tier, and 'what does this own' is the same question needed to safely retire it later; session-scoped build-and-prune artifacts are exempt from this gate's full rigor (M12-grounded).
- Retirement ceremony should match an artifact's blast radius as it currently stands, not the bucket it was filed in at admission (a skill can drift into de facto ambient use; a gated contract can sit unused and low-risk) — re-tier at retirement time; regardless of tier, every routing surface pointing to the retired artifact must be updated or removed in the same change (M14-grounded).
- Decision-ledger weight should scale with cost-to-undo: cheap/local/reversible admits need only a date and outcome; hard-to-undo, wide-blast-radius, or likely-to-resurface rejects/parks get the full record (date, surface, outcome, evidence, and an explicit reopen trigger) so expensive settled calls aren't re-litigated and parked items don't silently rot (M15-corrected).
- Overlap between co-loaded contracts is resolved by curation — a same-file negative 'Use when...not...' clause naming the exact sibling — never by an out-of-band precedence table, because such tables rot invisibly as the roster grows while a same-file clause cannot go stale without an edit that also breaks that skill's own description; apply uniformly since per-pair curation is cheap and the collision risk concentrates in the advisory/judgment lane where a silent misroute produces an undetected wrong artifact (M8-grounded).
- Delivery-mode facts (auto-fire vs invoke, recall vs precision, timing) are strong evidence toward a blast-radius judgment, never a mechanical substitute for one in either direction: two delivery variants of one substantive check should share a spec to prevent drift, but a manually-invoked, calm-looking action can still be catastrophic and must be gated on its own actual reversibility (M9-corrected).
- With no existing target and no concrete proposal, don't run the full design gate as an abstract exercise — name the likely work product and ask one calibrated question, or hand off; this is itself the proportionate move, not a shortcut around gating (M21).
- If a user explicitly requests machinery the gate doesn't justify, never silently substitute a lighter design — state the objection, propose the alternative, ask first, because a silent downgrade converts a private, possibly-wrong risk judgment into an unannounced change to the deliverable (M22).
- A design-justification gate decides only whether structure is justified, then hands off to the owning workflow carrying its structured risk rationale (not a bare verdict) — it must never itself perform the construction, review, or audit that follows, and skills must self-police mid-execution drift into a neighboring skill's territory (M33-corrected).
- Passing a design-justification test licenses only the specific guard being decided, not a whole framework built around it; a generalization (shared scaffolding, tiers, exemption workflows) requires its own justification test against its own blast radius — demonstrated recurrence, not extrapolation from one approved instance (M34).
- Before adding structure to an existing artifact, inspect the live target and nearby controlling authority rather than reasoning abstractly, because inspection is cheap relative to the cost of structure that gets consumed and becomes load-bearing before anyone notices a conflict; skip or bound inspection only where the inspection itself is the expensive step (M20-grounded).
- Default to context (guidance the agent can weigh, ignore, or you can delete at zero cost); reach for machinery only in proportion to how expensive the prevented mistake would be to undo — and machinery inherits this same proportionality test rather than an exemption from it, because machinery is itself the sticky, hard-to-undo artifact class once other agents depend on its presence (M16-corrected).
- The context checklist (examples, boundaries, counterexamples, recoverable state, ownership, structured evidence, preconditions, failure behavior) isn't owed uniformly: recoverable state and ownership are direct proxies for reversibility and accountability and should never be skipped; the other six sharpen judgment once stakes are known and should scale in depth with blast radius (M17-grounded).
- Machinery means decision-forcing structure — validators, routers, classifiers, scoring, confidence fields, hard rules, and any required field whose value drives a downstream branch or gate — and explicitly excludes purely technical/bookkeeping fields (timestamps, IDs) that carry no judgment content, so the taxonomy doesn't over-flag inert schema or, by crying wolf, get routed around entirely (M18-corrected).
- Add machinery only when a wrong value/step causes real, itemized damage (deletion, credential exposure, corrupted state, broken recovery, stale authority, unsafe actions, security failure, lost trust) — never generic caution — but treat this list as open, not exhaustive: unenumerated irreversible harm is itemized too, and gating weight should never be inferred from a domain-bucket gloss ('only commit/deletion/authority skills need weight') without re-auditing the actual corpus (M19-corrected).
- Judgment skills are graded on uplift-to-thinking and trust skills on reliable/repeatable execution — but which bar a skill's *output* earns is set by what that output is actually trusted to do: a judgment skill wired into an autonomous downstream action inherits trust-tier gating; a trust skill whose worst action is fully reversible does not need catastrophic-tier scrutiny merely because it executes (M23-corrected).
- In critique/judgment-output skills, structure is justified only if it provokes thinking that wouldn't otherwise happen; this test does not apply to control/safety-interlock skills guarding an irreversible live action, where judgment-independent gates (pre-registered abort thresholds, mandatory second-approver sign-off, automatic rollback triggers) are correct and must not be cut for 'failing to provoke thinking' (M24-corrected).
- A too-weak forcing function is fixed with a single sharper, unbypassable check for low/moderate-stakes mistakes; for catastrophic or effectively-irreversible mistakes, one sharpened gate is not sufficient — a lone choke point is a single point of failure, so the proportionate fix is deliberately redundant, independently-sourced layers stacked on top of the sharpened gate (M25-corrected).
- Trust-skill machinery is judged on reliability plus single-sourcing the mechanism (not duplicating the same gate's logic across skills, which drifts) — but single-sourcing means one mechanism with severity parameterized per call site by blast-radius tier, not one uniform threshold applied everywhere (M26-grounded).
- 'A capable model would do this anyway' is sufficient grounds to cut a trust skill only for low-blast-radius, cheaply-reversible actions; for high-blast-radius, hard-to-reverse actions it is not — there, demonstrate measured offload or name the specific failure mode under degraded conditions the gate catches (M27-corrected).
- Cognitive offload is a qualitative lens on any skill's worth, never a scored gating input, because scoring it invites Goodharting and lets a high offload score get misread as 'safe to gate lightly' even when the skill touches irreversible actions — gate on reversibility and blast radius directly (M28-grounded).
- Apply the judgment/trust bar per-part of a mixed skill, not one whole-skill label; when parts are causally entangled (a 'safe' step feeding a destructive one), the safe part inherits the tier of the riskier sink it feeds (taint-escalation), rather than reverting to a single ceiling label (M29).
- Judgment/trust classification is a contextual lens, not a frozen checklist — but it needs a durable, declared set of blast-radius/reversibility signals to calibrate against across stateless, memory-less sessions, since an anchor-free 'judge it in the moment' regresses to gut-feel and flat scrutiny; skills that set an explicit quality bar subject edits to their own bar-defining section (and to edits to the anchor signals themselves) to that same bar (M30-corrected).
- Design-decision reasoning belongs in chat/working notes by default; write it into the artifact only when high-risk, part of a durable contract, or hard to reconstruct later — routing the durable-write cost only to where losing the reasoning later would itself be expensive (M32).
- Treat every instruction in an agent-facing doc as an obligation a future agent must carry; name the exact obligation-verb (do/decide/avoid/verify/remember/maintain) before touching it — because these docs execute every future session at near-zero marginal cost to silently add or drop a guardrail, making a cheap universal per-instruction check the proportionate floor for this document class specifically (M35-grounded).
- Challenge every obligation against the concrete work it protects and delete what protects nothing real — but scale the deletion evidence-bar to the blast radius of what's guarded: low-stakes obligations can be cut on ordinary judgment, while rare/high-severity/irreversible guardrails require affirmative cross-instance evidence the risk class no longer applies, not merely 'nothing happened this one time' (M36-corrected).
- An obligation earns a concrete trigger/action/boundary/evidence/stop-condition (and loop-termination logic earns a precise failure-identity definition, e.g. identity+normalized-error-class excluding file:line) in proportion to whether its action compounds or is hard to undo; trivial, fully-reversible, single-actor obligations/loops may use a cheap heuristic instead (M37-corrected).
- Don't keep an obligation merely because it exists, and don't 'fix' a bad obligation by polishing its prose into a clearer bad obligation — obligation-set size must actually shrink to what proportional gating requires, or gate fatigue raises the real cost of the next genuine mistake (M38).
- Before editing an instruction document, read the live target plus the nearby controlling authority governing that obligation, to prevent two always-loaded documents from silently asserting incompatible rules (M39).
- Within always-loaded, high-fan-in, agent-facing artifacts specifically, any edit adding a net-new obligation or materially expanding duties/machinery must pass the stricter gate regardless of whether it's phrased as a rule, a worked example, or a boundary clause — because agents follow all three with equal behavioral force and the true blast radius of an added duty compounds invisibly as other sessions come to depend on it; this scope does not extend to low-fan-in, cleanly-revertible artifacts (M40-corrected).
- Edits that only remove/lighten/narrow/clarify an obligation can fast-track past the stricter gate — unless the obligation being loosened is itself a safety, security, compliance, or irreversibility guardrail, in which case it still requires the stricter gate even though it reads as a removal (M41-corrected).
- Review instruction text with the seven-item challenge order (unjustified, vague, unclear, overbuilt, unbounded, false-proof, conflicting) as a fast internal scan, not a mandatory report template — sized to what's actually wrong; if the scan reveals the artifact is more consequential than assumed (ambient, auto-invoked without a human gate, shipped beyond the repo), stop and route to the fuller charter-gated review instead of finishing the checklist (M42-corrected).
- Structural/source validation never substitutes for behavior/certification/sync/install/cache/runtime proof unless a realistic invocation was actually run, and every closing report states what was and wasn't verified — this is the corpus's most consistently instantiated proof discipline, though it is enforced as a shared skeleton wrapping domain-specific bodies, not literal boilerplate (M43-corrected framing).
- Validate agent-facing artifacts by first classifying the edited surface (standalone Markdown, skill-bundle behavior, metadata/routing fields, multi-surface) since surfaces fail differently and a small metadata edit can silently mis-route every future invocation; within a surface, scale scrutiny further by whether the change touches a decision/gate/hard rule versus prose/example (M44-corrected).
- When reporting review-only findings, lead with obligation failures ordered by impact and state the required edit shape, excluding generic writing-quality commentary by default — except for high-blast-radius, ambiguity-sensitive artifacts (runbooks, procedures executed under pressure), where an unclear sentence is itself the obligation failure and must be reported as one (M45-resolved).
- In agent-facing Markdown, write one logical line per paragraph/bullet rather than hard-wrapping at a fixed column, because hard-wrapping makes a single-word edit reflow and diff an entire paragraph — burying the real change and costing reviewers attention disproportionate to what changed, and breaking line-addressed tooling; code blocks, tables, and frontmatter are exempt, and a repo's own convention overrides (M46-grounded).
- Skills whose output gates a downstream action or chains into another skill close with a compact, labelled field:value packet sized to the action's reversibility; read-only or trivially-reversible skills are exempt from forced packet closes, and high-blast-radius closes require an explicit residual-risk field treated as a required human-acknowledgment gate, not a value downstream automation can silently branch past (M47-corrected).
- For git/VCS actions specifically, default to the smallest reversible slice (stop at local commit) and gate push/merge/protected-branch/history-rewrite on explicit protected-branch and dirty-worktree checks, escalating to a question rather than a silent choice; this mechanism is git-specific and does not automatically transfer to other irreversible-action domains (deploys, deletions, external sends), which need their own domain-appropriate hazard signals (M48-corrected).
- Verdict-rendering is partitioned by skill role (exploratory/adversarial skills never render a verdict; decision-owning skills render exactly one) specifically to prevent verdict-laundering and keep the gate singular and legible — but the ceremony behind that one verdict should scale with stakes, requiring an independent second check or human co-sign for high-stakes decision-owning verdicts rather than a uniform solo call (M49-corrected).
- Iterative loops may be licensed aggressive only when every action in their space — not merely their exit conditions — is read-only or freely reversible; any irreversible action upstream of the terminal exit needs its own gate (dry-run, circuit breaker, checkpoint), because exit-safety alone doesn't cover damage that already landed en route (M50-corrected).
- Jargon that functions as a gating trigger — its reading changes whether an agent escalates, asks, or proceeds unilaterally on a hard-to-reverse action — must be defined at first use or linked to a shared glossary; general stylistic shorthand in low-stakes prose is exempt (M51-corrected).
- Skill creation stays manual and judgment-anchored not because skills are the highest-leverage or most-gated artifact in the system (they are explicitly the opposite: build-and-prune, cheap to delete) but because a skill's real risk is behavioral and compounding rather than structural, and that gap between stated scope and actual blast radius is exactly what an automated structural risk-scorer would miss at creation time (M52-corrected).
- Scope should be stated negatively once, in the YAML trigger boundary, by default; add a body-level 'this is not a...' restatement only where the skill sits in a crowded, easily-confused sibling neighborhood or where its actions are expensive/irreversible to undo — not as a universal two-layer requirement (M53-corrected).

## Restructured philosophy (proposed)

TELOS: spend gating discipline, structure, and review weight in proportion to how expensive a mistake is to undo (blast radius, reversibility, real damage) — never uniformly.

0. THE SPINE (missing from the original corpus, now made explicit): this reversibility-proportionality law is not scoped to skill/rule curation — it is a domain-general design tenet independently re-derived in migration-safety, deploy-plan, runbook-authoring, decision-record, and incident-response, and it should be stated once, at the top of the instruction hierarchy, rather than left implicit everywhere it's used.

1. GATING SURFACE (M1-M6, M35, M40, M46, M51). Two defaults, both scaled by actual capability/consequence rather than storage format or category label: skills/commands are zero-gate build-and-prune UNLESS unattended-invokable or wielding irreversible-effect tools, in which case they inherit ambient-tier gating. Ambient/always-loaded contracts default high-gate because their damage has no invocation log and no single exercise point (detection latency, not vague 'entanglement') — but this is a floor a legible, low-stakes ambient line can discount from, not a plateau. Third-party material is gated specifically when it becomes ambient contract text, not by mere origin. Within always-loaded, high-fan-in artifacts, any net-new obligation — rule, example, or boundary clause alike — passes the stricter gate; jargon that functions as a gating trigger must be defined at first use; markdown stays one-logical-line-per-paragraph so a one-word fix doesn't cost a whole-paragraph review.

2. LIFECYCLE DISCIPLINE (M2, M7-M15, M31, M39). Consulting the charter is a category trigger whose weight scales with the specific edit's reversibility. Admission requires naming the owned work and the misroute prevented, with the evidentiary bar (single instance vs. repeated pattern) scaling by reversibility; a contract that can't name its own work gets no slot, because that's the same question needed to retire it safely later. Retirement re-tiers by current blast radius, not filing-time category, and sweeps every routing reference. The decision ledger's depth scales with cost-to-undo. Every gated edit reads the live target plus its nearby controlling authority first, to prevent silent disagreement between two ambient documents. Third-party material is mined and re-authored, never run as-is, and removed after mining regardless of yield (attribution/license material gets a citation stub outside the live instruction text, never a pass).

3. COLLISION AND SCOPE (M8, M9, M20, M53). Overlap is resolved by curation — a same-file negative clause naming the exact sibling — never a precedence table, applied uniformly because per-pair curation is cheap and misroutes concentrate in the undetectable advisory lane. Delivery-mode facts (auto vs. invoke, recall vs. precision) are evidence toward, never a substitute for, a blast-radius judgment. Before adding structure anywhere, inspect the live target and controlling authority — cheap relative to shipped contradictions. Negative scope lives in YAML by default; a body-level second layer is earned only by crowded-neighborhood confusion risk or expensive/irreversible actions.

4. THE GATE'S OWN BOUNDARIES (M21, M22, M33, M34). No target yet: name the likely work product, ask one question, or hand off — don't run full ceremony against a hypothetical. Explicit user request for unjustified machinery: object, propose, ask — never silently downgrade. The gate renders a justification verdict and hands off its risk rationale to the owning workflow; it never becomes the construction or audit. A passed justification licenses only that specific guard — any generalization into shared scaffolding needs its own justification test against demonstrated recurrence.

5. CONTEXT VS. MACHINERY, CORRECTED (M16-M19). Default to context; machinery is earned in proportion to itemized, real, hard-to-reverse damage (an open list, not a closed one) — and machinery itself is sticky once depended-on, so it inherits the same proportionality test rather than escaping it. Machinery means decision-forcing structure specifically (validators, routers, required fields whose value branches downstream); bookkeeping fields are not machinery and don't need justification. Within the context list, recoverable-state and ownership are the load-bearing reversibility/accountability proxies; the rest scale with stakes.

6. JUDGMENT VS. TRUST SKILLS, CORRECTED (M23-M30, M52). Uplift-to-thinking vs. reliable-execution are evaluation methods, not gating-tier assignments — gating tracks what the output actually touches downstream, per-part with taint-escalation for entangled steps. 'Structure only if it provokes thinking' governs critique-output skills; control/safety-interlock skills guarding irreversible actions get judgment-independent gates instead. A too-weak gate is sharpened for ordinary stakes; catastrophic/irreversible stakes get deliberately redundant independent layers, because a single sharp choke point is still a single point of failure. Trust-skill machinery single-sources its mechanism with severity parameterized per call site. 'A capable model would do this anyway' only licenses cutting low-stakes trust skills. Cognitive offload stays a qualitative lens, never a score. The judgment/trust lens itself needs durable, declared calibration anchors carried across stateless sessions — self-application extends to edits on those anchors. Skill-creation stays manual because its risk is behavioral and compounding, not structural — not because skills are falsely claimed to be the 'highest-leverage' artifact.

7. EDITING INSTRUCTION DOCUMENTS (M35-M45). Name the obligation-verb before touching a line. Challenge every obligation against real protected work, with a deletion evidence-bar that scales by what's guarded (rare/catastrophic guardrails need affirmative cross-instance evidence, not silence-this-once). Concreteness (trigger/action/boundary/evidence/stop-condition, precise failure identity) is owed in full where the action compounds or is hard to undo; trivial reversible loops get a cheap heuristic. Never keep an obligation for existing, never re-polish a bad one into a clearer bad one. Removal/narrowing fast-tracks unless it loosens a safety/security/compliance guardrail. The seven-item review order is a fast scan with a stated escalation trip-wire when the artifact turns out ambient/auto-invoked/exported. Validate by classifying the edited surface first (silent-failure metadata vs. visible-failure prose), then by decision-vs-prose within it. Review reports stay impact-first and style-free, except where ambiguity on a high-blast-radius, pressure-executed artifact is itself the obligation failure.

8. PROOF, OUTPUT, AND ACTION SAFETY (M43-M50). Structural checks never stand in for behavioral/runtime proof; every closing report states what was and wasn't verified — and this rule should be single-sourced, not hand-copied, closing the corpus's own worst violation of its single-sourcing bar. Gating skills close with a structured packet sized to what they gate; exploratory skills stay exempt; high-blast-radius closes carry a required-acknowledgment residual-risk field. Git actions default to local-commit-only with explicit protected-branch/dirty-worktree escalation; other irreversible domains need their own analogous hazard signals, not borrowed git checks. Verdict-rendering stays partitioned (exploratory none, decision-owning exactly one) to prevent laundering, with stakes-scaled co-sign requirements added on top. Loops are licensed aggressive only when every action in their space, not just the exit, is reversible.

9. THE ADDED LAYER (principlesToAdd): single-source cross-cutting obligations and periodically scan the corpus for the drift duplication already produced; add nearest-neighbor collision detection as the flat skill namespace grows; add a scheduled aggregate audit for the always-loaded instruction surface and any append-only ledger, since both have already silently bloated undetected; require a falsification channel at the moment any ambient value-belief is installed; require a circularity-escape step before any self-review is allowed to amend ambient contracts; require independent (non-self) verification before admission into the explicitly-unobservable contract class; add idempotency/safe-retry requirements to multi-step execution loops; add an explicit interface contract at skill-to-skill handoffs; write down the baseline-comparison justification test for new skills instead of leaving it as hand-copied convention; reconcile the auto-commit-vs-leave-for-review split for Markdown-writing skills into one stated rule.
