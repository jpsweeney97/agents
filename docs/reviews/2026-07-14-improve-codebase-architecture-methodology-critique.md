## 4. The badge warrant: confidence vocabulary the bundle never defines

Here is the central finding, earned from this target's text and the one report that met an adversarial reader.

The text: every candidate card must carry "**Recommendation strength** — one of `Strong`, `Worth exploring`, `Speculative`, rendered as a badge" (SKILL.md:64), and the report must end with a **Top recommendation** — "which candidate you'd tackle first and why" (SKILL.md:67). That is the entirety of the bundle's guidance on its verdict stratum. HTML-REPORT.md:47 adds colors. LANGUAGE.md — the file whose whole point is that "Consistent language is the whole point" (LANGUAGE.md:3), which defines eight nouns, four principles, five relationships, and three rejected framings — says nothing about them. What earns `Strong`? What does a badge claim about the *Solution* as opposed to the *Problem*? What can a single mind's single exploratory pass certify, and what can't it? The bundle's most consequential output vocabulary — the words a user reads to decide what to build next — is its only unlegislated vocabulary.

The fires show what fills the vacuum. Four reports, four badge distributions: 2/2/1, 2/3/0, 2/2/1, 2/3/0 — always five candidates, always exactly two `Strong`. And the one time a report was adversarially adjudicated (07-14: the Codex consumer ran `review-family:system-design-review`, treating "every claim in the HTML as an allegation"), the verdict cut precisely along the line the text never draws:

- **The observations largely held.** "Its repository census is often accurate." Six measurements verified correct by name, including the 24-closure count and the test-instantiation counts. This is the exploration and the deletion test paying.
- **Two current-state claims were false, stated as fact.** Candidate 5's premise — ranges recorded "while concatenating" rendered blocks — "That concatenation point does not exist" (the fire-D agent's own admission afterward: "one false current-state model I drew as fact"). Candidate 4's claim that 1,100+ lines of outline tests "can only run through the full ViewModel" — "That is false," with two counterexample test files cited. Both rode under decision-shaped badges. Neither was caught by anything the skill owns — not the exploration, not the Explore subagent's census, not the self-check grep (which polices vocabulary, not truth). A pasted sibling review caught them, same day.
- **The calibration verdict names the gap between observation and prescription:** "The problem is confidence calibration: 'Strong,' 'local-substitutable,' and the top ranking are not supported by migration boundaries, source-of-truth decisions, or proof requirements." And: "Its weakness is the jump from 'this coordination cost is real' to 'therefore this particular deep module is the right boundary.' **Several proposed after-states erase distinctions that are deliberate and load-bearing.**"
- **No candidate survived unmodified, and every correction pushed the same direction.** Candidate 2's proposed module exceeded CONTEXT.md's own definition of the Tab Set ("a new god object," the reviewer wrote); candidate 3 merged two resolvers whose contracts the glossary deliberately distinguishes; candidate 1 was "a strategic hypothesis, not a bounded next slice." The proposed after-states *over-consolidated* — which is exactly the lean a depth-as-leverage vocabulary should be expected to impart to solutions drafted in the same pass as the observations. The commission's aesthetic-imposing-itself case survives in this narrower form: the vocabulary does not manufacture friction, but it does shape prescriptions toward consolidation, and nothing in the method opposes that lean before a badge is stamped.

The badge miscalibration is not unique to fire D. Fire A's report badged `reading-item-rendering` as `in-process`; the next day's grilling overturned it against code — "That does make the new module local-substitutable rather than pure in-process, because it touches local files/images." A taxonomy claim (DEEPENING.md's category determines *how the deepened module is tested*) shipped wrong and was caught downstream by the re-verification habit, not by the survey.

Say the finding precisely. The method is a one-pass, one-mind survey. Its Problem fields report observed friction, and the record says those observations are good. Its Solution fields are *design hypotheses* produced in the same pass, by the same mind, with no counter-pressure — the skill's own grilling and INTERFACE-DESIGN steps exist precisely because design needs contest the survey hasn't had. But the badge and the Top recommendation grade the *candidate* — problem and solution together — in a single confidence word, and the report's closing act ("which candidate you'd tackle first") is an implementation-ordering claim. The 07-14 verdict demoted the artifact from exactly that altitude: "a strong discovery artifact, but it is not decision-ready." The method's whole gating design says design happens later; its verdict vocabulary speaks as if it already happened. That is where this skill's artifacts claim more than the method can warrant — a smaller, more precisely located version of what the arc found in tech-debt-scan's certification stratum, with the crucial difference that here the record shows the overclaim being *caught and cashed out* (§5).

A one-sentence footnote on the aim: "The aim is testability and AI-navigability" (SKILL.md:8) — "AI-navigability" appears exactly once in the bundle and never again in any file, card field, or fire. The fires pursued testability and locality throughout. The second master is a costume word; the honest aim sentence already exists in LANGUAGE.md's leverage/locality pair.

## 5. The throwaway artifact's double life

The report is designed to die: temp directory, fresh file per run, "so nothing lands in the repo" (SKILL.md:51), "throwaway" in the loader description itself. The record shows both what this design buys and the seam it leaves.

What it buys is real. Because the artifact carries no durability, nothing downstream treats it as authority: the noon re-fire read it "as another evidence artifact, not as authority by itself" and immediately caught it stale (its top recommendation had shipped hours earlier); fires B and C re-verified its claims against live code before scoping; consumers transcribe the accepted decisions into committed plans "so execution does not depend on a temporary file path," which forces exactly the re-ratification grill-with-docs' treatment found missing in hot glossary writes. The grill-with-docs defect — authority outrunning provenance — does not transfer here, and that is partly this design's doing.

The seam: the method's primary product has a lifespan of days, and its record of what it claimed dies with it. Every campaign-1 report is gone from disk; what survives is the one line consumers copied ("Architecture review candidate: `Deepen Reading View navigation intent`; the accepted decisions from that review are copied into this plan") plus a temp path in a committed plan that now dangles. Nothing in the artifact says which commit it read — fire A's report went stale within fourteen hours and carried no way to notice; the consumer had to discover it against live code. For a method whose value is a ranked survey of a *moving* codebase, the report's only freshness credential is the date in its header.

And on 07-14 the throwaway design was quietly outgrown in the most interesting event in the corpus: after the system-design-review verdict, the user had the *report itself edited in place* — "adjudicated same day — dispositions corrected in amber callouts" now sits in its header; candidate 1 re-badged "Worth exploring — design exploration + characterization first"; candidate 2's diagram relabeled "(policy owner)"; the Top recommendation replaced. The disposable survey became an adjudicated, versionless decision record living in a temp directory — consumed by four more subagent waves the same afternoon, cited by handoffs, feeding ADRs. The contract has no vocabulary for any of this: not for a report surviving its adjudication, not for amendment, not for who may rewrite a badge. The field invented a lifecycle the text says cannot exist. That is not a floor violation — it is the method's users telling it what its artifact wants to be when the stakes rise: a *dated survey* whose corrections deserve to be visible rather than silent.

That afterlife is mostly not this skill's to own — implementation-planning, characterization-tests, and the review family own their stages — but the text's silent hand-off (its side-effects list still points at INTERFACE-DESIGN.md as the one forward door, SKILL.md:88) misdescribes the road every real fire took.

## 7. The single-shot sensor and the leak the grep can't see

Two residual defects in instruments that otherwise work, both earned from fires:

- **The agreement-theater sensor fires once and disarms.** In fire C it fired after seven consecutive verbatim acceptances, asked the least-certain question — and then questions 8 and 9 were again accepted verbatim with no second naming. The rule's text ("if the user accepts a run of recommended answers verbatim, say the grilling has become drafting with their consent, then ask the design question whose answer you are least sure of or offer to stop," SKILL.md:81) reads as a one-time trip, and the fires execute it that way. A ratification run that resumes after the naming is more information, not less — the moment the rule's own logic says to offer stopping — and the current wording lets the run resume under a discharged sensor.
- **The vocabulary ban is enforced where drift doesn't happen and unenforced where it does.** The reports grep clean every time — because the agents grep them. The observed drift lives in the grilling prose: fire C's Question 7 used "boundary" twice ("across the ViewModel -> ContentView -> Reading View boundary"), the user's verbatim echo carried it forward, and it propagated into the plan's language — precisely the seam-vocabulary substitution LANGUAGE.md:15 exists to prevent, in the phase where decisions are being ratified. "Use these terms exactly in every suggestion" (SKILL.md:14) already covers the grilling; what the fires show is that the *enforcement habit* the agents invented is scoped to the artifact, so the ban is airtight exactly where a reviewer might check and porous where the design is actually settled.

Two smaller honesty notes belong with these. The noon re-fire ended its grilling without the closing file-report grill-with-docs' discipline requires — writes happened, no closeout named them. And the ledger blind spot (§2): the skill's first Claude fire produced no ledger row, which matters to every future census that trusts the ledger's suffix-aware count.
