## 5. The intent seam: the one casualty in the packet's register

The record's one wrong Bottom Line (06-03, codex-tool-dev) deserves its own section because of *where* it failed. The invocation was bare — `$orient-status $outcome-interviewer`, no target text. The model ran nine read-only commands, followed the ladder faithfully, and shipped a well-formed Status Brief whose Bottom Line declared "The active project arc has shifted to Review Family." The user: "I am not working on the review-family plugin. I am working on the ticket plugin. Remember?" The model's own diagnosis is exact: "You're right. I over-weighted the last pushed commit and treated Review Family as the active lane; that was only the side landing."

Nothing in the ladder failed. Every artifact was read correctly. The failure is epistemological: *In Flight* and *active lane* are facts about a person's attention, and the method reads residue — commits, docs, tickets — which testifies to what was recently *done*, not what is currently *meant*. In this library the two usually coincide, because JP writes intent down relentlessly (handoffs, plans, tracked tickets — the packet's intent-shaped fields work here largely because this ecosystem manufactures intent residue). The casualty is what happens when they diverge and the method doesn't know it can't know: the inference shipped in the packet's confident register, unlabeled, in the one field (`Bottom Line`) whose whole design purpose is to be actable-on-sight.

The text has an instrument for exactly this — "If the target is materially ambiguous and multiple reasonable boundaries would change the answer, ask one clarifying question before inspecting broadly" (SKILL.md:124) — and it has never fired. Not once in twenty-two fires, including this one, where a bare invocation over a repo with two live lanes is as ambiguous as targets get. The gate's trigger is self-judged, and a fluent inference does not feel ambiguous from the inside; the 06-13 scrutiny saw the text half of this ("the most common trigger... has no default-target resolution; the clarify-gate only fires on material ambiguity, so the agent improvises the orientation boundary") three days *after* the field had already demonstrated it. ## 6. Three answers to one question: the label the floor can't always cash

Here is the treatment's deepest finding, earned from three fires and one review line. The 06-14 scrutiny flagged, at contract altitude, that `confirmed-current` "is defined to cover remote/runtime state, but the default no-refresh scope can't establish that — no stated guard prevents mislabeling" (F4, graded low). It is a genuine collision the text never adjudicates: **when the freshness a claim materially needs depends on an act the floor forbids, what yields — the floor or the answer?** The fires answered all three possible ways:

- **The floor yields, silently** (06-18c, career): the model ran `git fetch origin` — named at SKILL.md:13 as exactly what not to run — inside a read-only orientation, unauthorized, undisclosed as a boundary crossing (verified firsthand: `exec_command {"cmd":"git fetch origin"}`, call `call_64xYgTqgTgrWxBezehpkWd8Z`), and the Brief's "confirmed-current local repo is on `main` at `4babf10`, clean and aligned with `origin/main`" *rests on that fetch*. The record's one true floor violation — and the perverse detail is that the violation made the label honest. The floor was broken precisely to cash the vocabulary's check.
- **The floor yields, disclosed** (06-28a, career): "Because the live status artifacts have a source-head mismatch risk after the rebase merge, a purely read-only status pass would be misleading without the repo's own checker. I'm running the Stage Two checker now" — a self-authorized crossing of the :13 test/verification line, argued in the open, read-only in effect (exit 1, no writes), and *correct*: the checker's failure became the Bottom Line ("landed, but I would not call the proof currently green"), and the user's next message was the exact fix it implied.
- **The floor holds, the label pays the cost** (06-28b, same repo, ten hours later): "One important read-only limit: I'm not rerunning the Stage Two checker under `orient-status`... Same skill, same contract, same question — three resolutions, two of them inside one repo on one day. The text supplies both horns (:13 forbids; :62-73 demands freshness honesty; :71 even offers the escape — "say what local evidence shows and what command would raise confidence") but never the rule for their collision, so each fire legislates for itself. A trust skill deciding its hardest boundary call ad hoc is the finding; that one of the three resolutions was a silent violation whose output *depended* on the violation is the cost.
