# Register Sweep — the stand-in words I actually use

2026-08-08. Commissioned by JP after a braindump about wording that forces him to translate.

## What this is

JP asked for plain, literal wording: say the meaning itself, not a metaphor or nickname that stands in for it, because for him the translating step is where meaning gets lost. Before finalizing an always-on rule about this, we measured my real habits instead of guessing: a multi-agent sweep read my actual replies from thirteen past sessions and collected every term whose meaning is not in the literal sense of the words.

## How it was measured

- Thirteen past sessions across four projects: seven in the playpen, four in `~/.agents`, one in cross-model, one in markdown-reader. Chosen for length and for dates with heavy activity (mid-July and August 3–8).
- One agent per session extracted only the sentences I wrote directly to JP — no tool calls, no code, no subagent traffic — and searched them.
- **Result: 455 distinct terms. Every one of the thirteen agents hit its 40-term reporting cap, so the true total is higher.**
- Counts are approximate (simple text matching). Tonight's session was excluded. Playpen sessions run invented personas, which raises their figurative density on purpose; the `~/.agents` and cross-model sessions have no such excuse and were just as dense.

Coverage: playpen 07-26 am/pm, 08-03, 08-06, 08-07, 08-08 early/late; agents 07-21, 07-22, 07-25, 08-06; cross-model 07-16; markdown-reader 08-07. All thirteen returned 35–40 terms each.

## Terms heavy inside a single session — the invented vocabularies

The biggest finding of the sweep is a habit the guessed list missed entirely: **I don't just borrow existing metaphors — I invent a themed word-set per project and then write inside it.** Three clear cases:

- **The skill-evaluation runs in `~/.agents` (July):** "envelope", "gate" / "Gate B" / "Gate C", "sealed", "wave", "ceiling", "crowning", "stems", "transport", "cold-judge". Between 9 and 94 uses each.
- **The playpen idea-review sessions (August):** card-game words — "card", "bite", "hand", "deal", "fold", "pull", "beat". This is the vocabulary that made the cold-open card unreadable after one day away.
- **The July 26 hooks workshop:** a machine shop — "bench", "gear", "mounted", plus a nautical wing: "lifeboat", "weather", "night porter", and "davits" (the small cranes that hold a ship's lifeboat — used seven times).

Each vocabulary was probably understandable the night it was coined, because the coining happened in front of JP. The cost arrives later: any reply, handoff, or re-entry card that reuses the word assumes a translation JP no longer has loaded.

## What follows for the rule

1. **The rule must be a test, not a list.** 455-plus terms cannot be enumerated, and most future offenders will be invented on the spot.
2. **The guessed examples were the wrong ones.** Corporate stock phrases ("north star", "low-hanging fruit") barely appear in my real replies. My actual habits are: physical images ("machinery", "spine", "seam", "wall", "in flight"), abstract stand-in words ("instrument", "surface", "ambient", "shape"), evaluation shorthand ("gate", "canary", "green", "load-bearing"), and coined session vocabulary.
3. **The rule needs a line the first draft didn't have:** never invent a themed vocabulary and write inside it. That single habit produced most of the 455.
4. **"Say the word" (6 uses, 3 sessions) always appears at the moment I ask JP for a go/no-go decision** — a translation cost at the worst possible spot.
5. "load-bearing" — the example JP named from memory — turned out to be the single most widespread term: six of thirteen sessions. His instinct about what bothers him was measurement-grade.

## Proposed rule text, for `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` → Communication

### Literal Language

JP reads words literally. A word that only points at its meaning — an image, a borrowed phrase, a coined shorthand — forces him to translate, and the translating is where the meaning gets lost. Test every sentence: is the meaning in the literal sense of the words? A few more literal words always cost him less than fewer, denser ones.

- Say the point, not a stand-in for it: "that's the crux" → "that's the point the decision turns on"; "this assumption is load-bearing" → "if this is wrong, the plan fails"; "say the word" → "tell me to start and I will".
- The test decides, not any list. Kinds it catches, with examples measured from my real past replies: physical images ("machinery", "spine", "seam", "wave", "in flight"); insider shorthand ("load-bearing", "gate", "lane", "canary", "green" meaning passing); abstract stand-in words ("instrument", "surface", "ambient", "the shape of"); vocabulary coined in past sessions ("the arc", "bite", "envelope").
- Never invent a themed vocabulary for a project (a card game, a machine shop, a courtroom) and write inside it — describe the work in ordinary words.
- Names of real things — files, tools, features — stay as names; state the plain meaning at first use each session.
