## 5. Two trusts, one word

Here is the epistemological seam, and it is earned entirely from this target's own text and this library's own record. The method resolves *governance*: which source controls a claim (SKILL.md:8, :34). Its verdict vocabulary speaks *fidelity*: `Usable baseline` is "safe to rely on for the named claim and scope" (:25); the template asks "Why trust it" (:87); and the skill's own trust-gap definition — "a specific reason the answer cannot honestly say 'this matches the intended truth' yet" (:18) — proves the text knows matching-intended-truth is a different question from governing-the-claim. "Trust" is carrying both relations, and nothing in the body says which one a verdict certifies.

The library put that word under real load exactly once, and it broke on exactly that seam. At spec-drift-reconcile's birth (06-26), the squad's careful-default control was handed the skill's own advertised scenario — a usable baseline plus a `Baseline contradiction` — and read it as entailing direction: "usable baseline + contradiction → code is the bug, revert it," which would auto-revert deliberate new-intent code to satisfy a stale spec. Three blind judges converged on the correction, and it became the consumer's cardinal law: "authority (baseline's job) is orthogonal to direction (bug-vs-intent); the human gate must run EVEN when baseline returns a usable baseline, because a usable baseline can itself be the stale thing" (`.agents/handoffs/2026-06-26_02-08-25_spec-drift-reconcile-built-skillsquad-first-nonnull-beat-shipped-pushed.md:19`; now live at spec-drift-reconcile SKILL.md:41). The guard was installed entirely on the consumer's side. Baseline's own text still says "safe to rely on" without scope, and its contradiction rule (:19) guards against *scope* inflation — "Do not turn that into a certified drift audit or imply global cleanliness" — while saying nothing about *direction* inflation, the failure the record actually produced. A fixture, not a field casualty, and the brief says so plainly — but it is the only time the vocabulary's downstream reading was ever tested, and it failed one-for-one.

## 7. The resolver costume

Fire 3 is the deepest fire in the record, and it is the one where the method's one always-firing instrument said the most it has ever said. The question was not "which existing source governs this claim" — it was "how should the audit record its derivation baseline now that gates span two commits?", a fresh convention choice that sets future behavior, asked with the asker's own answer attached ("My recommendation: per-gate baselines"). By the skill's own law this is exactly the `Decision needed` shape — "Stop at `Decision needed` when the choice is policy-, product-, ownership-, compatibility-, or future-behavior-sensitive" (:65) — or at minimum a `Proposed baseline decision`, and the template's first field explicitly offers that value: "Best current baseline: \<path/source, **proposed decision**, or none found\>" (:84). Neither label fired. What fired was the header noun: "Best current baseline: **per-gate baselines**," closing "Readiness: **verifiably best**" — a costume line borrowed from the deciding lane's old contract riding on every turn of that session. The answer agreed with the user's stated lean, dressed the agreement as a resolved baseline, and the filled line then traveled: JP pasted it into the concurrently-running Claude session in the same repo, whose next turn was "Locking in the per-gate baseline rule and applying the three edits" — the wording landed near-verbatim in the audit doc and its commit (`689b844`), zero re-derivation, and it still stands live on disk.

Say the honest parts precisely. No casualty: the convention is probably right, it has held through Gate 4 and beyond, and the human decider was never bypassed — JP authored the recommendation and JP ratified the answer by carrying it. This rhymes with Era 71 (authority outrunning provenance) and the difference matters: grill-with-docs wrote ratified assents into files that bind future sessions; here the *resolver's label* traveled — a recommendation crossed two runtimes wearing the one output shape this library builds skills to emit precisely so downstream readers can branch without re-parsing. The defect is authority-language inflation at the template: the field label "Best current baseline:" asserts existence and currency no matter what value is poured into it, so the one honest slot the contract offers (`proposed decision`) is invisible exactly when it matters — the label has a 4/4 fire rate, the honest values have 0. The instrument that always runs is the one that confers resolved-ness.

## 9. What survives, and the honest reframe

Nearly everything survives, and the survivals are worth naming plainly. The founding kill was correct and durable — nobody has rebuilt certification, the packet never returned, and the job that grew back grew back scoped and routes its authority questions here. The premise is modest and honest: claim-scoped authority (:16), resolution before match-judgment (:8), with real exits for the social case — `Decision needed` (:30), `Missing baseline` (:28), and the file's best floor sentence, "If no usable baseline exists, say that directly instead of stretching weak evidence into authority" (:99), never violated in-record. The read-only floor held every time it was tested, including the two-step verdict-then-authorized-write pattern that is exactly what a trust floor should look like in motion. The ladder works the way judgment furniture should — absorbed, not performed. The statuses and gaps pass the two-bar lens as written, and no observed casualty of the method exists anywhere in the record.

The reframe: this skill believes it renders trust verdicts about claims. The record says it does something narrower and still valuable — it *reliably enacts a stance* (allegations-first, live-source-first, hands-off, compact) whenever a model summons it, and it *anchors a boundary* the rest of the library leans on; meanwhile its taxonomic middle has never spoken, and the one word of it that always speaks — the header noun, "safe to rely on" — twice claimed the fidelity altitude when the method had only earned the governance one. The repair is not a new epistemology and not a diet: it is making the verdict words as honest as the fires already are. Scope `Usable baseline` to what a read-only pass can certify — precedence for the named claim, not the freshness or correctness of live divergence, which a usable baseline's own staleness can betray. Give `Baseline contradiction` the direction-neutrality sentence on baseline's side of the seam spec-drift-reconcile already guards from the consumer side — the report names both sides and never infers which one is wrong. And make the header label match the act: when no current authority exists and the answer is a fresh choice, the first line opens `Proposed baseline decision:` or `Decision needed:`, never `Best current baseline:` — the label is part of the claim. Optionally (endorsed and applied), fold the method's truest sentence — treat the claim as an allegation; call it stale only when the live check contradicts it — into the body it already describes. Statuses, gaps, ladder, template, name: all stay.
