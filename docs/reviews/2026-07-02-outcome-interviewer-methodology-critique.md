# Outcome-Interviewer: A Critique of the Methodology and Philosophy

> Saved 2026-07-02 at JP's request, to be re-readable. This is the critique delivered in-session when JP asked for pressure on `outcome-interviewer`'s methodology and philosophy — "don't scrutinize it as a skill." The target is `skills/outcome-interviewer/SKILL.md` and its examples as of commit `f3d9307`; inline line references resolve against that version (`git show f3d9307:skills/outcome-interviewer/SKILL.md`). JP endorsed the critique in full and it became the design brief for the `outcome-shaping` rebuild (commit `26d7ae9`), which renamed the skill and transplanted its philosophy. The body below is reproduced from the session verbatim, apart from converting the opening turn-report line to first person.

I read the skill, its examples, its metadata, and its full commit history back to the founding version. What follows is pressure on the method itself — the epistemology, the stance, the implicit theory of what "wanting" is — not on frontmatter or routing.

**TLDR:** The skill's philosophy rests on an excavation premise — that a determinate "what you actually want" pre-exists the conversation and the interview *recovers* it — and that premise is probably false in exactly the muddy cases the skill exists for. What the interview actually does is *manufacture* an outcome, and it manufactures in one shape only: relief-from-vigilance. Its exits all narrow and never widen, its epistemics terminate in the user's nod, and it trusts the user's process-level self-reports absolutely while its whole premise is that their object-level self-reports can't be trusted. Underneath, the strongest honest reading is that this isn't an elicitation methodology at all — it's a restraint system for known LLM pathologies, wearing an epistemology as a cover story. The cover story has started to believe itself.

---

## 1. The excavation premise: recovered or manufactured?

The opening line commits the skill to a metaphysics: "Help the user clarify what they actually want to make true" (SKILL.md:8). "Actually" is doing enormous work. It asserts a latent, determinate want that exists prior to articulation, currently fogged, waiting to be wiped clear.

Forty years of preference-elicitation research (Slovic's constructed-preference line, the whole stated-vs-revealed literature) says that for novel, complex choices — precisely the "still-muddy" territory this skill claims — preferences of this kind are *constructed in the act of articulation*, not retrieved. If that's right, the interview's questions don't reveal the outcome; they are the die it gets cast in. Every question is a frame, and this skill's questions have a very specific frame (see §2).

The skill's convergence machinery makes this worse, not better. The stopping condition is "the user would accept it without correction" (SKILL.md:198). Acceptance is a coherence signal, not a truth signal — and it's exactly the signal a maximally fluent summarizer can manufacture. The "My read so far" mirror is written by a model whose prose is more articulate than the user's inner state; fluency reads as truth; users sign crisp versions of their own muddle that are nicer than what they had and slightly wrong. The choice grammar compounds it: "My guess is A, but B would also make sense. Which is closer?" (SKILL.md:94) is a forced choice between two interviewer-authored options whose very phrasing presupposes one is close. The skill knows this — "Leave room for the user to reject the frame" — but a disclaimer doesn't neutralize an anchor; acquiescence bias means the user picks A.

So the deepest question the skill never asks itself: *when the interview converges, what exactly converged?* The user's want, or the user onto the interviewer's compression of it? Nothing in the method can tell these apart, because nothing in the method ever tests the clarified outcome against anything external — behavior, artifacts, revealed priorities, cost. In a repo whose culture is "do not claim work is done without fresh evidence," this is the one skill whose entire epistemics terminate in testimony and a nod. (`outcome-check` closes the loop eventually — but months later, after the manufactured artifact has already steered design, plan, and PRD.)

## 2. The vigilance ontology: every outcome comes out worry-shaped

Look at the sanctioned vocabulary. The plain-language verb list (SKILL.md:71-82): *do, notice, check, worry, trust, recover, decide, hand off, come back to*. Five of nine are vigilance verbs. The preferred questions (examples/interaction-examples.md:127-133): "Who needs to feel *comfortable*", "What should they be able to *stop checking*", "What would feel like a *bad surprise*", "When something *goes wrong*, what should still be easy", "What would make this feel *done enough*". Every single example in the corpus: stop wondering whether Ticket touched the wrong thing, stop paging people, tell the real outage from the flaky timeout, stop worrying that a cleanup changed behavior, trust the checks enough to skip re-reading.

That is not a neutral extraction instrument. It is an *anxiety-relief ontology of outcomes*: the assumption that what a person wants is always the removal of a vigilance burden. Absent from the entire vocabulary: want, learn, explore, prove, impress, enjoy, delight. Ambition-shaped goals ("I want the fastest parser in its class"), curiosity-shaped goals ("I want to learn what this architecture feels like"), aesthetic goals, identity goals ("I want to have shipped something") — passed through this interviewer, they all get re-rendered as negative space, someone somewhere ceasing to worry. That's a lossy, distorting translation for a large class of real motives, and the method has no way to notice it's distorting, because the user's correction happens *inside* the offered frame.

The origin is legible: the skill was born in an ops/infra/agent-trust world where relief-from-vigilance genuinely is the dominant outcome currency. It has overfit its home domain's outcome-shape and mistaken it for the shape of outcomes in general. AGENTS.md says to judge skills as global capabilities; this one's philosophy of wanting is narrower than its deployment surface.

## 3. Every exit narrows; none widens

Check the handoff table (SKILL.md:167-173): design-exploration, making-recommendations, grill-me, a review skill, or closure. Now notice what's missing: `ideate` and `prototype` — and notice that `ideate`'s own description routes muddy goals *to* outcome-interviewer, while outcome-interviewer never routes back. It's a one-way door into convergence.

This encodes a philosophical bet the skill never defends: that mud is always an *articulation* deficit, payable by Q&A. But mud comes in kinds, and at least three of them are not interview-soluble:

- **Option-poor** mud: the user can't say what they want because they haven't seen what's possible. They need widening, not clarification. This skill will faithfully interview them into a crisper version of their single mediocre idea.
- **Underinformed** mud: no amount of introspection can answer, because the answer lives in reality — they need a throwaway prototype or an experiment. Asking one careful question at a time of someone who lacks the information is a *confabulation pump*: people reliably generate confident answers to questions whose true answers they have no access to, and the mirror will then lovingly stabilize the confabulation.
- **Conflicted or avoidant** mud: the vagueness is load-bearing — two incompatible wants, or a known answer the user dislikes. Vagueness-as-ego-defense wants gentle confrontation; the skill explicitly defers challenge ("Do not immediately cross-examine," SKILL.md:140) and gates grilling behind opt-in — and the user who most needs grilling is precisely the one who won't opt in.

The method has no diagnostic step for "is this mud interview-soluble?" It assumes yes by firing. A serious version of this philosophy would treat mud-typing as the *first* interview move and would own divergent and empirical exits, not just convergent ones.

## 4. One question at a time: principle or ritual?

There is a strong defense of the one-question invariant, and the skill never makes it. The defense: in genuinely muddy territory, each question *should* be contingent on the last answer — if you could batch your questions, they were independent, which means you were administering a form, not interviewing. One-at-a-time isn't etiquette; it's the definitional test of whether interviewing is the right tool at all.

Because the skill treats the rule as turn-shape etiquette rather than as that contingency principle, two symptoms follow. First, it hard-contradicts your own global rule — "batch independent ones into a single ask" — in the one lane where questions are the product, with no acknowledgment that the override is deliberate or why. Second, the latest commit (f3d9307, "require one question even on inspection-first turns") institutionalizes exactly the thing the skill elsewhere despises: SKILL.md:40 forbids "manufacturing a performative question," yet a mandate that every turn end in a question regardless of state is a performative-question generator. The rule is being maintained as ritual shape, patched at the edges, rather than derived from a principle that would tell you when it doesn't apply.

And there's a quieter observation underneath: read the examples closely and the *question* is often the least important element. "My read so far: … Is that the shape, or is it more that…?" — the mirror does the clarifying work; the question is mostly "correct me." The skill's true engine is the Rogersian reflection loop, and the interrogation format may be ceremony around it. If so, the skill misdescribes its own mechanism — it thinks it's Socratic when it's actually Rogerian, and the design energy spent policing question-count is spent on the wrong invariant.

## 5. Plain language cuts on the wrong axis

The translation mandate's real target is legitimate and important: technical vocabulary smuggles *solutions* ("audit log," "observability") into outcome talk, and de-jargonizing forces the want apart from the mechanism. That's the best instinct in the skill.

But the rule as written fires on plain-vs-technical, when the load-bearing distinction is *outcome-vs-mechanism*. "Snapshot isolation" is technical language that names an outcome with decades of compressed precision; translating it to "people aren't surprised by interleaved writes" *loses* information and forces an expert to audit your folksy paraphrase for semantic drift. The correctability rationale — plain language is easier to correct — is dubious for precise speakers: experts correct exact claims faster than fuzzy ones.

The sharpest edge: translation is authorship. The interviewer holds the pen on the read; the read's vocabulary flows into the capsule, the capsule into design-exploration, the design into the PRD. Systematically replacing the user's chosen words with the interviewer's paraphrase makes the agent the author of the project's language — under a method that describes itself as helping the user find *their* want. Sometimes a person's exact words are load-bearing, and this method's reflex is to take them away.

## 6. Wants elicited in a cost vacuum

The elicitation grammar asks for point-preferences with no prices attached. "What should someone no longer need to double-check?" — everything, obviously, if it's free. Real preferences are demand curves: "I want X" at cost 1 and "absolutely not X" at cost 10 are the same person. Every serious elicitation discipline (conjoint analysis, forced trade-offs, stated-vs-revealed comparison) makes people choose *between bundles*, not describe desiderata. This skill's "choices" tool is used to disambiguate *meaning*, never to force *trades*; "main tradeoff" appears once, as a checkbox in the stopping list (SKILL.md:198), not as a move in the method. The predictable output bias: clarified outcomes that are systematically utopian — lists of things that would feel nice, uncalibrated by what the user would actually pay in time, complexity, or risk. Downstream lanes then inherit a "settled outcome" that was never priced.

## 7. The trust asymmetry

The skill's premise distrusts the user's object-level self-report — "what they *actually* want" implies the stated want isn't it. Yet it grants absolute deference to the user's process-level self-reports: "Ask before switching: name the move and let the user decline. A handoff the user did not choose is just the interview ending early" (SKILL.md:175). So: your report of what you want is fog to be seen through; your report of how you want to be helped is sovereign. That asymmetry is undefended, and it bites in a known way — the confused user says "yeah, just recommend something" to escape the discomfort of being interviewed, at exactly the moment the skill's own premise says interviewing is most needed. Therapy has a name for this (flight into health); the skill's consent architecture has no answer to it beyond compliance.

The same asymmetry shows up as testimony-over-evidence. The skill *has* inspection powers, but subordinates them: "Inspection has one job: improve the interview" (SKILL.md:36), and discovered issues must be metabolized into the next question rather than stated as findings (SKILL.md:44). A more empirical methodology would let artifacts testify against testimony: "You say latency doesn't matter; you've merged three latency PRs this month." The skill can technically smuggle that into a question — but its philosophy demotes the strongest clarification instrument it has (revealed preference in the repo itself) to question-fodder.

## 8. The evolving read: elegant, lossy, and never audited

The rewrite-don't-append read is the skill's best invention and it carries three unacknowledged risks.

*Premature coherence.* A single compact read wants to be one sentence, and sentences want single subjects. Muddy ideas are often muddy because they contain two incompatible wants; the format pressures them into one want plus "an unclear part." The method offers rival interpretations turn-locally but never *maintains* rival reads across turns — there's no superposition, only early collapse.

*No conservation law.* Rewriting is lossy by design, and the audit mechanism is the user noticing what's missing — but humans are excellent at checking what's present and terrible at noticing absence. A constraint confirmed at turn 2 can be silently compressed out by turn 6 and nothing checks for it. This is aggravated by the substrate: this skill runs in a context-compaction runtime where the transcript itself gets summarized, so late in a long interview the read may be the *only* surviving record — rewrite-only plus lossy compaction means settled facts can evaporate without trace, and the capsule's "what is settled" gets reconstructed from whatever survived.

*No adversarial self-check.* The read is always produced by descent from the previous read — never re-derived fresh, never confronted with its strongest rival, never devil's-advocated. In a repo whose review skills are saturated with adversarial verification of the agent's own conclusions, the one artifact that steers everything downstream — the interviewer's compression of the user's intent — is checked by nothing but the user's nod, which §1 argued is manufacturable. The method polices the interviewer's *output shape* obsessively (no ledgers, no findings, one question) and its *interpretive drift* not at all.

## 9. Two masters

Officially this is a thinking partner: success is the user understanding their own want, even if the want dissolves ("actually I don't want this at all" should count as triumph — nothing in the skill says so, but it follows from the philosophy). Structurally, though, it's a requirements-extraction front-end for an agent pipeline: the warm-handoff capsule exists so "the next lane" doesn't re-interview, and the closure template has fields — outcome, audience, qualities, failure, next move — which is a spec schema wearing a conversational hat. The skill denies spec-ness aesthetically ("Do not create a formal spec") while enforcing it structurally.

These masters pull opposite directions: partnership optimizes for *fidelity* (report the mud as mud), pipelines optimize for *completeness* (fill the beats). The skill fights its own gradient with "omit any beat with no real content rather than manufacture one" — an instruction paddling against the format's current. And the commit history shows which master is winning: the capsule was added (4728fdc), steering vocabulary accreted, turn-shape got mandated (f3d9307). The skill is evolving toward spec-writer while its philosophy still says midwife. The disclaimer at SKILL.md:217 — the capsule "does not gain authority the interview never reached" — is wishful: authority is conferred by readers, not disclaimed by authors, and downstream lanes receive a section titled "what is settled."

## 10. What survives, and the honest reframe

Three things hold up under all of this. The mirror loop — externalizing a compact interpretation for cheap correction — is genuinely good cognitive technology; it exploits the real asymmetry that people can't state what they want but can instantly recognize what they don't ("no, not that"). Designing openers for *correctability* rather than impressiveness is a real epistemic virtue most agent output lacks. And the anti-solutioning instinct — pry the outcome apart from the mechanism before mechanism talk starts — is the right medicine for the most common failure in software conversations, even if the plain-language rule is the wrong knife for it.

But the most honest reading of the whole artifact is this: most of its "philosophy" is actually *negative space* — don't dump findings, don't build ledgers, don't write specs, don't ask twelve questions, don't decide for the user. Those are restraints on known LLM pathologies, and as restraint engineering the skill is excellent. The excavation story, the plain-language doctrine, the one-question liturgy — these are the cover story that makes the restraints feel like a coherent method. The trouble with cover stories is that they accrete features as if they were true: capsules, steering grammars, turn-shape mandates, each patch defending the story rather than the function. If the skill knew itself as a governor — "keep the model quiet, correctable, and out of the user's way while the user thinks" — it would be smaller, it would batch independent questions without guilt, it would route to widening and prototyping when the mud isn't interview-soluble, and it would let the mirror, not the question, be its declared engine.

The heart of it, then: this skill believes it helps users discover what they actually want. Under pressure, it helps users *commit to a well-written want* — worry-shaped, cost-free, convergent, authored substantially by the interviewer, and certified by a nod the interviewer's own fluency earned. That's often still useful. It is not what it says on the tin.
