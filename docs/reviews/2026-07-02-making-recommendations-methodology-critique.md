# Making-Recommendations: A Critique of the Methodology and Philosophy

> Saved 2026-07-02. JP asked for `making-recommendations` to get the same treatment `outcome-interviewer` got (`docs/reviews/2026-07-02-outcome-interviewer-methodology-critique.md`): pressure on the methodology and philosophy — the epistemology, the stance, the implicit theory of what deciding is — not a skill-contract scrutiny. The target is `skills/making-recommendations/SKILL.md` and its support surfaces as of commit `95a1ccb`; inline line references resolve against that version (`git show 95a1ccb:skills/making-recommendations/SKILL.md`). This critique served as the design brief for the same-day rebuild (commit `01d9313`), which kept the name — the name describes the output, which remains true; it was the method that lied.

I read the skill, its examples, its high-stakes reference, its metadata, and its full commit history back to the founding version. What follows is pressure on the method itself.

**TLDR:** The skill rests on a measurement premise — that a single model, scoring options criterion-by-criterion and aggregating under a named weighting, produces knowledge about which option is best. That premise fails in exactly the cases the machinery exists for. Where the call is clear, the protocol is decoration; where the call is close, the ranking is decided by exchange rates between the user's values that the agent invents, announces, and computes with — a values call laundered into an evidence artifact. The Mediating Assessments Protocol it cites by name is a noise cure for panels of separate judges, transplanted into a single judge making a single pass, where there is no independence to protect and no noise to cancel — only bias, which passes through wearing a lab coat. Meanwhile the best-documented failure of an LLM asked "should I do X or Y" — ranking the option the asker visibly favors — is never named anywhere in the skill, and the protocol's authority certifies its output. The restraints (honest exits, generation before evaluation, no invented alternatives) are excellent governor engineering; the decision-science liturgy is a cover story that has started believing itself.

## 1. The measurement premise: MAP without a panel

The workflow's engine is step 5: score "every option against one criterion before moving to the next, holding off on a holistic impression until every criterion is scored (the noise-reducing Mediating Assessments Protocol)" (SKILL.md:61), then step 6 aggregates the scores under a weighting (SKILL.md:62). The citation is real — Kahneman, Sibony, and Sunstein designed MAP — and the transplant is the problem. MAP's mechanism is *independence*: separate assessors, or at minimum genuinely separated assessments, so that one global impression cannot contaminate the per-dimension judgments, with the holistic verdict delayed until the independent parts exist. Its target is noise — the scatter across judges and occasions that panels and repeated judgments exhibit.

Here every "independent" assessment is produced by one model, in one context window, in one autoregressive pass, with all options, all criteria, the user's framing, and the model's own emerging conclusion simultaneously in attention. There is no independence to protect. An instruction to hold off on a holistic impression commands a transformer not to have a representation it already has; what the instruction actually controls is the *order of the text* — the format of delayed judgment, not the fact of it. Scoring sequence is not information hygiene. And a single judge on a single occasion has no across-judge scatter to cancel: the disease MAP treats is absent, while the diseases this judge actually has — anchoring on the user's framing, agreement drift, fluency mistaken for evidence — pass through the ritual untouched. Worse than untouched: laundered, because the output now carries a protocol's authority.

What genuinely survives of the transplant is forced coverage — every option must be addressed on every criterion, so an inconvenient cell cannot be quietly skipped. That is real and worth keeping. It is a completeness device, not a noise reduction, and it does not need numbers.

## 2. Reproducibility theater

"State the scale or basis each criterion is scored on so the same inputs would reproduce the same score later, not an unanchored gut rating" (SKILL.md:68). Two claims — an anchored scale, and reproducibility — and neither survives contact. There is no procedure by which "ops familiarity scored against this team's current stack experience" (examples/behavior-examples.md:34) yields a 3 rather than a 4; the stated basis is not a measurement protocol, it is a caption. The same decision re-run tomorrow yields different cells, and no caption changes that. The cells are point estimates of nothing.

Then step 6 does arithmetic on them: aggregate the per-criterion scores into a ranking under a named weighting (SKILL.md:62, :69). Weighted sums over confabulated integers, with the weights invented in the same breath (§3). This is precision laundering — prose judgments in, matrix out, and the matrix format claims interval-scale knowledge nobody has. The deeper cost is rhetorical: a user can contest an argument, because an argument shows its premises. Nobody can contest a cell. The matrix does not make the reasoning more auditable; it makes the reasoning *less* visible while making the conclusion look more earned.

## 3. The fact–value collapse: whose weights are these?

In every close call, the ranking is decided by the weighting, and a weighting is not evidence — it is an exchange rate between goods the user cares about. The skill glimpses this at its edges: readiness `decision needed` exists "because values, ownership, policy, product meaning, or risk tolerance controls the answer" (SKILL.md:87), and one example line allows `decision needed` "if the weighting itself is a values call the user should make" (examples/behavior-examples.md:37). But the method's default motion runs the other way: decompose, score, "name the weighting or priority basis behind the aggregated ranking so the user can see it and override it" (SKILL.md:69), rank, recommend.

Naming a weight is not eliciting one. Disclosure is doing enormous work in that sentence, and it cannot carry it: acquiescence to an announced assumption is the same nod-epistemics the interviewer critique caught in its §1 — the fluent packet arrives with a ranking already inside it, and the values call the taxonomy says "a human must make" has been anchored by the verdict before it was ever posed. The user, presented with a matrix, a named weighting, and a recommendation, overrides approximately never — not because they endorse the weight but because the packet is finished and finished things get accepted.

The inversion the method misses: weight-sensitivity is the finding. If the ranking is stable across any reasonable weighing of the criteria, that is a clear call — and the matrix was unnecessary. If it flips on one exchange rate, that exchange rate *is* the decision, and the honest deliverable is the priced question — "would you give up most of the scaling headroom for tooling the team already knows?" — not a ranking computed under an exchange rate the agent authored. The method has no move for surfacing the flip point; sensitivity appears only as "1-2 realistic flips" at medium stakes and above (SKILL.md:79), an appendix to the verdict rather than its replacement.

## 4. Sycophancy: the enemy is never named

The best-documented pathology of a language model asked "should I do X or Y" is recommending the option the asker visibly favors. The lean leaks through everything — option order, "keep the current" versus "switch," which option got the adjectives, what the user sounds excited about — and this skill contains not one sentence about it. No instruction to notice the lean, none to handle agreement with it differently from disagreement, none to deliver a contrary call plainly rather than softening it into "either could work."

The scoring machinery makes this worse, not better. The scores are free parameters (§2): a model drifting toward the user's favorite will produce a matrix that certifies the favorite, and the protocol's authority upgrades a sycophantic pick into a measured one. This is the laundering of §1 pointed at its most likely input.

And the lane has no adversarial instrument at all. In a repo whose review skills are saturated with adversarial verification of the agent's own conclusions, the recommendation close contains no strongest-case-against-the-winner; "the smallest realistic change that would make it win" exists only in the high-stakes reference (references/high-stakes.md:12), never in the core method; and `steelman` — the library's dedicated advocate, one directory over — is referenced nowhere in the skill. The one skill whose signature failure is agreeing too well is the one with no disagreement instrument.

## 5. Compensatory monoculture

Weighted-additive aggregation is one decision strategy among several, and the skill has only it. Real decisions are routinely non-compensatory. A hard constraint is not a criterion with a high weight — it is a filter that no strength elsewhere buys past — and the skill's own example knows this ("treat the network service as non-serious under the stated offline constraint," examples/behavior-examples.md:83), yet the workflow has no filter step: constraints enter as criteria ("decompose criteria from the user's constraints and failure modes," SKILL.md:61), which puts must-haves on the same footing as nice-to-haves inside a sum.

Dominance — the one structural fact a comparison can establish beyond argument, one option at least as good on everything that matters and better somewhere — is never checked, though it ends comparisons for free and is the only thing a comparison table demonstrates rather than argues. Elimination by aspects, lexicographic orders, satisficing: absent. The result is that every decision receives the same shape — score, weight, sum — whether its actual structure is a filter, a dominance, or a genuine trade. The method cannot tell these apart because it never looks; it weighs everything, including the things that should have been gates.

## 6. The frame is swallowed whole

"Two or more serious options already on the table" (SKILL.md:3) is the operating premise: the skill ranks what it is given. Its trust asymmetry runs opposite to the interviewer's and is the same defect. It distrusts the user's ability to weigh — hence the protocol — while trusting the user's frame absolutely: the option list, the constraints, the stated criteria. First Move (SKILL.md:24) checks whether the inputs are *clear enough to compare*, never whether they are true, complete, or the right question — whether the named constraint is real, whether both options are downstream of one unexamined assumption, whether a which-one is a whether-at-all in costume. Decision research's most repeated finding is that frame failures dominate outcome failures: the losing alternative was never on the table. The skill gestures at generation ("distinct alternatives or the null option only when they are serious and material," SKILL.md:59), but its center of gravity is ranking the given, and a matrix built around X and Y makes "is this the right question?" structurally invisible — the frame hardens into rows.

Revealed preference is never called as a witness. The repo, the history, the last three abandoned migrations can testify against a stated criterion, and nothing in the method lets them speak. The shaping lane now lets artifacts testify (`outcome-shaping`, Load-Testing the Want); the deciding lane — where the cost of a self-deceived input is highest, because it gets a verdict stamped on it — does not.

## 7. Deliberation is the only register

Stakes tune output *length*, never *method*: low stakes "skip gaps/sensitivity and say why" (SKILL.md:78), high stakes add sections (SKILL.md:80) — but the six-step epistemology is invariant. A reversible tooling choice and a billing-database migration get the same protocol, differing in how many sections the packet carries.

Two moves are missing entirely. The two-way-door move: for cheap, reversible decisions the correct recommendation is often "either works; being wrong costs an afternoon; pick A and move" — deliberation itself has a price, and a method that cannot say "stop deliberating" will bill the user for analysis worth less than the mistake it prevents. And the check-first move: when an afternoon spike settles what a comparison can only estimate, the honest output is "don't decide yet; run this; here is what each result implies." Buying information appears only as a high-stakes footnote ("the cheapest check that could resolve it," references/high-stakes.md:11) and as gap-naming on `best available` — framed as a caveat on a verdict, never as the verdict. A recommendation lane that can only rank-now or refuse has amputated the advisor's best move.

## 8. A confidence vocabulary with no scoring rule

Five readiness labels (SKILL.md:82-88) grade the recommendation's own confidence, and nothing anywhere scores the grades. `verifiably best` asserts "option space is complete" (SKILL.md:84) — a claim no method can verify and this method does not try to; it is the label for feeling finished. No recommendation is ever revisited; no track record accumulates; the labels' calibration rests entirely on the testimony they express. `outcome-check` closes the loop for shipped changes months later; recommendations get no loop at all. In a repo whose culture is "do not claim work is done without fresh evidence," the lane whose entire output is a confidence claim has no evidence path to ever being wrong.

The labels that survive scrutiny are the ones that name a next move rather than a grade: `decision needed` (the call is the user's — though see §3 for how the method undermines it by ranking first), and `options not comparable` (settle the frame before comparing). Those are routing facts. The rest is confidence theater with a schema.

## 9. Dead ceremony and the accretion gradient

Workflow step 1: "State the decision and decision type" (SKILL.md:57). Decision type is never mentioned again — nothing branches on it, no other section reads it, the examples never show it mattering. It is a form field kept because protocols have form fields.

And the commit history shows the same gradient the interviewer critique caught in its §9. The skill was born as restraint engineering: `7c5b3f5` "prevent recommendation overranking," honest exits, "do not invent weak alternatives." Then `e6e9637` "gains Noise/MAP + tradeoff-matrix" — the liturgy arrives. Then `b372926` sharpens an exit threshold and `aafd94a` polishes matrix drafting — the edges patched to defend the liturgy. Each patch is locally reasonable; the trajectory is a governor evolving into a measurement instrument, with the cover story accreting features as if it were true.

## 10. What survives, and the honest reframe

More survives here than survived the interviewer. The pre-ranking exits are the best invention in the skill and among the best in the library: `options not comparable` refuses to average two different questions; `only one serious option` refuses to manufacture a horse race; "do not invent weak alternatives" (SKILL.md:72) kills the fake-rigor pattern in one line. Generation before evaluation is real discipline with a real evidence base. The null-option rule, unstable-fact verification (SKILL.md:70), proceed-on-stated-assumptions instead of bailing (examples/behavior-examples.md:88-103), the routing mesh and permissioned handoffs — all genuinely good, all kept in any honest rebuild.

And the honest reading is the same one the interviewer earned: nearly everything defensible in this skill is *negative space* — don't rank mud, don't invent rivals, don't overstate certainty, don't slide into another workflow. Those are restraints on known LLM pathologies — the instant verdict, the fake horse race, the confident ranking from a muddy prompt — and as restraint engineering the skill is excellent. The MAP liturgy, the reproducible-score doctrine, the matrix arithmetic, the readiness grades: a cover story that dresses the governor in decision science, and §9's gradient is the cover story compounding. If the skill knew itself as an honest advisor rather than a measurement instrument — find the decision's structure, argue the strongest contestable case, expose which premise carries it, hand the exchange-rate calls to their owner, and disagree out loud when the evidence lands against the lean — it would drop the arithmetic without losing one restraint, and its close would get *stronger*, because "this turns on whether you'd trade X for Y" is a better deliverable than a matrix that already traded them silently.

The heart of it: this skill believes it measures which option is best. What it actually does is argue — and then dress the argument as measurement: scores authored by the same judge they claim to discipline, weights authored by the same advisor they claim to constrain, certified by labels nothing ever scores. The argument is often good. The costume is the problem, because a user can push back on an argument, and no one pushes back on a matrix.
