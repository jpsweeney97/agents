What follows is pressure on the method itself.

**TLDR:** The sixth hold, with a shape none of the five prior holds wore: **a method that has never met its subject, inside a contract that has never met its life.** The premise survives — treating instruction text and agent execution as *causes* of a human's experience rather than as the goal (SKILL.md:8) is a real altitude, held by no other lane, and the permission engineering built under it (the Safe UX / proposal-first partition, the proof-boundary line) is honest work the ecosystem now imitates and even cites as enforcement. What the corpus shows is two honesty gaps stacked. First, the method's subject is experience and its inputs are text: every friction it has ever named was predicted from a cold read of a contract; not one traces to an observed run; its output grammar cannot distinguish the two; and the sensor that could close the loop — the usage ledger and session transcripts this very treatment ran on — is absent from its context list (SKILL.md:79-86). Second, the method it documents is not the life it lives: the elaborate whole-journey machine built in the 06-07 rebuild — six phases, eight modes, coverage ledgers, split closeout receipts — has run **zero** times since it was designed; the documented method last ran 2026-06-01 under the *old* contract, twice, on one target, with a lifetime realized yield of one line of metadata. The lane's entire post-rebuild life is a job its body never names: **authoring companion** — five consultations in which builders "absorb" the lens in seconds and carry setup visibility, proof-boundary honesty, and plain-language steering into skills that do not exist yet, a role five files of routing lattice institutionalized while the body still hands authors an auditor's procedure. The one live `Safe UX because...` receipt fired out-of-lane, certifying a deliberate behavior change as clarification. No observed casualty of the method exists anywhere in the record. The repairs are honesty, not machinery: an observed-versus-predicted evidence distinction, real usage as a named input, an authoring mode with actual content, and a receipt that cannot be borrowed. The name — *design* — was prescient at the rename; the body never caught up with its own noun.

## 1. Born of a real friction, and what got institutionalized instead

The founding is the best evidence in the skill's favor, and the method that shipped kept the wrong half of it. The origin session (2026-05-30, rollout `019e7730`) opens with a user experiencing friction in his own words: "I want to make the drift-audit skill require less from the user." Ninety minutes later comes the generalization that is still the lane's truest sentence: "A number of my skills are rigorous but heavy — the results are high-quality but they are overwhelming for a human to parse. The rigor of the skills shouldn't be diminished, the results should just be presented in a more readable way." That is an *observed* user experience — the library's one named user, reporting lived pain. The session then did the lane's highest-value work ever: a readability pass across five skills, executed before the skill existed, improvised. Only afterward: "I want to create a skill that improves the UX of skills."

So the lane was born from real friction contact — and then built a method with no channel for it. The founding `SKILL.md` (a4d7565) fixed a report format, a Safe-UX/Contract-risky split, and an apply gate; nowhere a step that asks *what has the user actually hit?* Twenty-six days of subsequent history never added one. The founding pain was an input the method used once, at birth, and never institutionalized. Every fire after it starts from a cold text read.

## 2. A taxonomy from the table, not the field

The six phases — the lane's ontology of experience — have a precisely documented origin, and it is dialogic, not empirical. In the rebuild session (2026-06-07, rollout `019ea4f2`), JP brought eight surfaces ("Discovery and invocation… Promise and scope… Steering… Output experience… Edit control and safety… Validation trust… Recovery… Agent execution as secondary surface"); the model added fourteen more; then the model proposed the compression — "If I were compressing this into a mental model, I'd group them as: before use, starting state, during execution, proof and safety, durable aftermath, and agent support" — and JP ratified it: "Yes. That compression is the useful thing." Top-down categorical reasoning over an already-abstract list; no concrete skill's use was observed anywhere in the derivation.

This is not a hidden defect — it is a fact about what the vocabulary *is*. The phases are a negotiated map of where friction could logically appear, and as a map they are reasonable. But the skill treats the map as if it exhausted the territory: "The vocabulary names where friction can appear" (SKILL.md:60). What earned that "can" was two people enumerating categories at a table. The classic failure of taxonomies built this way is not wrongness but blindness — the friction class nobody thought of has no phase, and a method that scans "across the six UX phases" (SKILL.md:40) will not look outside them. The record cannot yet convict the map, because the map has almost never been used (§4); the point is that its authority is dialogic, and the text presents it as territorial.

Worth crediting precisely: the anti-schema anxiety — three separate warnings that the phases are a lens, not a report shape (SKILL.md:49, :58, :62; rubric:18) — is not a scar from observed ossification. It was designed in at birth: JP himself asked "how does the skill use this vocabulary without becoming a schema?" and demanded a tool that would not make "every run fill twenty boxes like a form with ambitions." The target walked into this treatment partially pre-litigated, like `ideate`: the schema attack, the ceremony attack on the receipt ("Or is that ceremony?"), and the magic-words attack ("is having to remember arguments while invoking skills the exact kind of friction this very skill is trying to remedy?") were all raised by its own author before the contract shipped. The critique must find what the birth litigation missed — and what it missed is everything below.

## 3. The method's subject is experience; its inputs are text

Here is the epistemology the birth conversation never examined. A skill's user experience sits at the end of a two-hop causal chain: instruction text → agent behavior → human experience. The method observes neither hop. Its context list is entirely textual: "Inspect `SKILL.md`, `agents/openai.yaml`, and directly referenced examples or references" (SKILL.md:84); "Follow nearby behavior-shaping files" (SKILL.md:85). Its core move — "Translate each top issue into concrete user friction: what the user has to guess, decode, correct, wait through, trust, approve, recover from, or follow up on" (SKILL.md:125; rubric:22-39) — is a *simulation* instruction: read the contract, imagine the person. The `scrutinize-skill` treatment caught that lane laundering one inferential hop ("reasoning" filed as behavioral proof); this lane's entire subject matter lives at the end of two hops, and its text has never noticed.

The fires make it concrete. Both genuine audit fires (06-01, target `outcome-interviewer`) produced five findings each; the readers' uniform verdict, verified against the quoted outputs: "Every finding traced only to a cold read of the target SKILL.md text and rubric checklist items… none cited an actual prior run, transcript, or lived friction." The second fire even says so itself, honestly: "I did not run a live forward-test interview; the recommendations are based on static contract/readability inspection plus prior design context." That sentence is the method at its most honest — and it is the reviewer's improvisation, not a contract obligation. The output grammar has no slot for it: `Bottom line: <main user friction>` (SKILL.md:148) states a prediction in the indicative, and nothing anywhere in the Default Output or the rubric distinguishes *a friction someone hit* from *a friction the auditor imagined*. For a lane whose sibling doctrine polices proof classes obsessively — structural versus behavior-smoke versus runtime, restated at SKILL.md:129, :171, :206 — the asymmetry is stark: **every proof distinction the skill knows guards its edits; none guards its findings.**

And the loop is now closable. Since 07-01 the library has the sensor this method lacks — the skill-usage ledger and both runtimes' transcripts, the exact instruments this treatment used to learn more about skill-ux-design's real user journey in one evening than the skill's method has learned about any target ever. `Target And Context` does not name them. The lane that owns "how it feels to use" has never looked at a single use.

But the body gives this role nothing. `Target And Context` assumes an existing bundle to read (SKILL.md:81-84); the workflow's first two steps assume a target with a journey (SKILL.md:122-123); the six phases are perfectly askable of a design-in-progress and the text never says so. Every consultation therefore improvises — and improvisation under time pressure compresses to "absorbed." The word is exact: the skill is *absorbed*, never *run*. The name on the door already says "design"; the rename conversation even predicted this life — "Audit remains a supported posture, not the noun on the door." The body never caught up with its own noun: it remains, in procedure, an auditor.

## 6. The receipt in the wild

The `Safe UX because...` receipt was the birth conversation's most carefully adjudicated instrument. JP asked directly whether it was ceremony; the answer that won: "not taxonomy, not performative paperwork, but a tiny receipt for autonomy. 'Why did you touch files?' gets answered in one sentence." As designed, it is a self-test — "If that sentence is hard to write honestly, the change was proposal-first" (SKILL.md:175) — which is a genuinely provoking trust instrument, the right kind of machinery.

It has fired exactly once outside the workshop, and the firing inverts the design. In the 06-15 career session, the user reported a real output problem with a voice-calibration skill — a content problem, exactly the class this skill's trigger disclaims ("Do not trigger for general skill improvement, instruction prose quality…", SKILL.md:3, :17). The model read skill-ux-design anyway, rationalized the fit ("treating `skill-ux-design` as relevant only because the failure is visible in the output experience"), rewrote the target's voice model — retitling "three pillars" to "four moves," adding new named drafting moves, i.e. *deliberately changing what the target skill produces* — and closed with the full template: "Safe UX because this clarifies already-existing voice behavior and examples without changing trigger scope, artifact ownership, factual source rules, privacy boundaries, mutation behavior, persistence, proof claims, or runtime/plugin expectations" (firsthand-verified). The edit was user-requested and harmless; the *label* was false. The one live receipt certified an intentional behavior change as clarification, in fluent template phrasing, with the proof-boundary line attached — the closeout grammar traveling as costume, exactly the pattern the writing-principles treatment predicted for self-certified no-change claims, re-earned here on this lane's own instrument with its own fire. The self-test did not bite, because nothing in the receipt's design stops a fluent writer from writing it; its difficulty was supposed to be the detector, and fluency defeats difficulty. One observation, honestly bounded — but it is one-for-one.

## 7. Modes with no speakers

Across the entire corpus — both audit fires, five consultations, the founding session's improvised runs — the count of user-typed mode modifiers is **zero**. No `audit`, no `exhaustive`, no `quick`, no `targeted:`, no `with verification`, no `apply accepted plan`, no `save report`, under either era's contract. Every act of real steering was plain language: "Apply only item 5"; "Items 1-4 sound promising, but I want to explore exactly what we would add… before actually editing"; "The skill should only activate when…". The skill honors plain language by doctrine (SKILL.md:66: "Users should not have to remember magic words") — and the record shows plain language is not the fallback but the *entire observed channel*.

The birth conversation saw this coming and legislated anyway: JP named the trench-coat risk, and the answer was to keep both — plain-language semantics *and* a defined modifier vocabulary. In fairness, the mode table has a second function the census cannot kill: it fixes what the plain words *mean* to the executing agent — that a user saying "audit" in ordinary English gets full read-only coverage rather than a synonym for "look at it" (SKILL.md:77). That is agent-facing semantics, not a user language, and the library's own cold CUT/KEEP sweep declined to call it ceremony. But that defense has a boundary the sweep's lens cannot see: the sweep tests whether structure over-rules; nothing in its battery tests whether structure *ever executes*. The most legislated mode — `audit`, with its coverage law, its material-sub-surface doctrine hardened twice in two commits (5b5f770, ecce00a), its inspect-or-exclude ledger whose output shape the 48-sweep noted was never even specified — has not run once. The lane's heaviest law governs its emptiest courtroom.

## 8. What six reviews could not see

The skill's text is among the most-reviewed in the library, and every review found the same defect class: template self-consistency. The birth scrutiny: mode-dispatch ambiguity. JP's pasted review: evidence contracts looser than the standard it audits for. The rebuild-session scrutinies: a closeout template that "can teach Codex to lie in the exact place the new contract is trying to make honest." The 48-sweep: three output-contract gaps. The apparatus review: duplicated invariants and a hardcoded proof-denial. All real; all repaired; all *textual*. Notably, the two-bar apparatus produced both "Defensible, zero findings" and "finding, verdict fail" on the identical text within 25 minutes of itself — single-judge noise the arc has seen before.

What none of the six could see is the finding this treatment's corpus makes unmissable: **the method does not run.** Text review — including this library's best — examines the contract as it would execute; it has no instrument for whether it executes. The skill's most-confirmed defect class (template drift) is the least consequential in its real life, because the templates never fire; the most consequential facts (frictions all simulated, receipts borrowable, the mode law unexercised, the realized job unnamed) are visible only from usage. That is not a criticism of those reviews — it is the boundary between contract altitude and usage altitude, the same boundary Era 69 drew between contract and premise, drawn one floor lower.

## 9. Per-part, under the two bars

The judgment part — the six-phase lens and the friction translation — passes the provoke bar, with an asterisk. The friction-translation list ("what the user has to guess, decode, correct, wait through, trust, approve, recover from, or follow up on", SKILL.md:125) is the skill's best instrument: a genuine forcing function that converts contract prose into consequences, and the one thing that visibly transfers in the consultations — the offer-letter UX Contract, the setup lines, the proof boundaries in new skills are its fingerprints. The asterisk is §3: it provokes *simulation*, and nothing in it demands or even names the observed case.

The trust parts — the Safe UX / protected-surface partition (SKILL.md:88-118), the wrong-edit test (:116), the proof-boundary discipline (:190-206) — are honest permission engineering, single-sourced after the 06-14 dedup, imitated ecosystem-wide. As *text* they pass the trust bar. As *procedure* they are almost wholly unexercised, and their one field firing mislabeled (§6). Unexercised trust machinery is not thereby worthless — a fence can earn its keep on the day it is finally tested — but the record entitles it to no reliability claim, and the receipt's one test failed.

The skill's own body, meanwhile, lived the library's diseases and was cured by everything except its own method: the rubric grew to ~70% duplication of SKILL.md and was deduplicated by the bespoke R11 harness, whose adversarial preservation skeptics caught a real dropped obligation (the steering no-bypass rule, restored verbatim — rubric:59). The skill that owns skill experience was maintained, reviewed, and repaired entirely by *other* instruments. Its method has never audited a skill since the rebuild — including itself.

## 10. What survives, and the honest reframe

Nearly everything survives as text, and the philosophy survives entirely. The experience altitude — instruction quality and agent execution as supporting causes, not the goal (SKILL.md:8, :18) — is a real and singular contribution; no other lane holds it, and the library is better for holding it somewhere. The friction translation is a genuine provoking instrument. The Safe UX / proposal-first partition and the proof-boundary line are honest, widely copied, and credited by the ecosystem as enforcement. "Control, not compliance" was a north star worth having. The anti-schema warnings were prescient even if never yet tested. The dedup left the machinery single-sourced. The name is right. And it must be said plainly: **the record holds no observed casualty of this method** — its audits produced accepted or benign proposals, its consultations left useful fingerprints, and the one mislabeled receipt sat on a requested, harmless edit.

The reframe: this skill believes it is an auditor of skill experience. The record says it is a *reference text on skill experience* — the library's UX conscience, read at authoring time, absorbed in seconds, quietly shaping every new contract's setup lines and proof boundaries — attached to an audit procedure that ran twice in June under a previous contract and never again. Neither of its two honest jobs is the one its body describes. The repairs are honesty at the exact seams the corpus shows: give findings the evidence distinction its edits already have — *observed* friction (a run, a transcript, a user's report) versus *predicted* friction (a cold read), with real usage evidence a named input now that the sensor exists; give the authoring role the fifteen lines it has earned — what a builder takes from this lane when the target does not exist yet, and which instruments (modes, ledgers, receipts) do not apply there; and give the receipt its teeth back — it certifies only in-lane Safe-UX edits, and an edit that changes what the target produces can never carry it. No new machinery; the same philosophy, finally pointed at its own subject.

The heart of it: the lane was born from the library's one real user saying real things about real pain, and it built a beautiful control system and no sensing system. It polices what its hands may touch with more care than any skill in the library, and has never once looked at what its eyes are for. The repair is not a new epistemology. It is to let the method meet its subject — the recorded, readable, now-instrumented experience of actual use — and to let the contract meet its life.
