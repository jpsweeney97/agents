# Implementation-Planning: the plan as a second implementation, and who actually reads it

> Saved 2026-09-24. This is the first treatment of `implementation-planning` and the first on a plan-cycle skill, commissioned fresh by JP on the bare path with no emphases. The commission dossier (`.agents/treatments/2026-09-24-implementation-planning/DOSSIER.md`) was sealed before any transcript was read and is corrected in writing in section 9. The seal was not clean: the same-day design-exploration brief, the plan-cycle CHANGELOG, and commit `2fa887f` had already handed this target two findings and two repairs hours old (the 2026-08-31 plan written on three unconfirmed compressions; five receiving-lane decisions made without a check-in), and the dossier disclosed them as hypotheses to re-verify, not findings to carry. Three circularity traps were named and refused: the critique was not routed through `scrutinize-skill`, whose chat-only review produced today's F1–F4; it was not conducted as an implementation plan (no numbered tasks with complete replacement text, no `Planner decisions` list, no Self-Review or Outside-View pass on the critique); and the adversarially won laws (the Outside-View pass, `9b70de8`; the grounding and ordering bullets, `2d2e75b`; the Stage B folds; today's F1–F4 and seam bullet) were not re-litigated cheaply. Privacy: the career venue, JP's work repository, and several personal projects hold most of the fires; all were read for method behavior only, no career, work, or personal substance appears here, and nothing outside this repository was touched. Line references resolve against `git show f20c51e:plugins/plan-cycle/skills/implementation-planning/SKILL.md`; fire references name the conversation extract in the treatment workspace (`.agents/treatments/2026-09-24-implementation-planning/evidence/transcripts/`) and the turn or extract line; `execute-plan` references resolve against the same commit.

I read the current text and its ten commits, the donor it was mined from, the consumers that name it, the complete fire corpus on both runtimes at whole-transcript grain, seventeen reader reports, twelve transcripts whole myself, the Outside-View section of every plan on disk that has one, and every turn a claim below rests on. What follows is pressure on the method, in the words the fires used.

**TLDR:** A hold. The method's premise is a fiction about its reader and a prediction dressed as a fact, and both turn out to be productive. The text writes for "a skilled engineer with zero context for this codebase" (SKILL.md:8) and asks for "complete code in every code step" and "exact commands with expected output" (:24). In about two dozen execution episodes across the record, no human ever executed a plan by hand and no reader without the repository ever arrived; every executor was a model with the tree in front of it, and the one real zero-context reader is the fresh implementer subagent that `execute-plan` hands a single task packet and forbids from reading the plan (execute-plan:27, :32). Complete code is unrun code: it carried compile-breaking names in at least four plans, an impossible red-then-green sequence in one, a wrong predicted test count in another, and one renderer design that 749 passing tests could not fault and a Release smoke did. The field answered the prediction problem itself, five different ways the text never names: it ran the plan's code in a scratch mirror, assembled and measured the whole implementation and removed it, executed a schema in memory, probed the language with one-line scripts, and generated a real fixture to check a command. The method's own instruments do what they can. Grounding fires everywhere and catches real things before drafting. Self-Review runs in nearly every fire and catches something in about half, always a defect of shape: a placeholder, a drifting name, a fence, a file that does not exist. The Outside-View pass is present in 51 of 56 plans of its era on both runtimes, draws its base rate from the repository's own plans in nearly all of them, closes honestly in nearly all, and adds completeness tasks; it never caught a design or compile defect, and three times its section credits itself with catches that grounding or a review panel made. Every defect that would have broken execution was found by an instrument the text does not name: a readiness review, a pasted critique, an adversarial panel, `execute-plan`'s review gates, or execution itself. The plan's exactness is what makes those reviews productive, and it is also what makes them not converge: one plan drew a fresh Medium from each of twelve four-seat panels across seventeen plan-only commits, another grew to 3,800 lines over 86 plan-only commits and was retired by splitting. The record holds one observed casualty of the method, the 2026-08-31 plan built on three compressions the source had marked unconfirmed, already repaired by a sentence one day old with zero fires; it holds no observed casualty of an executor following a plan exactly. The repairs the record warrants are honesty at four joints and one sentence of fact, no new procedure: say what "complete code" and "expected output" claim and prefer the measured kind; name the executor that exists and let that fact carry the repeat rule the fiction carried; say what Self-Review is and is not; say what the grounding pin claims and that a frozen pin is itself a staleness hazard; and let a test reference only what an earlier or the same task defines, because three Swift plans learned that the compiler reads the whole target. The stop condition the hardening loop lacks belongs to `plan-panel-loop` and transfers there. This is the twentieth hold, and the shape is new to the genre in one way: the findings come from the plan's consumers and from what the fires invented around the method, more than from the method's own moves.

## 2. The implicit theory: a program for a reader who never arrives

The text's theory of the activity is stated in its first sentence and its standards. Planning is the complete transfer of a settled design into a document that "a skilled engineer with zero context for this codebase could execute without guessing" (:8). The transfer is complete when every path is exact, every code step carries complete code, every command carries its expected output (:24), nothing is left as a placeholder or a cross-reference "because the executor may read tasks out of order" (:25), and the plan "settles details the source never decided, and the executor will build those choices exactly" (:26). The theory of what the method can know is narrower than the standards, and the text says so in one place: grounding makes "every path, pattern, and command the plan names one you verified, not one you assumed" (:18). Verified covers the paths, the patterns, and the commands. It does not cover the code, and it does not cover the output the commands are expected to produce, because neither has run.

That is the joint the dossier named as the tempting inversion: the plan is the implementation written twice, in the register of verified fact, for a reader who never arrives. The record confirms the premises and refuses the inversion, and the reasons are worth stating exactly.

**The reader.** In every execution episode the executor was a model with the repository in front of it. `execute-plan` in subagent mode ran the July markdown-reader plans (`codex_2026-07-01_ef2168ef54`, `codex_2026-07-02_d94d160512`, `codex_2026-07-09_a7517c9a42`, `codex_2026-07-14_e72d7fa7c7`) and the agent-os plan (`codex_2026-09-05_2a6ede2051`); inline mode ran the later markdown-reader arc after the user said "Don't use subagents" (`codex_2026-07-24_8b1aa9c0b7 U7`) and then baked that into the goal text; the same assistant continued without the skill in two fires. No human executed a plan by hand. No executor read the plan as someone with "nothing about this repo's toolset, domain, or conventions": `execute-plan` re-checks every cited path and line against the current tree before each task (execute-plan:32, :46), and executors re-derived truth from the tree rather than the text when the two disagreed, deleting nothing when a property the plan said to delete still had seven call sites (R06 #4), and pixel-diffing frames rather than trusting either a human's PASS or its own first mechanical override (`codex_2026-07-12_863a963064 :1058`).

But one zero-context reader is real, and the text has it half right. In subagent mode `execute-plan` dispatches "a fresh subagent per task" (execute-plan:27), writes that task's packet with "the full task text, the live paths and line numbers, exactly the context the task needs," and instructs the coordinator: "do not make it read the plan file or inherit session history" (execute-plan:32). That implementer has the repository and does not have the conversation, the design, or the other tasks. It is exactly the reader for whom "repeat it rather than cross-referencing" (:25) and "reference no type, function, or method that no task defines" are written, and it is why the founding text's "the executor may read tasks out of order" is true in a sense the donor did not mean: the implementer does not read the tasks out of order, it reads one task and no others. The other sentence, "the executor runs them in document order" (:22, added 2026-06-15), is true of the coordinator. The dossier's Q8 asked which executor is real; both are, and they are different agents in the same run. The fiction of the zero-context engineer collapses two readers into one, and the standards it justifies are justified better by the two readers that exist.

**The code and the output.** "Complete code in every code step" was honored everywhere in the record; Self-Review removed the few prose-only steps that slipped (`claude_2026-08-26_51bf2f0a18 A129-A130`), and no committed plan carries a placeholder. Complete code was also, repeatedly, code that did not compile. A test-helper name copied into a plan that does not exist in the live tests (`codex_2026-07-06_3667341380`, found in the second of six pasted reviews); parameters shadowing the helper methods they call, verified by a reviewer with `swift -e` and re-verified by a `review-reviewer` adjudication with its own probe, "cannot call value of non-function type '[Int]'" (`codex_2026-07-07_ca3e0dde63 :1693, :1958-1974`); a recursive view builder that a compiler probe proved does not build (`codex_2026-07-05_5488fbdbdd U7`); a model change whose call site the plan never updated, "will not compile" (`codex_2026-06-28_a1331a95cc A19`). "Exact commands with expected output" produced one wrong number in the record, a test asserting 11 where the live fixture yields 12, caught by a readiness review's in-memory reproduction (`claude_2026-08-30_66f9597f60 U15`), and one impossible sequence, a task instructing four of five new tests to go red when the previous task's shape already satisfied them (`codex_2026-07-24_c4a19572c9 :1168`). The largest wrong call in the record was a design, not a line: a plan that rendered a whole document as one large SwiftUI `Text` with invisible anchors, which passed Self-Review, the Outside-View pass, and 749 of 750 automated tests, and failed the attended Release smoke on a 3,000-paragraph fixture with a blank view at a deep jump (`codex_2026-07-25_fbd7af9fb9 A164-A187`). Nothing in the method could have caught that one; the point is only what its passage through every instrument says about what those instruments test.

**What the field did about it.** The record's answer to the prediction problem is the most important thing in it, and the text does not name any part of it. In the newest fire the planner wrote the plan, then said: "Now verifying the plan's code by running it in a scratch mirror of the skill (not the satellite), so the stated test counts are measured rather than predicted" (`claude_2026-09-24_bc2abe8216 A48`), extracted every code block into the mirror, ran lint and tests at each task stage, found three defects (a helper polluting a host directory, a test reading through a symlink, imports failing lint at an intermediate commit), spliced the fixes back, and wrote a table of measured counts per stage. Seven plans in JP's work repository state that their implementation was assembled in the working tree, every claim measured live, and then removed before the plan was handed on, under a convention the fires call the ADR 0008 precedent (`claude_2026-08-26_51bf2f0a18 A134`: "the plan carries verified code, not predictions"). An agent-os plan executed its SQL schema in an in-memory database with the pinned runtime and wrote "This validated those plan elements only" (`codex_2026-09-05_2a6ede2051` plan :666). Two markdown-reader planners ran `swift -e` against a rendering assumption before writing it down and found a soft-line-break mismatch (`codex_2026-07-07_0e79579da0 A41`). A video-editor planner generated a real H.264 fixture and ran `ffprobe` on it to check the exact command shape before the command entered the plan (`codex_2026-06-28_6abeed1a3e A88-A89`). Two work-repo planners wrote throwaway parsers to prove the plan's embedded code blocks parse (R06 #3, #4). And the plan whose expected outputs were all qualitative ("nonzero executed-test count", "the build fails because X does not exist") never predicted a number it could not know (`codex_2026-07-25_fbd7af9fb9`).

So the field already distinguishes the two kinds of expected output the text conflates: the kind that was measured because the planner ran the command, and the kind that was predicted because it could not or did not. The honest statement of what `:24` warrants is that distinction, and the honest instruction is to prefer the first and mark the second. It sits in tension with `:14`, "Writing the plan grants no execution authority. Do not start implementing," and the work-repo convention resolves that tension by building in the tree and removing; the scratch mirror resolves it more cleanly. The repair is a sentence, not a procedure: measure what can be measured in a scratch copy, never on the target tree, and say which counts are predictions.

## 3. Where the plan is hardened, and the seat that does not harden it

Self-Review (:28-36) asks the author to check the draft "with fresh eyes" for coverage, placeholders, and consistency, then "fix issues inline and move on." In the record it runs in nearly every genuine fire, and what it is in practice is a regex scan for the placeholder patterns, a whitespace check, and a reread. It catches something in about half of the fires, and what it catches is always a defect of shape: placeholder-like markers and a literal hash string (`codex_2026-06-19_31548e8665`), a "squishy line" where a property name drifted (`codex_2026-07-08_5c1ffbe108 A65`), a code-fence nesting bug in a fixture (`codex_2026-07-06_254a1fe83f A23`), a file map naming a `ReaderState.swift` that lives in `ReadingTab.swift` (`codex_2026-07-04_dfb9c7d5c1 A89`), a `set -e` script that would exit nonzero on the no-match case its success path required (`codex_2026-07-09_a7517c9a42 A50`), a test sequence whose sleepers might never be entered (`codex_2026-07-25_571406de1a A8`). Twice it caught something deeper, and both times because the author ran a command: a wrong SHA-256 and a wrong expected string (`codex_2026-06-17_e829062f80`), and a shell function whose loop variable shadowed `$PATH` in zsh (`codex_2026-06-19_df7dd45513 A30`). The dossier expected a stamp that finds something in fewer than one fire in five (E4); the record is better than that on frequency and exactly as narrow on depth.

What Self-Review did not catch is every defect in section 2 and the whole list a reader in the career venue drew from four review rounds on one plan: "a literally impossible strict-tuple event match," a path-safety check routed to the wrong validator, a three-way enum spelling drift, a scenario count of six where the plan held nine (R07 #7). The reader's conclusion, which the rest of the corpus supports, is that the Self-Review triad "structurally cannot catch this class of bug, since it requires reasoning about the plan's own effect on the state it certifies." The one fire that ran an in-session adversarial self-review said so itself: "I authored this plan earlier in this session, so this is an adversarial self-review, not an independent seat," returned Ready to Execute with two Lows, and the independent Codex seat JP chose instead returned Patch Before Implementation with four findings, then one more (`claude_2026-08-31_68968f4f0c A85; relay packets 02, 04`). Self-Review is the author's seat. "Fresh eyes" (:30) is a phrase, not a mechanism.

The hardening that mattered came from a layer the text never names, and it ran in every venue. Pasted execution-readiness reviews with the verdict vocabulary "Patch Before Implementation" and "Ready to Execute" (the work repository, the career venue, agent-os); `review-reviewer` adjudications that confirm each claim against live code before patching (`codex_2026-07-05_5488fbdbdd A7`; `codex_2026-07-06_3667341380 A92`, "useful but not authoritative"); user-requested adversarial panels of three or four subagents (`codex_2026-07-02_d94d160512 U28`; `codex_2026-07-03_7c1cc93577`; `codex_2026-07-03_dcf209e261`; `codex_2026-07-03_83f716c77a`; `codex_2026-07-04_dfb9c7d5c1 U34`; `codex_2026-07-08_b3fa87f647 U6`; `codex_2026-07-08_5c1ffbe108 U25`); background cross-model reviews (R01 #4, #7); and `execute-plan`'s two-stage review gates, which surfaced defects the plan had not anticipated in essentially every executed task of the agent-os plan (R02 #3) and four `DONE_WITH_CONCERNS` verdicts in one refactor (`codex_2026-07-09_a7517c9a42`). The panels converged: three reviewers with different lenses found the same two P1 defects by different routes (`7c1cc93577`), and the reader's verdict on the July markdown-reader corpus is the sentence a judge should weigh most: "the skill's own two built-in review passes did not catch any of these defects in any transcript; every substantive defect surfaced either from an unscripted adversarial-panel step the user asked for, or from execution-time review gates in `execute-plan`" (R12).

The exactness earns this. A plan with complete code has a defect surface a review can hit, and a reviewer can run the offending snippet through `swift -e` and get a compiler error back; a plan of prose would have produced an argument instead (R14: "that literalness is exactly what let the bug be caught mechanically rather than argued about"). This is the strongest defense of `:24` in the record, and it is a defense of reviewability, not of verification. The plan's standard moves defects from execution time to review time, provided a review runs; the method's own review is the author's, and the text says nothing about the other kind. The consumers do: `execute-plan` begins with "review it critically before starting… do not execute a plan you do not believe in" (execute-plan:12), `scrutinize` carries the readiness register, and `plan-panel-loop` was built on 2026-07-08 to encode the panel loop (`4616498`). The repair in this text is honesty about the seat, not a review step; adding one would copy machinery the consumers own.

The same exactness is why hardening does not converge. The plan carries as many decisions as the code will, so a fresh panel finds a fresh Medium. In the 2026-07-08 fire the user said "Continue this loop until subagent panels deem the plan defensible" (`5c1ffbe108 U30`), and the loop ran from 03:10 to about 05:15 across twelve or more four-seat panels and about seventeen plan-only commits (3b2ea2d through 4daf4a1); each panel found at least one new Medium after the previous panel had cleared its lanes (module arbitration, post-integration proof, stale-smoke staleness, restore-marker uniqueness, a priority-latch lifetime, non-restore settlement), a reviewer subagent launched its own nested panel and edited the plan it was meant to review ("yeah Feynman edited the plan," U82), and "defensible" was declared when one panel returned three clean lanes. The next day JP asked what to change in `plan-panel-loop` and containment rules were added (`ddf1e6b`). The highlight plan drew six pasted review rounds and six patch commits before anyone executed it (`3667341380`), and the Flat Outline governing plan grew from 1,568 lines to 3,800 across 88 commits, 86 of them touching only the plan, until a bounded diagnosis called it "authority collapse: one 3,800-line artifact had become product plan, proof-controller specification, incident ledger, cleanup design, and visual oracle" and split it into three (`codex_2026-07-14_2d64de19b3 :346, :801`). The median task-shaped plan on disk is 1,216 lines. None of this is a casualty; every one of those plans shipped. It is the cost of the standard, and the text does not name a stop condition because the loop is not the text's; it is `plan-panel-loop`'s, and the finding transfers there (section 11).

One small instrument defect belongs here. The placeholder scan is a regex, and the plan's own sentence reporting that it contains no `TBD` contains `TBD`; the scan tripped on itself in at least four fires and the planners renamed the section ("Placeholder scan" to "Filler scan") or reworded the sentence to stop it (`d94d160512 A86-A88`; `dcf209e261 A107`, "Very on-brand for placeholder scans"; `5c1ffbe108 A67`; `ca3e0dde63 A54`). Harmless, and every time self-caught; a phrase in `:33` would end it.

## 4. The Outside-View pass in the field

The pass (:38-48) is the one instrument with a named epistemology and an adversarially won birth (`9b70de8`, an eight-lens default-to-refute review with no finding surviving and a five-scenario forward test). The dossier expected it to fire by heading in most post-06-29 Claude fires and fewer than half of Codex fires, to draw its base rate from general knowledge in most, to add tasks in about half, and to close honestly in a minority (E5). Every part of that was wrong, in the pass's favour. On disk, 51 of 56 task-shaped plans of its era carry the section, on both runtimes, plus 4 of 4 in the tells era and the one 09-24 plan; the reference class is drawn from the repository's own prior plans in nearly every one ("Comparable repo work includes Tab Continuity, Reading Positions, Local Images, and Readable Tables," `2026-07-04-outline-sidebar.md :1710`), and the class visibly compounds, each new markdown-reader plan naming the siblings before it (R12); the honest close is near-universal ("Limit of the outside view: … it does not certify that every macOS runtime will expose link activation identically," `2026-07-06-fragment-heading-links.md :1382`; "The plan does not certify the slice complete," `2026-07-06-highlight-return-points-v1.md :1913`). The readers who reported the pass "essentially unobserved" (R04, R05, R13) were reading transcripts whose patch payloads were hidden; the plans they could not see carry it. Uptake is near-total.

Value is the second column. The pass adds completeness, and it does so visibly: five tasks added or widened in the reading-positions plan (lifecycle flush, stale-position smoke, a settings-change regression, a closed-tab fresh-open smoke, final scope checks; `d94d160512`), two protections in the write-coalescing plan (generation-guarded sleepers, the existing message path for delayed failures; `571406de1a A7`), two omissions in the insulation plan (quiet-position protection, a protected Release relaunch smoke; `fbd7af9fb9 A140`), two execution hazards in the Flat Outline plan (presentation activity must exclude raw source-block changes; selected suites that run zero tests pass falsely; `ee5ddb6dde :1644`), three items in a work-repo plan (`66f9597f60 A72`). What the pass adds is proof and lifecycle work the class reliably needs; that is what its text asks for. What it never did, in any fire, is catch a design or compile defect, and it was not built to; the one plan in a work-repo group written without it later needed four Highs from an external review, which the reader rightly declined to call causal (R06 #1).

Two departures from the honest close, both small. One plan's chat close reads "Three outside-view reviews report no remaining model, test, runtime, or landing blockers" (`ee5ddb6dde :1942`), a stamp of the kind `:48` forbids, sitting above a plan whose section on disk does not stamp. The first plan in a new repository drew its base rate from general knowledge with no comparable record and did not say so (`codex_2026-07-01_b9d8a97b46`, reddit-app plan :376-382); today's F3 asks exactly that disclosure, and it had zero fires, so this is the shape it would have governed, not a miss against it.

The finding the record adds is attribution. The section is written last and after the fact, and three times it credits the outside view with what something else found. The playpen plan's closing section says the pass caught "the full-system pointer sweep beyond the obvious config (the two app bundles and the compiled menu-bar binary were exactly the stragglers it caught)" (agent-os dcff146 :793); the transcript shows grounding finding both at A40-A43, eleven turns before the plan was written at A51 (`claude_2026-08-31_1698540644`), and the same section absorbs "three review rounds" that ran in other sessions before the first commit. The navigation-intent plan's final section, after seventeen plan-only commits, lists as "Plan edits from the outside view" the explicit automated stale-command proof, the GUI-visible stale-outcome proof, and the restore seed and relaunch proof (`2026-07-08-reading-view-navigation-intent.md :1231`); those are the twelve panels' High findings (`5c1ffbe108 U25` onward). The highlight plan's Self-Review coverage list was rewritten after each of six pasted rounds so that "the coverage summary names the new safety checks" (`3667341380 A26, A60, A80, A109, A123`). None of this is dishonest in intent; the section is a record of what the plan covers, and it grows with the plan. But the text's close asks the pass to name what the comparison added, and a section that lists a panel's catches as the outside view's makes the pass look stronger in the record than it was. The repair is one clause: credit what the outside view added, and not what grounding or a later review found.

## 5. The plan's authority and afterlife

The text's artifact section (:50) names the path, the source-path header, the commit convention, and the executor, and stops. Downstream, `execute-plan` says what the plan is: "The plan is the contract; this lane owns faithful execution, not redesign" (execute-plan:8), plan changes "go back to the user or to `implementation-planning`" (:12), and since 1.2.0 (2026-09-22) a plan-text correction is recorded in the run's log without editing the plan, and every divergence is logged with its reason (:20-21). The record shows two other consumers the text does not name, and one fact the text has never stated.

The fact is the grounding pin. The dossier expected no plan to carry the commit it was grounded at (E9); the field carries it in at least five forms. A `BASE_REF` capture in Task 0 (`d94d160512`); "Source: `docs/specs/…` at live `HEAD` `3e4da73`" in the header (`codex_2026-06-24_4d15032b64 A19`); "Derived against main at" and "Planning baseline" in the work repository; and, three times in the career venue, "Source HEAD must equal current HEAD" as a durable-enabled proof, a rule that breaks the instant the plan's own committing task lands, invented independently in three plans and caught each time only by an external review (R07, cross-transcript). ## 6. The task grammar and the toolchain

Two bullets carry the task grammar: "each is buildable and verifiable given only the tasks before it" (:22) and "write the failing test, run it and watch it fail, implement minimally, run and watch it pass, commit" (:23). The second is the donor's; the first is house text from 2026-06-15. Together they assume a toolchain that can run one task's tests while later tasks' symbols do not exist. Swift under Xcode cannot. "Xcode compiles the whole test file even with `-only-testing`, so the focused extractor tests cannot pass while the Task 1 view-model test still references the not-yet-created `renderedBlocks` member. This is not a product-design problem, but it is a plan-ordering problem" (`d94d160512 :1302`, found by the executor, fixed by treating two tasks as one verification unit with the user's approval at U26). "Task 2's focused tests cannot compile in the planned order … Xcode still type-checks the whole test target before running selected tests" (`b3fa87f647 :592`, found by a panel, fixed by re-splitting the test file). "Impossible Task 2 sequencing instruction," four of five tests required to go red when Task 1's shape already made them pass (`c4a19572c9 :1168`, found by an inline `implementation-review` before publication). And the model change whose call site no task updated, "will not compile" (`a1331a95cc A19`). In these toolchains "watch it fail" means "watch the target fail to build," and a test that references a symbol a later task defines breaks `:22`'s guarantee even when every task is written in order.

The rule in `:25`, "Reference no type, function, or method that no task defines," is the right rule stated at the wrong grain: what matters is that no test references a symbol that a later task defines. With section 2's reader in view, the coordinator runs tasks in order and the implementer sees one task, so the tightening costs nothing. One clause on `:25`, "that an earlier or the same task has not yet defined," and one on `:23` that a failing test is a failing assertion, not a failing build, would have prevented all three.

The dossier expected TDD-shaped steps in under a third of tasks (E7). The Swift venues run far above that (about two thirds of tasks in the July plans, the rest being preflight, smoke, and publication); the work-repository plans show test language in 23 of 56; the four task-shaped plans in this repository for skill text carry no red/green steps at all, because there is no test to write for a sentence. The text has nothing to say about plans whose tasks are prose, config, or documents, and every such plan in the record simply omitted the grammar without comment. That is correct behavior against silent text; a clause saying so would cost one line and remove a false obligation.
