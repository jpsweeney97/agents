# Execute-Plan: the contract that the coordinator writes, and who actually decides

> Saved 2026-09-25. This is the first treatment of `execute-plan` and the second on a plan-cycle skill, commissioned fresh by JP on the bare path with no emphases. The commission dossier (`.agents/treatments/2026-09-25-execute-plan/DOSSIER.md`) was sealed before any transcript was read and is corrected in writing in section 9. The seal was not clean: the day-old implementation-planning brief had already characterized this target from the consumer side (executors re-derive from the tree; five unasked design decisions; two mid-run re-plannings; "Don't use subagents"), and the 1.2.0 and 1.4.2 commit messages and the unlanded 2.0.0 draft were read as mandated calibration. The dossier disclosed each as a hypothesis to re-find from the execution side; every claim below carries its own execution-side citation and none is cited from that brief. Three circularity traps were named and refused: the critique was not routed through `scrutinize-skill`; it was not conducted as a plan execution (no task packets, reviewer instruction files, rulings, status words, or timed coordinator log for the readers; the critique is single-mind prose); and the adversarially won laws (the 1.2.0 practices, the 2026-09-04 gap-review repairs, the idempotent-resume line, the 1.4.2 afterlife paragraph, the superpowers admission) were not re-litigated cheaply. Privacy: JP's work repository, the career repository, agent-os, playpen, and several personal projects hold most of the fires; all were read for method behavior only, no work, career, or personal substance appears here, and nothing outside this repository was touched. Line references resolve against `git show 8c8e826:plugins/plan-cycle/skills/execute-plan/SKILL.md`; fire references name the conversation extract in the treatment workspace (`.agents/treatments/2026-09-25-execute-plan/evidence/transcripts/`) and the extract line, or the on-disk record and its line; `LOG-M6 :n` is `~/agent-os/os-design/m6-execution-record/coordinator-log.md`.

I read the current text and its six commits, the donor practices it was mined from and the field run it was rebuilt from, the consumers that name it, the complete fire corpus on both runtimes at whole-transcript grain through twenty-seven reader reports, the three execution records on disk whole, the M5 method-changes file and the contained-review trial material whole, and every turn a claim below rests on. What follows is pressure on the method, in the words the fires used.

**TLDR:** A hold. The text's premise is "the plan is the contract; this lane owns faithful execution, not redesign" (:8), and the record shows that the contract is real and that the coordinator is the party who decides what it means. In about seventy-five runs on both runtimes, executors followed plans exactly where the plan's text was exact, and where it was wrong or silent they ruled: every task in the M5 and M6 records carries three to nine "divergences accepted" adjudicated by the coordinator; the M6 coordinator held a design checkpoint inside a plan task and issued nineteen numbered rulings, three of them outside the task's file list; and under the 1.2.0 text whose one sentence says "When the plan does not settle it, stop and ask" (:20), the same coordinator wrote "the plan does not settle it, and the ruling is the coordinator's (a divergence, in the report)" (LOG-M6 :685) and continued. The two bullets at :20 and :21 are the seam: one says ask, the next licenses a logged departure with a reason, and practice files nearly every open question under the second. That is not a casualty. The divergence log is the best-attested instrument in the corpus, the one thing every venue invented before the text named it, and the observed casualty is its absence: the July flat-outline saga, where a plan became the correction diary from design v14 to v48 across about forty-seven gate attempts and the coordinator said afterward "I should have changed strategy much earlier". The method's other claims fare as follows. The two-stage review is the instrument that finds defects, in nearly every subagent run: a credential at rest, a live-host deletion introduced by a fix round, a plan-embedded scanner bypass committed byte-exact, two ADR rules the plan's replacement text had dropped; its one observed miss was a design over-reach past both per-task and final review, caught by JP. Its absence is the second observed casualty: inline mode promises "the same gates" (:46) and names no reviewer, and three inline runs shipped correctness defects that an outside review then caught. The coordinator rerun is universal and caught wrong reports four times; the coordinator's own reads were also wrong once, and its claims about idle subagents wrong twice, caught by JP from outside. The subagent default (:27) never chose a mode: the Codex harness's own delegation policy chose inline from the first month, JP's word chose inline per session in July and by standing rule in the work repository, and JP's word chose subagents with Opus in agent-os; the text's trigger for inline mode fired in no run. A stop-and-ask on Codex was answered twice by a goal-continuation envelope rather than a human, and the coordinator proceeded on its own proposal. The 1.2.0 half of the text was not designed; it was transcribed from what the M5 and M6 coordinators had already built under a text that said none of it, at JP's ask, and it has three organic fires since. The 1.4.2 afterlife clause is one day old with zero fires and forbids two mid-run plan edits the record shows JP approving; that is a boundary to keep testing, not a finding against a day-old sentence. The repairs the record warrants are honesty at six joints and no new procedure: say who rules when the plan does not settle a question and where that ruling lives; say that mode is fixed by the runtime's policy or the user's standing word before the text's default speaks; say that inline mode has no reviewer and what to do about it; say that the coordinator's own reads and status claims are reports too; say that only a human turn answers a stop-and-ask; and make a plan that contains its own landing step a Load And Review question. This is the twenty-first hold, and its shape is new to the genre in one way: the fire layer here shows the text being written from the fires, so the findings are about the seams between what the text says and what the harness and the user already decided.

## 2. The implicit theory: a contract, and the party who interprets it

The text's theory of the activity is in its first sentence and its second section. Execution is faithful performance of a written contract: "The plan is the contract; this lane owns faithful execution, not redesign" (:8); "do not execute a plan you do not believe in, and do not silently 'fix' it either; plan changes go back to the user or to `implementation-planning`" (:12). Its theory of what the method can know is narrower and is stated at the record: the coordinator log is "the run's account in time order" (:18), a plan-text correction is allowed only when "the plan's own text settles which part governs" (:20), and every other departure is "a divergence" logged "with its reason" (:21). Where the plan does not settle a question, the text has one answer: "stop and ask" (:20).

The record confirms the contract and locates the interpreter, and the two are worth separating exactly.

**The contract is real.** Where the plan's text was exact, executors followed it exactly, including into defects. The cross-model coordinator failed a spec review over two import placements with "no behavior or ladder effect" and ruled fix-now because "the plan's text is unambiguous here" and "a departure from unambiguous text is a divergence the implementer did not report" (cross-model `coordinator-log.md` :96; `task-5-rulings.md`). A quality review in JP's work repository found a scanner bypass in code that matched the plan's embedded source byte for byte, and the coordinator asked "Do you authorize that repair?" before touching it (`codex_2026-08-25_29620fbf4b`). A Claude run in `.agents` wrote three quality findings in plan-specified code "for JP at completion — not implementer errors" and shipped the code as the plan wrote it (`claude_2026-07-16_9dd6d1ed37 :362`). A Codex run in JP's work repository sent a "refresh" of the plan back to `implementation-planning` before executing because "execute-plan explicitly treats the plan as immutable execution authority" (`codex_2026-07-14_e72d7fa7c7 A15`). The lane holds its boundary against the plan's own defects, and it says so.

**The interpreter is the coordinator.** Where the plan was wrong, silent, or behind the tree, the coordinator decided. Every M5 task carries a "Divergences accepted" list of three to nine items adjudicated by the coordinator, and a "plan-text corrections" list beside it (`m5-execution-record/coordinator-log.md` :7, :14, :19, :23, :26, :30, :31, :34, :40, :61; corrections at :8, :13, :17, :20, :21, :44, :59, :62, :74, :89). The M6 coordinator held a "design checkpoint" inside Task 15a and issued rulings D0 through D18, accepting, modifying, and refusing an implementer's design, three of the rulings recorded as divergences outside the task's file list, each with a reason (`m6-execution-record/tasks/task-15a-design-rulings.md`). The M5 coordinator wrote "Ruled by the coordinator: option 2 ... a sheet would change that design without JP; JP is told the sheet exists as an option" (M5 :89). Under the 1.2.0 text, the M6 coordinator wrote, on a question it named as unsettled: "the plan does not settle it, and the ruling is the coordinator's (a divergence, in the report)" (LOG-M6 :685, 09-23 07:45), and at :354 and :464 "a divergence authorized by the coordinator" for an internal accessor and an observer's shape. The M6 log has three occurrences of "authorized by the coordinator" and two stop-and-ask phrasings in 1,750 lines. Outside agent-os the same seat did the same work with less ceremony: "Ruling: remove the composer's ... button" to an implementer, not escalated (`claude_2026-09-06_5bee273c3b :1454`); "I ruled that the Rename box stays as it is, and the gap is recorded" (`claude_2026-09-15_1476e495b1 :1041`); "Ten places the plan was wrong, all resolved and each recorded in its commit message" in an inline run before any record existed (`claude_2026-08-20_d16507dcba :1173`).

The stop-and-ask branch does fire, and the record shows what it fires on. It fired on plan self-contradictions that the coordinator could not resolve from the tree ("Do you authorize ...?" four times in one inline run, `codex_2026-09-01_a3446555de`), on a design that collided with a live fact (D10 in `claude_2026-09-09_5160a16f31 :849–869`, JP ruled, the amendment landed as its own commit before the task commit), on a plan-versus-toolchain sequencing contradiction (`codex_2026-07-02_d94d160512`, JP: "I agree with that recommended correction"), on a policy conflict between commit rules and a no-commit commission (`codex_2026-08-09_9cc0c65daa :760–775`), and on a finding that turned out to be a defect in the approved design, routed to `spec-drift-reconcile` on authorization (`codex_2026-08-28_5f5215322c`). What went to JP was what changed the design, the scope, or what the user had asked for. What stayed with the coordinator was what the task's own verification and review could check.

So the text at :20 and :21 says two things in consecutive bullets, and practice reads them as one rule the text does not state. "When the plan does not settle it, stop and ask" (:20) is followed by "every departure from the plan's text ... goes in the log with its reason" (:21), and the coordinator files its open-question rulings under the second. The dossier asked where authority sits during a run (Q1): with the coordinator, with the divergence log as its license and the written reason as its accountability. That is the same seat the sibling brief found on the planning side, found here from the execution side with its own citations. It is not a casualty. The rulings were right nearly always, wrong once in the record and caught by a reviewer: "My own ruling caused that" (`claude_2026-09-14_d409428811 :1666`). The honest repair is not to forbid the practice but to say it: name which questions are the coordinator's to rule and which are the user's, and say that a ruling is a divergence with a reason, which is what the log already holds.

**The theory's second half, the record as the run's account, is the best-attested claim in the corpus, and its absence is the observed casualty.** Every venue invented a divergence record before the text had one: commit messages (`d16507dcba`), completion reports, `docs/verification/*.md` proof documents in the work repository, the M5 log from 09-13 under a text that said nothing about a log. The July markdown-reader arc is what the method looks like without it. Under the founding text, whose only sentence on plan changes sent them "back to the user or to `implementation-planning`" (:12), the executor had nowhere to write a correction except the plan, and it did: the flat-outline plan was patched and committed by the executor from "design v14" to "design v48" inside one run and to v57 or later across seven sessions, almost all self-directed, through `spec-drift-reconcile` and `agent-facing-design`, never through `implementation-planning` (`codex_2026-07-11_0e3e10a93c`; R22's seven sessions). About forty-seven gate attempts ran on the Task 8 gate, most of them "self-inflicted automation failures, not MarkdownReader failures" (:4428), and when JP asked why, the coordinator said "I should have changed strategy much earlier" (:4460) and, asked whether the lesson was captured anywhere, that it was not (:4473). The plan had become the correction diary and the run had no instrument that said stop and redesign. The text's later sentences, a log separate from the plan (:16–:21), "do not edit the plan" (:20), and "Do not rewrite the plan to match the code during the run" (:63), are that saga's repair, written two months later from a different venue's practice; the saga is the evidence for them, and it predates them.

## 3. What a per-task review can know

The dossier asked (Q2) whether fresh reviewers with a packet and a diff find what execution would have found anyway, or things only a reader finds. The record answers: things only a reader finds, in nearly every subagent run, and the misses are of one kind.

The catches are not nits. A quality review of a documentation task found a credential-at-rest defect in already-committed code because the reviewer checked the document's claims against stored data (`claude_2026-09-10_459c99ffa8 :612`). A spec review found "Three blocking misses", and a re-review after the fix round found "a trash step that would have removed a live host's folders", a regression the fix round had introduced (`claude_2026-09-07_713d375583 :432, :1228`). An M5 quality review found a HIGH outside the task's diff, a configured git clean filter that still ran, and the coordinator reproduced both vectors before accepting the repair (M5 :43). A spec reviewer in July found a state race by which "the settings store can save its old tab list and silently drop the newly opened tab" (`codex_2026-07-02_ccb0baa550`). A cross-model quality review found that the plan's own replacement text had dropped two standing ADR rules, and the coordinator ruled fix-now under the plan's own rule that the ADRs win (cross-model log :136). Two independent reviewers converged on the same blocker (`713d375583`). The order rule at :35, spec before quality, was enforced literally: "No quality review will resume until this specification pass is clean" (`29620fbf4b`).

The trust runs one way, and the text says so at :41 but not at :36. Reviewers took the coordinator's self-reported results as given: "I did not rerun `xcodebuild test`... I treated your stated 12-test pass as known verification" (`codex_2026-07-02_c1c70e84c6`); a final reviewer "did not run `xcodegen generate`, `xcodebuild test`, or the manual GUI smoke because this was requested as a read-only review" (`ccb0baa550`). The reviewer is read-only by the text's own design (:36), so what it can know is the diff against the packet and whatever runtime evidence the coordinator's instruction file gives it. The M5 seat scrutiny of the contained-review trial found exactly this: the original quality reviewer had five coordinator questions steering it to both defects plus runtime evidence, and the trial's bare reviewer had neither and found neither (`m5-execution-record/method-changes.md`; `reviews/contained-review-trial/summary.md`). The instruction file at :36 is not decoration; its content is what the reviewer knows.

The one miss the record holds is of a kind the reviews are not built to catch. In JP's work repository, a final `implementation-review` returned "Ship, zero findings", and JP's own independent re-measurement found that a repair commit had made a verifier "stricter than the design authorized"; the coordinator wrote "Position: reversed ... My previous zero-findings conclusion was wrong" and refused to merge (`codex_2026-08-31_f98eea0d3a`). Per-task and final reviews checked plan conformance and code quality; a design-level over-reach relative to a document neither reviewer was given slipped past both. The reviews know the packet and the plan; they do not know the design unless the packet carries it.

The ruling seat moved, and the record shows both positions. In July the implementer adjudicated the reviewer's findings itself and won: "The implementer's adjudication was accepted on re-review: all three quality findings were withdrawn" (`codex_2026-07-09_d8201116e0 :371`; also `06f1a93358`, "a naming preference, not a required correction"). The 1.2.0 rulings file (:37) put the seat with the coordinator, and in the 1.2.0 fires that is where it sat. No casualty of implementer self-ruling is in the record; the withdrawn findings were withdrawn on argument. The seat move is a design choice about who answers for a closure, not a repair of an observed failure, and the text should not claim otherwise.

The packet boundary (:32) fails from both sides, and the text names no remedy. Two reviewers breached it on their own ("a broad search accidentally exposed plan excerpts"; "cited plan lines despite being told not to read the plan") and the coordinator discarded both reviews and re-ran with fresh reviewers (`codex_2026-08-30_6c3a1d512f`); an implementer "found the live plan" and read it "to respect the task boundary" (`codex_2026-07-03_e2a06cf3ec`); and the boundary once hid a user authorization from an implementer, which "correctly refused because its isolated context could not see [JP's] original execution authorization", so the coordinator did the task inline (`codex_2026-08-26_0d011359e3`). On Codex the implementers reached the status vocabulary by reading the skill body themselves (`codex_2026-07-26_22f801ae41 :26`; the five M2 implementer transcripts, R09), which the text does not anticipate and does not need to.

## 4. Who verifies the verifier

The dossier's E5 guessed that under the pre-1.2.0 text the coordinator accepted reports without its own rerun in about half of subagent fires and that at least one wrong report went uncaught. Both halves were wrong in the direction that favors the method. The coordinator rerun before commit (:41) is universal in the corpus before and after the text named it, and it caught wrong reports: an implementer claimed fifteen of sixteen findings repaired and the coordinator's own read found one missed and three docstrings naming the wrong state (LOG-M6 :270); a final reviewer's ledger said twenty-one files and the coordinator's count said seventeen, and it required the reviewer to reissue its verdict (`codex_2026-08-31_f28f66928b`); an exit code was not saved (`codex_2026-08-28_5f5215322c`); bytecode caches inflated a count (`0d011359e3`). Task 8's implementer in M6 reran its measurements without the build slot and the coordinator ruled "its numbers are not the record's" (LOG-M6 :194–196). The cross-model coordinator's rerun matched the implementer's numbers at every task (cross-model log :35 through :139). No wrong report went uncaught in the record.

What the text does not say is that the coordinator's own reads are reports too. The coordinator misread a screenshot as a blank frame, classified it "Production behavior failure", committed the classification into the plan, and a later pixel-diff pass proved the frame unchanged (`codex_2026-07-12_863a963064 :724, :801`). Twice the coordinator told JP a re-review was running when nothing was: "It doesn't look like there is a re-review running" (`claude_2026-09-09_5160a16f31 U13`, "You're right. The quality reviewer went idle ... and never resumed"); "It doesn't appear to be running" (`claude_2026-09-14_d409428811 :746`, "You were right: nothing was running. The implementer started the check in the background and was never woken"). Once it "corrected a stale coordination status" of its own (`codex_2026-09-05_2a6ede2051`). The M6 coordinator wrote log headings ahead of the clock four times, which is why the `x`-digit rule at :18 exists and why it is honesty rather than ceremony: the corrections are marked in place (`claude_2026-09-24_c80faa5c44 :2805, :7277`; cross-model log :90, :93). The dossier's Q4 asked whether the timestamp discipline is honesty or ceremony; it is honesty about a specific failure the coordinator kept committing, and it is used.

The stop-and-ask has an assumption the text does not state: that the next turn is a human. On Codex under a goal loop it was not, twice. "May I make that narrow plan deviation and continue?" was followed by `<codex_internal_context source="goal">` "Continue working toward the active thread goal", and the coordinator proceeded on its own proposal (`codex_2026-07-25_98ba06521d :957, :970`). "My recommendation is a bounded correction ... That is additional implementation beyond the plan" was followed by an envelope re-issuing "`$execute-plan` ... inline - do not use subagents" (`codex_2026-07-25_fbd7af9fb9 :4722, :4736`). One genuine human answer to the same question shape exists in the same repository the next day: "May I proceed on that basis?" → "Proceed on that basis" (`codex_2026-07-26_01eedaefd9 :631, :695`). A Codex goal directive also overrode the repeated-failure stop from outside the skill: "Continue retrying until Task 8 passes ... Only use status 'blocked' when the same blocking condition has repeated for at least three consecutive goal turns" (`codex_2026-07-11_eac0853a28 :1492, :1531`). The text's "Ask rather than guess" (:58) cannot tell a human turn from a harness turn, and the record shows the harness answering.

## 5. Mode: a default that never chose

The text makes subagent mode "the default when subagent tooling is available" and inline mode the case "when no subagent support exists or the user prefers it" (:27–:28). In no run did the text's trigger decide. On Codex, from the first month, the harness's own delegation policy decided: "I'm going inline because the available subagent tool has a stricter rule: it can't be used unless you explicitly ask for subagents" (`codex_2026-06-26_940236fac6`); six of eight June career runs gave the same reason (`08eccd434c A11`, `8900cf032e`); the first video-editing run stopped to ask which mode and JP said "execute it inline with the same task gates and review checks", and the next session treated that answer as standing (`codex_2026-06-28_c96c01fb65`; `62e0b303a1`). In July JP said "Don't use subagents" or "Execute inline, not in subagent mode" in at least five markdown-reader sessions and then baked it into the goal text (`codex_2026-07-24_8b1aa9c0b7 U7`; `586da93a13 :829`; `fbd7af9fb9 :4736`). In JP's work repository on Claude, inline was "per your standing rule" (`claude_2026-08-20_551da2adf3 :227`). In agent-os on Claude, subagent mode was JP's word ("use Opus for the subagents"), and once JP moved one task to "a fresh session with subagents" mid-run (`codex_2026-08-14_6f9cbbd0cc U8`). Two hybrid shapes the text does not name recurred: inline implementation with subagent review gates ("keep the critical path local for the file edits and use agents as the review gates the execution skill asks for", `codex_2026-07-02_c1c70e84c6`), and one reviewer seat carried through both stages by follow-up (agent-os M1, `codex_2026-09-05_96c7962984`).

The dossier's E3 guessed the mode split was roughly 40/60 subagent/inline; the number is about that, but the guess was about the wrong cause. Mode was set by the runtime's policy and by the user's standing word, and the text's paragraph reads neither. The cost of that silence was small and recurrent: the correction was repeated per session in July because nothing in the text said that one answer stands for a repository, and one interrupted quality review was never rerun inline after a mid-run switch (`8b1aa9c0b7 :515, :526`).

**Inline mode has no reviewer, and the text's "same gates" (:46) is a claim it cannot honor.** The two-stage review, the instruction file, and the rulings file are subagent-mode instruments; :46 says "Packets, reviewer instructions, and rulings belong to subagent mode; the execution record and the checks below apply here too", and names no review for inline runs. Inline executors verified themselves rigorously, and three inline runs shipped correctness defects that only an outside review caught: the coordinator's own final review found nothing, JP invoked `implementation-review` separately, and it found three logic blockers ("Blocked", `codex_2026-06-18_eb18b44e90`); an "all green" self-verified inline run was followed by a pasted `review-reviewer` adjudication that found three correctness gaps reproduced by execution (`codex_2026-07-06_8933fbe963 U10`); and a 648-test suite was green while "Two concrete proof failures have now survived direct probes" (`codex_2026-08-20_25140120cf`). ## 13. What survives, and the honest restatement

What survives is the whole method, restated more honestly. The plan is the contract, and the coordinator is its interpreter: where the text is exact, execution is exact, into defects if the plan wrote them, and the reviews exist to catch that; where the text is wrong or silent, the coordinator rules, writes the reason in the log, and sends to the user what changes the design, the scope, or what was asked for. The divergence log is the method's best instrument and its absence is the method's worst observed outcome. The two-stage review finds real defects nearly everywhere it runs and does not run in inline mode, which the text should say. The coordinator's rerun catches wrong reports and the coordinator's own reads need the same suspicion. The mode is chosen by the runtime and the user before the text speaks. A stop-and-ask waits for a human. The 1.2.0 instruments were built in the field and carried into the text at the user's ask, and their job is to reach the coordinators who have not built them yet.

The house signature is signed consciously: the twenty-first hold, with a shape none of the prior holds wore. The fire layer here did not fund a verdict; it did something the genre had not yet seen it do, which is show the text being written from the fires, so that the findings are all at the seams between what the text says and what the harness, the user, and the coordinator had already decided.
