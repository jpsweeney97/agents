# Scrutinize: A Critique of the Methodology and Philosophy

> Saved 2026-07-03. JP commissioned the eleventh methodology-and-philosophy treatment, on `plugins/review-family/skills/scrutinize` — the generic adversarial-review lane. The named groove hazard was the remix trap at its arc maximum: no prior target adjoined a precedent as closely as this one adjoins Era 69 (same plugin, shared CANON cores, shared verdict family), so every Era-69 finding was treated as a hypothesis to re-earn from this lane's own fires or drop — and the two biggest (the altitude ceiling, reasoned-filed-as-behavioral) were dropped for lack of local evidence, not imported. The secondary hazard, inversion hunger after eight consecutive holds, did not decide: the verdict is a hold because the fires say so. Circularity refusals held: nothing was routed through `scrutinize-skill`; this critique is not conducted as a scrutiny of scrutinize (no reject-default stance, no severity labels, no scrutiny verdict); the adversarially-won laws (Era-69 altitude law, the bar-lens differential proof, the CANON cores) stand un-relitigated. The target is `plugins/review-family/skills/scrutinize/` as of commit `9392439`; inline line references resolve against that version. JP endorsed the critique in full on 2026-07-03 and it became the design brief for the repairs (commit `3d6594d`, review-family 0.7.0; the repairs invalidate specific line numbers but not the readings).

I read the bundle, its full lineage back through three repos to the founding prompt, the CANON drift-check script that pins two of its sentences, its prior text-review record, and — the arc's largest fire corpus by an order of magnitude — a scripted census of its entire two-runtime life plus deep reads of all thirty-five current-era fires and a twenty-one-session stratified sample of its earlier eras. What follows is pressure on the method itself.

**TLDR:** The ninth hold, with a shape none of the eight prior holds wore: **a verdict here is a fact about a search, and the contract mistakes it for a fact about the artifact — while the method's real life, an iterative convergence gate and lately a review panel, runs on discipline the text never wrote down.** This is the library's oldest and most-fired skill: born 2026-04-02 as a 47-line personal prompt ("Be an unforgiving reviewer. Assume this is not ready and that your job is to prove it"), hardened through two repo migrations, and consolidated on 06-08 by absorbing two retired siblings as its two modes — roughly 200 fire sessions across nine venues and two runtimes, almost all user-summoned, the purest case of capitalized articulation in the library. When it fires, the core pays extraordinarily: read-before-judge held everywhere; reviewers stood up broken HTTP servers, ran `swift -e` probes, executed checkers, and ran `strings` on a compiled binary to earn severity; they caught their own subagents hallucinating symbols and overrode their own evidence-sweep's false refutation by running the test suite; the record shows active anti-padding ("I am not padding this section to look thorough"), named refutations of the reviewer's own candidate findings, and a read-only floor clean in 55 of 56 sessions — the commission's severity-theater expectation is largely refuted in-session, and the one April natural differential (same ask, no contract loaded) produced a visibly less disciplined review, the rare direct evidence a contract earns its keep. What the corpus indicts is the contract's self-understanding. Sixty-eight of seventy-three multi-verdict sessions climb the ladder Reject→Major→Minor→Defensible: the dominant heavy use is scrutinize-as-convergence-loop — patch, re-fire, climb — and no era's text says one word about re-review; the discipline that makes the loop honest ("verify against the live file and the commit, not the description, before judging"; treat prior findings as hypotheses; credit exactly what held) is folk practice living only in transcripts, and once a user had to ask for it by hand. The clearance verdicts are where the philosophy bites: `Defensible` and `Ready to Execute` are exhaustion reports — the reviewer stopped finding — and the record demonstrates it twice (a design cleared `Defensible` whose committed artifact promptly yielded two new Criticals; a `Minor revision` plan whose next revision introduced a fresh Critical the prior three rounds could not have seen). The founding prompt knew this and priced it — "note it briefly only after exhausting serious attempts"; "if you cannot find many major flaws, say why it survives, then focus on residual risks" — and both the price and the honest exit were cut at the 05-28 compression and never returned. Meanwhile the newest life (twelve of the last fourteen fires are caller-scoped panelists) overrides every section of the template and keeps only the living core, improvising "for this lens only" while the CANON-pinned bounded-review core — the contract's own instrument for exactly that scoping — has fired zero times in the method's entire recorded life. The normal verdict enum is still sole-homed in a conditionally-loaded reference (a defect three blind reviewers kept on 06-16 and a 06-14 commit deliberately left), and the register drifts accordingly. The repairs are honesty and codification of what the fires already do — a verdict-scope sentence and the restored survives-clause, a re-scrutiny paragraph, bounded discipline extended to externally-narrowed reviews, the enum homed inline, one mode-honesty clause — and no new machinery.

## 1. A prompt that grew a plugin

The provenance is a shape the arc has not seen: not imported, not squad-born, not founding-rewritten, not assembled from mined material — **prompt-hardened and fold-assembled**. On 2026-04-02, in `claude-code-tool-dev` (`9f13c5c8`), JP wrote a 47-line personal prompt with an `$ARGUMENTS` tail and `disable-model-invocation: true`: "Be an unforgiving reviewer. Assume this is not ready and that your job is to prove it." Already present at birth: the two passes, the domain-adaptive lens list, per-issue fields, the four-token verdict enum, the adversary menagerie ("what an adversary, skeptic, reviewer, user, attacker, competitor, or reality itself would exploit"), and — mark this — an honest clean-target exit: "If something appears solid, note it briefly only after exhausting serious attempts to find weaknesses. If you cannot find many major flaws, say why it survives scrutiny, then focus on residual risks and failure scenarios." The intermediate version (04-02→05-06, same repo) added the loader description, "at least 3 adversarial perspectives," the bounded-mode ancestor ("prioritize the highest-risk areas and state what you did not review deeply"), and an ordering rule sharper than anything in the current text ("Do not look for what works until you have exhausted the serious ways it could fail") — and it kept the survives-clause. The 05-28 compression into this repo (`afa339f`, 34 lines) killed the survives-clause and the exhaustion price, keeping only the prohibition ("do not pad with weak objections"), while giving birth to citation calibration and the `uncalibrated / citation not inspected` marker. Then one day, 06-08 in the mirror repo, did the consolidation: `8b65c309` installed the `Target And Evidence` opener and gated the perspectives behind a materiality rule; `f9153b72` retired `adversarial-review` into the Formal Stress Test mode — killing its 1–5 numeric confidence score in favor of prose, a real epistemics win; `9cb5d0cf` retired `pragmatic-review` into the Execution-Readiness mode, importing `Ready to Execute` and `Patch Before Implementation`. Since canonicalization (06-09, `047c8ca`) the method text has only had edge work: description tighten, two single-sourcing passes, a `/triage` pointer, a reflow, a `steelman` handoff.

One more organ died in the folds, and its death is freshly relevant. The unversioned Claude-side variant that served `/scrutinize` in early June (recovered verbatim from three transcripts, 06-04/06-05) carried an **Evidence Sweep** clause: an opt-in orchestration (79–97 agents in the observed runs) that gathered and adversarially filtered citations while the text insisted "the sweep never renders a verdict. You keep the verdict" — and obligated the reviewer to spot-check the sweep's own claims. In the fires it worked exactly as designed: one sweep's refute-layer falsely dismissed a Critical and the reviewer caught it by running pytest itself; another reviewer caught a subagent hallucinating a symbol location and re-grounded every Critical by hand. That clause did not survive into the plugin. Three weeks later, orchestrated review became the method's dominant fire mode (§6) — with no contract governance at all. The consolidation shed its only orchestration law right before orchestration became the life.

## 3. What runs when it runs

The commission pre-registered severity theater — an obligation-to-attack stance manufacturing findings. **The fire corpus largely refutes it in-session.** What the readers found, uniformly, was the opposite discipline:

- **Read-before-judge held everywhere**, and routinely exceeded the text. Reviewers extracted a plan's embedded fetch script and stood up a deliberately broken HTTP server to prove its exception handling wrong before filing the finding (`5f6ca6b7`, re-verified: the probe produced the spec-compliant `fetch failed:` output only after the patch); ran `swift -e` probes to confirm or *discard* candidate findings ("If it's legal, I won't pad the review with it" — `019f25b1`, probe confirmed legal, finding dropped); executed the target repo's own checkers and test suites to ground stale-baseline claims; ran `strings` on the Codex binary to verify scan-path claims (`a7bc7b74`); web-fetched platform docs before severity-rating cross-browser claims.
- **Anti-padding is enacted, not just written.** "Critical Failures: None currently firing… I am not padding this section to look thorough" (`9892017c`); a candidate objection pruned as "a layering smell, not a hole" (`c62e35b1`); four refuted candidate findings surfaced by name rather than silently dropped (`560188a5`); explicit credit sections — "What Holds Up," "What Checked Out Clean," "Verified-OK (credit)" — appear across eras with no contract clause asking for them.
- **The method distrusts its own instruments.** A reviewer caught its evidence-sweep's refute-layer falsely dismissing a Critical and overrode it by running the suite itself (`ee93cd5f`); another caught a delegated subagent hallucinating a symbol at a specific line and re-grounded every Critical/High by direct grep before the verdict (`365c42fa`); a third re-ran its own line-numbering tooling after catching a sed slicing bug, "enforcing the citation-calibration norm against its own tooling" (`019f218b`).
- **The read-only floor held in 55 of 56 sessions.** The one wobble is self-caused and disclosed: a 05-03 review ran `trash` on the `.pytest_cache`/`__pycache__`/`.venv` residue its own test run had created inside the target tree (verified to the exact calls) — a cleanup of self-made mess, not a target mutation. Post-verdict mutations waited for explicit authorization everywhere, twice elegantly (review → user authorizes → patch → separate "Commit this" gate, `5f6ca6b7`).
- **The contract demonstrably adds discipline.** The corpus contains a natural differential: in April, the same user framing ("I will share the draft with you for a scrutinizing review") *without* the skill card produced a visibly weaker artifact — no verdict token, no premise check, no perspectives, closing "I would not approve the plan as written" (`019d9c86`, verified) — and the same session's carded rounds snapped to full discipline. Era-70's writing-principles critique asked what a bare careful agent would lose; here the record answers it directly.

Observed finding-noise is small and worth stating precisely: one headline finding retracted after the user corrected a two-plugins conflation ("I retract the blanket claim…" — `019d6616`); one Critical filed on conditional inference without a runtime check and flagged by the reader as an overclaim (`019eb2de`); one severity elevated in the write-up past the reviewer's own mid-review hedge (`019f2626-a3b3`); and one clearance revised downward when a pasted counter-review's live check contradicted it — "I mis-assessed it as sound" (`05405277`, verified firsthand). Against ~200 sessions, that is a noise record most human reviewers would envy — and it locates the method's real epistemic exposure not in its findings but in its clearances, which is §4.

## 4. A verdict is a fact about a search

Here is the implicit theory, made explicit. The stance — "reject until evidence earns a better verdict" (SKILL.md:8) — produces two epistemically different products under one vocabulary. A defect finding is an **existence claim**: this flaw is here, at this line, and it fails this way. The corpus shows the method earning those claims with evidence discipline that borders on excessive. But `Defensible` and `Ready to Execute` are **absence claims**: no disqualifying defect remains. A text pass cannot verify absence; what it can honestly report is exhaustion — *I searched seriously and stopped finding*. The verdict, in other words, is a fact about the search that produced it, and the contract's vocabulary presents it as a fact about the artifact.

The record demonstrates the gap twice, cleanly. In `019e3e71` (05-19), a design was scrutinized through two rounds to an explicit "**Defensible.** This is now ready to write as the artifact design" — and one round later, scrutiny of the *committed artifact* found two new Critical Failures, including a proof-gate missing its positive control (verified: the positive-control gap is in the transcript), forcing `Major revision` on material the design-level clearance had blessed. The reviewer had said the right thing on the way in ("I'll scrutinize the committed artifact itself, not the summary") — the point is that `Defensible` at round N was a true report of an exhausted search over one text and a false predictor of its neighbor. In `019df92c` (05-05), a spec climbed Reject → Major → Minor — and round 4 found a *fresh* Critical (an in-flight guard that could permanently wedge the tab) that rounds 1–3 could not have seen because the revision that introduced it did not yet exist; the trajectory ran Reject → Major → Minor → Major → Minor and never reached `Defensible`. Verdicts expire with the search that earned them, and nothing in the vocabulary says so.

The corpus-wide verdict distribution corroborates the reading. `Defensible` fired in 44 files — overwhelmingly in the April–May loop era, where multi-round exhaustion could genuinely be demonstrated — while the one-shot 48-skill sweep issued zero `Defensible` across 48 targets, and `Ready to Execute` appears in 11 files against `Patch Before Implementation`'s 59, essentially only on round-2 re-reviews after patches landed (`019f0f60` ×2). The top verdicts are reachable almost exclusively through the loop, because only the loop manufactures the exhaustion the clearance silently claims. That is not a pathology; it is the method's actual epistemology, working — un-stated.

What makes this a defect rather than a philosophical observation is that the founding text had already solved it and the solution was lost. The ur-prompt priced clearance in exhaustion terms — "note it briefly only after exhausting serious attempts to find weaknesses" — and gave the clean-target case an honest exit: "If you cannot find many major flaws, say why it survives scrutiny, then focus on residual risks and failure scenarios." Both clauses survived every version in the old repo and died at the 05-28 compression. The current text's only clearance guard is a necessary-condition sentence for `Ready to Execute` (SKILL.md:48); nothing anywhere says what a clearance *is*. Meanwhile the fires kept the practice alive as folklore — the credit sections, the residual-risk pivots at `Defensible` ("I would still be strict when condensing it into the dispatch packet, because the remaining risk is…" — `019dccb8` R6) — which is the recurring shape of this whole critique: the method is healthiest exactly where its text is silent.

The repair is verdict honesty, not machinery: a sentence stating that a verdict reports this pass's search — `Defensible`/`Ready to Execute` claim serious search exhausted without a disqualifying find, they do not certify soundness, and they expire when the artifact changes — plus the restored survives-clause. This rhymes with Era 69 (verdicts outrunning a true method) and Era 75 (the verdict noun doing work the method never did), and the difference should be owned: the sibling's verdicts silently claimed an altitude never examined; baseline's noun claimed fidelity when the method resolved governance; here the verdict claims a property of the *artifact* when the method earned a property of the *search* — and unlike both precedents, this target's founding text had already written the honest version.

## 5. The loop nobody wrote down

The trajectory census is the strongest structural fact in the corpus: of 73 sessions with two or more distinct verdict emissions, **68 climb** (Reject → Major → Minor → Defensible direction), 2 fall, 3 mixed. The dominant heavy use of this method — across April plans, May specs, June career plans, July app plans — is not a review; it is a **convergence gate**: scrutinize, patch, re-fire, climb, and stop when the verdict clears. Sessions run up to seven rounds. Users paste "All N fixes applied" and re-invoke. The verdict ladder functions as the progress meter of an iterative protocol.

No era's text acknowledges that this protocol exists. The contract is written as a single-shot review: one target, one pass, one verdict. Everything that makes the observed loop epistemically honest is folk practice, reconstructed independently by reviewers session after session:

- **Verify the patch, not the patch note.** "Good — you acted on the review. Let me verify against the live file and the commit, not the description, before judging" (`23875d0c`, verified verbatim) — that reviewer re-derived pass/fail from live code all four rounds. Round-2 reviewers re-ran the exact probes that had exposed the original defect (`5f6ca6b7`), re-executed checkers against new line numbers (`019f0f60`), and refused to recycle a stale verdict after a `/compact` ("I'll re-verify against live state before re-rendering the verdict" — `af39fea4`).
- **Treat prior findings as hypotheses.** The five-round Page Turner review re-read the file fresh each round with `git diff` and framed prior findings as "hypotheses, not conclusions" — which is precisely what let round 4 catch the fresh Critical the artifact's own revision had introduced (`019df92c`).
- **Credit exactly what held.** Round-over-round crediting ("confirmed resolved with specific new line citations"; "Stricter than my `[]` suggestion and correct" — `ee93cd5f`) is what makes a climb informative rather than a ratchet.
- **And once, the user had to ask for it.** "Ready for your fresh adversarial review… independent of the focus of the previous rounds" (`019dccb8`) — the anti-anchoring move arrived by request because no text supplies it.

The loop's failure mode is visible too, at low grade: a three-round session whose structurally different targets all landed exactly `Minor revision` (`019e3979` — the reader flagged possible verdict-anchoring), and a plateau session that never issued its terminal token, ending in unstructured prose ("this is now a credible drafting authority") after four straight `Minor revision` rounds (`019d72fe`). Re-arguing your own prior review is not re-searching, and nothing in the text marks the difference.

This is the arc's Era-73 lesson wearing a new face — discipline installed in the environment rather than the text — but with a harder edge: design-exploration's missing discipline lived in its counterparty; here it lives in the method's *own dominant use pattern*, invented convergently by its executors, and one paragraph writes it down. On re-scrutiny of a revised target: re-read the live artifact fresh; verify claimed fixes against the artifact and diff, never the description; treat prior findings as hypotheses to re-earn; hunt new defects, not only compliance with your own required changes; credit what held. Every sentence of that paragraph is already in the transcripts.

## 6. The panel turn, and the instrument that never fires

Since 06-28 the method's newest life is neither the solo review nor the loop: **twelve of the last fourteen recorded fires are caller-scoped panelists** — "You are adversarial reviewer 2 of 3… attack the plan from the persistence, migration, and data-integrity lens only" — spawned by orchestrators in `markdown-reader` and `career`, each self-selecting `review-family:scrutinize` for the posture, reading the cached body (three self-corrected a stale 0.5.0 cache path to find 0.6.0 — a version-drift hazard the readers flagged), and then delivering under the *caller's* format. In these fires the contract's scaffolding is overridden nearly totally: no default section list, no `Premise Check`, findings as flat severity-tagged lists, `Required Changes` folded inline, one caller even pre-imposing a truncated verdict enum that drops `Partial Review Only` (`019f23e1-83e2`). What survives caller override, every time, is the living core: the stance, read-before-judge with live probes, the verdict token, the read-only floor. The contract is functioning as a **posture and an evidence discipline**, not as a template — and it has nothing to say to its most common new executor.

Now set that against the contract's own scoping instrument. Bounded review mode (SKILL.md:72) — CANON-pinned byte-identical across three skills by `check-review-family.sh` — has fired **zero times in the method's entire recorded life**. Not once in 56 read sessions, all eras. Its function, meanwhile, gets improvised at the seams: two panelists invented "Execution-readiness verdict *for this lens only*" (`019f23e1`, `2c8bf4a2`); a lens-restricted re-review wrote its own prose scope note ("Scope: proof-boundary blockers only, per your request. I did not re-audit…" — `019f0f60`); others rendered unscoped verdicts on single-lens passes; and the one target that genuinely exceeded a single pass — a 1000+-line plan sampled in disjoint line windows — rendered a full-confidence `Patch Before Implementation` with no `Partial Review Only` flag and no omitted-range caveat (`019ed6f6`, the corpus's one bounded-mode near-miss). The diagnosis is precise: the bounded core's trigger is written for one case ("when the target is too large to inspect completely in one pass") and the fires' actual partial-coverage cases are different ones — a caller narrowed the lens, an orchestrator assigned one seat of three, a reviewer sampled windows. The instrument is dead not because the discipline is unwanted — the improvisations prove the want — but because its trigger names the wrong doorway. One rider sentence beside the CANON core (leaving the canonical sentence byte-identical) extends the same discipline to externally-narrowed scope: a review whose scope was narrowed by the caller, an assigned lens, or sampling is a bounded review — state the subset, scope the verdict to it, and leave full-clearance tokens unissued.

The panel turn also completes the Evidence Sweep story from §1: the library's need for orchestrated adversarial evidence did not die with the clause — it moved outside the contract, into orchestrator prompts that re-invent reviewer independence, lens assignment, and adjudication ad hoc, invisibly to the skill. The contract should not try to own the rig (Era 69 made that call for the sibling and it was right); but it should know it can be a panelist, because that is what it now mostly is.

## 7. Modes the reviewer chooses, silently

The 06-08 consolidation left mode selection to the reviewer's judgment (steps 3–4), and the fires show that judgment exercised confidently, mostly well, and almost always silently. Self-escalation into the execution-readiness register is routine: bare "Review `<path>`" asks — no readiness language anywhere — were classified as execution-readiness reviews and answered with the readiness token set in at least eight fires (`019ede08`, `019edc84`, `019f1172`, `019ee32a`, `019ece70`, plus panels), the readers repeatedly flagging "the skill's own routing logic, not user wording, decided the verdict register." Mostly this is sanctioned and sensible — the targets were build-from plans, squarely inside the trigger text. The mode's authority-language question is real but mild here: the observed self-chosen verdicts were overwhelmingly `Patch Before Implementation`, the safe direction; the two self-mode `Ready to Execute` clearances were caller-requested re-reviews of patched plans.

The Formal Stress Test trigger tells a sharper story. In ~35 current-era fires it self-fired three times — the two charter reviews and one implementation-baseline review — twice with visible, spec-grounded reasoning ("Because the charter is decision-critical and runtime-mutating… I am adding the formal stress-test sections") and once incompletely (`019ea8f4` ran the Assumptions Audit, Pre-Mortem, and Dimensional Critique but silently dropped the mandated Confidence Boundary). The user has never once requested it. And the trigger's own criteria — "high-stakes, irreversible, publication-bound, security/trust-sensitive, **runtime-mutating**" (SKILL.md:28) — never fired on the fires that literally matched them: a plan whose manual smoke mutates the user's real `ReaderState.json` with no restore path (`019f2627-eb31`), a schema/data-migration plan (`019edc84` — the reader: the trigger "simply never came up"). The mode fires on target *gravitas* (governance documents) and stays silent on target *mechanics* (mutation, migration). Against that, the healthiest observed behavior is a reviewer weighing the escalation aloud and declining it ("I'm doing a concise review rather than a formal stress-test packet unless the findings demand that heavier format" — `019ee32a`).

Both observations converge on one cheap repair, judgment-shaped: when the reviewer — not the user — chooses the register (readiness vocabulary, stress-test escalation, or its decline on a trigger-matching target), say so in a clause. The tags stay; the discretion stays; the choice stops being invisible. No new trigger rules — the two-bars lens is clear that sharpening the reviewer's judgment here beats adding a classifier, and the observed misses are visibility misses, not judgment misses.

## 8. Vocabulary that cannot be reached

The normal scrutiny verdict enum — `Reject` / `Major revision` / `Minor revision` / `Defensible` — is defined in exactly one place: the conditionally-loaded reference (review-format.md:55). SKILL.md names `Defensible` in the bounded-mode fence (:72) without ever defining the set. This is a known defect with a paper trail: three blind reviewers independently kept "verdict-vocab unreachable" as a trust defect on 06-16 (`judgment-trust-flip-set-results.md:76`); the 06-14 single-homing commit saw it and deliberately left the enum in the reference ("the Full Template and its sole-home normal-scrutiny verdict enum… untouched" — CHANGELOG 0.3.9); and the 06-17 apparatus review then stated as library doctrine that "each review skill's verdict vocabulary stays inline" (`skill-library-apparatus-review-2026-06-17.md:132`) — a doctrine this skill's layout has violated since, unnoticed because nobody re-read the layout against the doctrine.

The fires price the defect. Register drift is endemic exactly where the reference wasn't loaded: free-composed normal verdicts ("Sound core, not fully defensible as written" — `69dba33b`; "Defensible, not yet optimal" — `a7bc7b74`), readiness vocabulary leaking into ordinary-scrutiny closes ("Patch before execution" under a plain `Verdict` header — `019eafda`), a self-invented hybrid ("Minor revision. Execution-readiness equivalent: patch before implementation" — `019ed37a`), ad-hoc severity scales (`[P1]/[P2]/[P3]` twice, against the reference's Critical/High/Medium/Low), an invented section name ("Evidence Boundary" — `019f0f60`), and the mandated `Execution Readiness Verdict` header replaced by generic `Verdict` while emitting a readiness-only token (`019ece70`). Honesty requires the other half: several free compositions were *better* than the bare token — "Defensible, not yet optimal" is a more truthful clearance than `Defensible` — and no observed drift changed a decision. So the repair is homing, not enforcement: define the four-token enum in the always-loaded body (one line; the reference keeps its template with a back-pointer), and bless what the fires already do — a canonical token plus a scoping gloss. The severity scale can ride the same line. This closes a three-week-old kept finding at the cost of a sentence, and brings the layout under the library's own stated doctrine.

## 9. What survives, and the honest reframe

Nearly everything survives, and this hold has the arc's best numbers behind it. The stance survives: reject-until-earned demonstrably buys evidence discipline without the padding cost the commission predicted — the fires show restraint *enforced by* the stance ("I am not padding this section to look thorough" is reject-until-earned applied to the reviewer's own findings). The evidence architecture survives whole: read-before-judge, citation calibration (the marker never spoken in 56 sessions because reviewers simply read what they cited — a rule that never needs its own penalty clause is a rule that is working), proof-class disclosure in the openers, live probes as severity backing, distrust of delegated evidence.
