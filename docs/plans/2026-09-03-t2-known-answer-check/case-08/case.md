# Decision case: checkout abandonment before the holidays

## Decision question

How should Fernhill Goods, an online retailer, reduce checkout abandonment, which rose from 52 percent to 71 percent of started checkouts over the summer, before the holiday season starts in seven weeks?

## Background, as the user stated it

An online store doing about 9,000 orders a month, with a three-person in-house tech team and a development agency on a retainer of ten hours a week. Payments run through a provider called Paylane. About two thirds of traffic is on mobile. Abandonment (a shopper who starts checkout and does not complete it) was steady near half for two years and rose through June, July, and August to about seven in ten.

## User's candidates (marked as theirs, quoted exactly)

- **Add guest checkout** — "Let shoppers buy without creating an account." (user's)

## Hard constraints the user confirmed, with what each costs

1. No change to the payment provider's contract and no new provider before the holidays: the contract runs to March, switching needs ninety days' notice and a compliance re-certification. What it costs: replacing the provider is out.
2. Fraud screening stays: every order passes through the current fraud rules. What it costs: options that bypass fraud checks are out.
3. Development capacity is the agency's ten hours a week for seven weeks, about seventy hours. What it costs: rebuilding the checkout is out.

## Values the user stated

- Conversion matters more than average order value this season.
- Mobile shoppers first.
- Do not add steps for returning customers.

## Survivor count

About four.

# Field

Grouped by what each option changes: the account step, the forms, costs and incentives, the payment step, and follow-up. Order within a group is not a quality order.

## The account step

**Add guest checkout** (user's) — "Let shoppers buy without creating an account."
Sets it apart: removes the account wall entirely; the classic abandonment fix.

**Simplify the account creation form** — Cut account creation to email and password, with everything else optional.
Sets it apart: keeps accounts but makes them cheap; a form change.

## The forms

**Remove the mandatory phone-number field** — Make the phone number optional at the address step.
Sets it apart: one field; costs almost nothing to try.

**Reduce the address form to the minimum** — Drop every address field the carrier does not require and use address autocomplete.
Sets it apart: shortens the longest form in the checkout; needs an autocomplete service.

**Add a progress indicator** — Show shoppers which step they are on and how many remain.
Sets it apart: reassurance rather than removal; a display change.

**A/B test the checkout layout** — Run layout variants against each other for the season and keep the winner.
Sets it apart: lets data pick the layout; takes weeks to read.

## Costs and incentives

**Show shipping costs earlier** — Display shipping on the product and cart pages rather than at the shipping step.
Sets it apart: removes a surprise; a display change.

**Raise the free-shipping threshold visibility** — Promote the free-shipping threshold in the cart with a progress bar.
Sets it apart: nudges order size and reassures on cost; a cart-page change.

**Add an exit-intent discount** — Offer a small discount when a shopper moves to leave the checkout.
Sets it apart: pays shoppers to stay; costs margin on every triggered order.

**Add trust badges at the payment step** — Show security and guarantee badges beside the card form.
Sets it apart: addresses hesitation at the moment of payment; a display change.

## The payment step

**Add express wallet payments** — Offer wallet buttons so shoppers can pay with a saved wallet instead of typing a card.
Sets it apart: skips the card form for shoppers who have a wallet; a provider feature.

**Add more payment methods** — Offer instalments and bank transfer alongside cards.
Sets it apart: gives shoppers a way to pay that suits them; each method is an integration.

**Switch the payment step to the provider's hosted checkout page** — Send shoppers to Paylane's own hosted payment page for the card step instead of the card form embedded in the store's checkout.
Sets it apart: hands the payment step to the provider's page; adds a redirect the store cannot style.

**Replace the payment provider** — Move to a different payment provider with a better mobile checkout.
Sets it apart: the largest change in the field; bets the provider is the problem.

## Follow-up

**Retarget abandoned carts by email** — Email shoppers who abandoned with a link back to their cart.
Sets it apart: recovers after the fact rather than preventing; a marketing change.
