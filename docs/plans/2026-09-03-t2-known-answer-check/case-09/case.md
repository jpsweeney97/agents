# Decision case: the pantry's volunteer schedule

## Decision question

How should the Harbor Food Pantry fix its volunteer scheduling, which now leaves shifts unstaffed or double-staffed several times a week, before the holiday distribution surge in six weeks?

## Background, as the user stated it

A food pantry with about 140 active volunteers, about thirty shifts a week, and one part-time paid volunteer coordinator on twenty hours a week. Shifts are scheduled in a shared spreadsheet, with claims and changes arriving by email. Last month nine shifts ran unstaffed and fourteen ran with more people than needed. Volunteers have started complaining, and some have stopped signing up.

## User's candidates (marked as theirs, quoted exactly)

- **Move scheduling to the volunteers' group chat** — "Post the week's shifts in the group chat and let people claim them there." (user's)

## Hard constraints the user confirmed, with what each costs

1. No new software spending this year; the board froze new subscriptions after a budget shortfall. What it costs: buying a scheduling product is out.
2. No new paid staff or contractors before the year-end audit. What it costs: a second coordinator or a hired scheduler is out.
3. Volunteer personal data (names, phone numbers, availability) may only be held in systems the pantry already has a data-protection agreement for. What it costs: free apps and new services that hold volunteer data are out.

## Values the user stated

- Shifts staffed reliably matters more than the coordinator's convenience.
- Volunteers' experience: no one should turn up to find the shift full.
- The coordinator's twenty hours are protected; scheduling should take far fewer of them.

## Survivor count

About four.

# Field

Grouped by what each option changes: the tool, the shift structure, the people, and the process. Order within a group is not a quality order.

## The tool

**Move scheduling to the volunteers' group chat** (user's) — "Post the week's shifts in the group chat and let people claim them there."
Sets it apart: meets volunteers where they already talk; no setup at all.

**Rebuild the spreadsheet with validation and locking** — Add formulas, drop-downs, capacity counts, and protected ranges to the existing spreadsheet.
Sets it apart: keeps the tool everyone knows; makes it stricter.

**Use a shared calendar with sign-up slots** — Publish shifts as appointment slots in the pantry's calendar and let volunteers book them.
Sets it apart: a tool the pantry already has for other purposes; a calendar rather than a grid.

**Use a free scheduling app** — Adopt one of the free volunteer-scheduling apps and invite volunteers to it.
Sets it apart: built for exactly this job; a new place volunteer data would live.

**Use the scheduling module of the pantry's donor-management system** — Set up volunteer shifts inside the donor-management system the pantry already uses for its donor records, and have volunteers sign up there.
Sets it apart: no new tool, if the module is available on the pantry's plan; a setup and training job.

**Have a volunteer build a custom tool** — Accept a volunteer developer's offer to build a small scheduling site for the pantry.
Sets it apart: exactly what the pantry needs, built for free; maintained by whoever is around.

## The shift structure

**Set fixed weekly shifts per volunteer** — Assign each volunteer a standing slot rather than open sign-up.
Sets it apart: removes weekly scheduling almost entirely; asks volunteers for a standing commitment.

**Reduce the number of shift types** — Collapse the current shift variety into two or three standard shifts.
Sets it apart: fewer things to schedule; less flexibility for the work.

**Require sign-up a month ahead** — Close each month's schedule four weeks before it starts.
Sets it apart: gives the coordinator time to fill gaps; volunteers must plan far ahead.

## The people

**Recruit shift leads to manage their own shifts** — Give a volunteer lead per shift type responsibility for filling and running it.
Sets it apart: spreads the coordinator's load across volunteers; depends on the leads having a way to see who has signed up.

**Over-recruit volunteers to absorb no-shows** — Bring in more volunteers so that gaps are covered by numbers.
Sets it apart: treats unstaffed shifts as a supply problem.

**Train the coordinator on advanced spreadsheet use** — Send the coordinator on a short course so the current tool is used to its full ability.
Sets it apart: invests in the person rather than the tool.

## The process

**Send confirmation reminders two days before each shift** — Email or text every volunteer a reminder of their shift.
Sets it apart: attacks no-shows; changes nothing about how shifts are claimed.

**Put a paper sign-up board at the pantry** — Post the schedule on a board in the pantry and let volunteers sign up in person.
Sets it apart: no technology at all; sign-ups happen only on site.

**Weekly scheduling call with the coordinator** — Hold a short weekly call where volunteers claim shifts live.
Sets it apart: one synchronous moment settles the week; misses anyone who cannot attend.
