# Decision case: the press that keeps stopping

## Decision question

How should Halden Print reduce unplanned downtime on its main press, which lost fourteen production days last quarter, before peak season begins in ten weeks?

## Background, as the user stated it

A family print shop with nine staff, one main sheet-fed offset press bought in 2015, and two smaller machines. Peak season runs October to December and is about half the year's revenue. Last quarter the main press lost fourteen production days to unplanned stops, against three the quarter before. Two operators are trained on it. Jobs that miss the press are either delayed or turned away.

## User's candidates (marked as theirs, quoted exactly)

- **Buy a second press as a backup** — "Purchase a used press of the same class so work can move to it when the main press is down." (user's)

## Hard constraints the user confirmed, with what each costs

1. Capital spending before peak is capped at $20,000. What it costs: a press, even used, or a major refurbishment is out.
2. No planned stoppage of the main press longer than two consecutive working days before peak. What it costs: teardown-scale work is out.
3. Under the shop's insurance only the two trained operators, or the manufacturer's certified technician, may work on the press. What it costs: any option needing other hands on the press has to go through the manufacturer.

## Values the user stated

- Reliability through peak season matters more than throughput.
- Keep the two operators' confidence in the machine.
- Avoid depending on a single vendor's service contract.

## Survivor count

About four.

# Field

Grouped by what each option changes: capacity, how the press is maintained, the press itself, the shop around it, and how work is scheduled. Order within a group is not a quality order.

## Add capacity

**Buy a second press as a backup** (user's) — "Purchase a used press of the same class so work can move to it when the main press is down."
Sets it apart: the only option that keeps printing during a stop; the largest purchase in the field.

**Outsource overflow to a partner shop** — Arrange with another shop to take jobs when the press is down.
Sets it apart: capacity without capital; depends on the partner having room when Halden does not.

## Change how the press is maintained

**Take the manufacturer's annual service contract** — Buy the manufacturer's service plan with scheduled visits and priority call-out.
Sets it apart: puts the expert on a schedule; a recurring cost and a single-vendor dependence.

**Introduce a preventive-maintenance schedule** — Write and follow a weekly and monthly checklist for the press, done by the operators.
Sets it apart: costs time rather than money; catches wear before it stops the press.

**Train the operators in fault repair** — Send both operators on the manufacturer's repair course so common faults are fixed in-house.
Sets it apart: shortens every stop by removing the wait for a technician; changes nothing about how often stops happen.

**Stock spare parts for the common failures** — Keep the parts that have failed most often on the shelf.
Sets it apart: cheap and immediate; turns a multi-day wait into an hours-long swap.

**Hire a maintenance technician** — Employ a technician to look after the press and the two smaller machines.
Sets it apart: dedicated attention every day; a salary rather than a capital cost.

## Change the press

**Replace the press's controller** — Fit a current-generation control unit in place of the 2015 one.
Sets it apart: modernises the part most likely to be obsolete; a manufacturer job.

**Install condition sensors on the press** — Fit vibration and temperature sensors that warn before a component fails.
Sets it apart: turns unplanned stops into planned ones; warns but does not prevent.

**Run the press at reduced speed** — Cap the press's speed below its rating for the season.
Sets it apart: costs throughput, nothing else; bets the faults are stress-related.

**Full refurbishment by the manufacturer** — Send the press through the manufacturer's rebuild programme.
Sets it apart: resets the machine; the most thorough option and the longest stop.

## Change the shop around the press

**Service the shop's compressed-air system** — Have the air-supply contractor overhaul the compressor, dryer, and lines that feed the press and the two smaller machines.
Sets it apart: routine work on the building's services; nothing on the press is touched.

**Upgrade the shop's electrical supply** — Replace the shop's aging distribution board and add surge protection.
Sets it apart: protects every machine at once; bets the faults are electrical.

**Add climate control to the pressroom** — Install air conditioning and humidity control in the room the press runs in.
Sets it apart: stabilises the press's environment year round; the largest building change in the field.

## Change the work

**Change paper stock supplier** — Move to a supplier whose stock feeds more reliably.
Sets it apart: no work on the press at all; bets the stops are feed problems.
