# 04-close — recommendation on Brightline's doubled support queue

Recommend stage of the `deliberate` run. Written against `making-recommendations`, including `references/high-stakes.md`, which this decision qualifies for on blast radius.

## Registrations, before the comparison

**My first lean, on first read of the shaped surface:** correct the export date format. The shared findings put 482 of August's 830 tickets on one documented defect with a two-day fix on file, and none of the four options touches it. That lean did not move during the structured pass. Crediting the case rather than the method: this was clear from the first reading of the evidence, and the comparison below confirmed it rather than produced it.

**The user's visible lean:** none stated. The only visible signal is that "Add a chatbot to the help center" is the user's own candidate in the user's own wording; the other three were generated by the run. That is a weak signal, and my call goes against it. It is delivered plainly below rather than softened.

## One option added to the field, and why

`making-recommendations` permits adding a distinct alternative when it could realistically win or change the recommendation, and forbids adding one to manufacture a contest. This addition does the former and is the opposite of the latter: it collapses the contest rather than widening it.

**Added: correct the export date format (bug tracker issue #2291).** Pin the CSV formatter back to the documented ISO 8601 form, ship the per-account toggle for customers who have adapted, and notify them (E2, E3).

The Shape stage recorded that no option in the frozen field acts on the cause of 482 of the 830 tickets, and that adding a candidate was not that stage's authority. It is this stage's authority, and the addition is stated openly rather than smuggled in as a caveat.

The candidate is available under all three confirmed constraints:

- **Constraint 1, budget freeze, no hires or outsourced staff.** The fix uses existing engineering and buys nothing. It passes.
- **Constraint 2, ninety days' notice before a change to the format of data customers receive.** E3, the company's own legal note from July, states that restoring the documented format is a correction to the promised format rather than a change to it, and needs no notice. The same note records that the company is currently out of compliance with its own terms because the May change shipped without notice, so continuing the broken format is the ongoing violation, not the repair. It passes, and this is the single premise the whole call rests on. The check is named under Commitment Point.
- **Constraint 3, one engineer for one week.** E2 estimates two days including tests, and the estimate already includes the per-account toggle. It passes with about three days of slack.

## Structure of the decision

Taken in the order the method requires: filters first, then dominance, then whatever genuinely trades.

**Filters.** The three constraints were confirmed at their price in the setup, so they are tested and applied rather than re-litigated. Constraint 2 acts on nothing in this field, and E3 clears it for the added option. Constraint 1 and constraint 3 remove one option, the chatbot, on both of its routes: a vendor subscription is new spend under a freeze whose stated cost puts support vendors out, and a self-built assistant is a new subsystem, which constraint 3's stated cost puts out. A cut record is appended.

**Dominance.** Two options compete for the same single engineer-week: the known-issues banner and the added fix. Both act on the same 482-ticket slice, both fit inside the week on any reasonable reading, and the fix is better on ending the weekly breakage, better on the user's top-stated value, and better on keeping the product simple, since it adds no permanent interface surface. The comparison between them is over. A cut record is appended.

**What genuinely trades.** Expanded documentation and canned macros are neither filtered nor dominated. They spend support-team hours rather than engineer time, so they do not compete with the fix for the same resource. They are not recommended now, for the reason given under Why, and they are not cut: no constraint removes them and nothing dominates them. Nothing in the field is silently dropped.

**A resource distinction that carries much of the structure.** Constraint 3's engineer-week and the two-person support team's hours are separate resources. The fix and the banner draw the first. Documentation and macros draw the second, which is the resource the stated sustainability value protects and the one already at a nineteen-hour first response.

## Decision

How Brightline should spend its available capacity this quarter to bring the support queue back to a sustainable level before its two-person team burns out.

## The Call

**Clear call.** Spend the engineer-week on correcting the export date format, bug tracker issue #2291. Do not start any of the four options first.

This is a clear call rather than a conditional one because the trades point the same way under any reasonable weighing of the user's three stated values, and because the constraint that could have blocked it is cleared by the company's own legal note. It is not a check-first call: the two unknowns worth checking are cheap and belong before the release rather than before the decision, and they are named under Commitment Point.

## Why

**The queue did not grow; one defect did.** Tickets rose by 420 between April and August, and the export-dates category alone accounts for 482 of them. Every other category held flat or fell, and non-export volume declined in absolute terms while accounts grew from 780 to 900. Customer growth runs the wrong way to explain any of this.

**The cause is documented, priced, and inside the constraints.** Release 5.12 upgraded a CSV library and unintentionally changed the export date column away from the documented format. The fix is on file at two days including tests and the per-account toggle, which fits the one available engineer-week with slack.

**Nothing else in the field acts on it, and the four options together reach a small fraction of what the fix reaches.** The best case for the chatbot is roughly 24 to 35 tickets a month, for documentation roughly 18, and the macros option's largest opportunity is already captured. The banner reaches the right slice but only notifies.

**The work the defect creates is close to a full person-month.** About 482 originating tickets plus roughly 2.3 follow-ups each is near 1,590 touches a month, and at the six-minute median that is roughly 160 hours out of a two-person team. This assumes follow-ups cost about what an initial macro reply costs, which the evidence does not state. Even at half that figure it is the largest single claim on the team's time.

**The fix is the only move that serves the user's own stated priority.** The user said customers getting what they need matters more than deflecting tickets. The customers' need is a working export. Every other option leaves them re-applying a workaround every week, because the scheduled export re-runs weekly.

**After the fix, the residue is smaller than the level the same two people already handled.** Roughly 348 tickets a month remain, against 410 in April at a four-hour first response. On the evidence supplied this is a defect problem, not a capacity problem, which is why no support-hour spend is recommended alongside it.

**Continuing without the fix keeps a compliance problem open.** E3 records that the company is currently out of compliance with its own terms because the May change shipped without the promised notice. The correction closes that; none of the four options does.

## The Case Against

**The runner-up, and its strongest honest case.** The known-issues banner is the only option in the frozen field that acts on the 482-ticket slice at all. It can go live before any code fix ships, it tells the truth to the 74% of export-dates tickets coming from the two highest-paying plans before those customers spend time diagnosing a problem Brightline caused, and unlike a one-time fix it is a durable capability for the next known issue. If the July legal reading turns out to be wrong, it becomes the leading option the same day, with no alternative behind it.

**The smallest realistic change that would make it win:** counsel reverses E3 and rules that restoring the documented format is itself a format change needing ninety days' notice. Then the fix cannot ship this quarter and the banner leads.

Extending the same discipline to the other three, as a decision at this blast radius requires:

- **Chatbot** wins if the freeze owner confirms a subscription sits outside the freeze, someone other than the one available engineer ships the export fix, and a sample of twenty recent how-to tickets shows most are answered by documentation customers did not find. Its honest ceiling stays at E5's 20 to 30% of 118.
- **Expand documentation** wins if that same twenty-ticket sample lands the other way, showing the how-to tickets are genuinely uncovered topics, and the support team has hours to write that the nineteen-hour first response says it does not.
- **Canned macros** wins as a companion rather than a rival if the support lead's macro inventory shows a large uncovered set in billing and other replies. It costs nothing and no engineer time, so it never had to lose to the fix; it simply is not the answer to this surge.

## What Would Flip It

- **E3 is superseded or wrong.** This is the one premise the call rests on. If restoring the documented format needs ninety days' notice, the fix is out this quarter and the banner leads.
- **The two-day estimate is stale, or the engineer-week is already committed.** If #2291 no longer fits the week, the allocation question reopens.
- **An earlier stage of this run considered the export fix and rejected it for a reason not visible on the shaped surface.** The Shape stage recorded only that adding a candidate was outside its authority, not that the fix had been ruled out. The Contest stage can see the cuts this stage cannot, and this is the first thing it should check.
- **More customers have adapted to the broken format than the three on record.** That does not flip the fix, but it changes how the release is staged; see Rollback below.
- **The twenty-ticket how-to sample.** It does not affect the fix. It settles which of the two support-hour options is worth anything afterward, and it is cheap enough to run while the fix is in flight.

## Commitment Point

The commitment point is the release that ships the format restoration, not the decision to schedule it. Up to that release everything is reversible at no customer cost.

Two cheap confirmations belong before that release, and neither is a reason to delay the decision:

1. **Confirm E3 with whoever owns it.** One question: is the July reading still the company's position, that restoring the documented format is a correction and needs no notice? This is the premise the call rests on and it costs a message.
2. **Identify who has adapted to the broken format.** Three customers are on record. Announce the correction to scheduled-export users a few days ahead and offer the toggle. This is prudence, not a compliance requirement, and E3 is explicit that no notice period is owed.

What remains reversible after the release: the format itself, by reverting the release or by flipping the per-account toggle for any customer who asks.

## Rollback and Blast Radius

**Blast radius.** Scheduled exports are used by 610 of 900 accounts, so the release changes data that most of the customer base receives. Three customers are known to have adapted their systems to the accidental format and will break when it is corrected; the number who adapted silently is unknown and is the real exposure. Against that, 482 accounts' worth of tickets a month say the current output is already breaking downstream systems.

**Rollback options that actually exist.** Revert the release, which is a normal deployment operation. Or leave the release in place and flip the per-account toggle for affected customers, which E2 already scopes into the two-day estimate and which is the better instrument because it does not re-break the majority.

**Owners.** The one available engineer for the fix and toggle. The support lead for the customer notification and for tagging any post-release breakage. Whoever owns the legal position for the confirmation above.

**Against the simplicity value.** The per-account toggle adds a small permanent setting. That is a real cost against "keep the product simple," and it is smaller than the permanent interface surface a known-issues banner would add.

---

This close does not claim the option space is complete or the ranking verified. It claims one thing that is cheap to contest: the evidence supplied names a single cause for 482 of 830 tickets, prices its correction at two days inside the one available engineer-week, and clears the only constraint that could have blocked it. If any of those three is wrong, the call changes.
