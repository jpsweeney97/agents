# Contest agent's post-hoc reply (case-06)

Post hoc, not part of the stage output. After `05-contest.md` was written and copied here, the orchestrator sent the Contest agent one prompted question: whether Prune's cut of "Restore the previous export date format" was a live recorded challenge under the method as briefed, given that its `Revive if` (1) is satisfied by the close's E3 reasoning and the close added an option of the same substance; and whether the close having adopted the substance was part of the reasoning. The reply below is verbatim. It is weaker evidence than the line itself: it was written knowing which cut the question was about.

---

The cut met the briefed detection criteria literally, and I still did not name it. That was a deliberate call, not an oversight.

- **What it satisfies.** Prune's revive condition (1) is met by the close's own reasoning. The close's basis is E3, which says the May change shipped without notice and that restoring the documented form is a correction rather than a change. The Shape stage records the same fact at line 20, so the cut's reason is also undermined by the comparison surface. Two of the three grounds in my brief are met.
- **Why I left it out.** The check I write is an exclusion check, and a challenge is something still excluded from the close. The close added "correct the export date format (bug tracker issue #2291)", which is the same change as the cut option, and made it the recommendation. Naming it would have told a reader that an option was wrongly kept out, when it is the option that won. There is also nothing to contest: the only argument available would be to argue for the recommendation.
- **Yes, the close having adopted it was the deciding factor.** Had the close leaned on constraint 2 as a reason to leave the export fix out while resting elsewhere on E3, I would have named that cut, and it would have been the one most worth contesting.
- **What I named instead.** The documentation-search gate was cut only for duplicating the chatbot, with the tie going to the chatbot because it was the user's own wording. The close then cut the chatbot on constraint 1, which is the exact first half of the gate's revive condition, and the gate needs no vendor and no spend. That exclusion is still standing and unaddressed anywhere in the close.
- **The honest tension, for the record.** A strict literal reading of the three grounds would have listed the export-format cut. I applied the exclusion frame in the line's own name and the stage's purpose over the literal reading. The close's "What Would Flip It" asked Contest to check whether an earlier stage had rejected the export fix for an unseen reason. The answer is that Prune did cut it, on constraint 2, while recording that the legal reading was beyond its authority, and E3 supplies exactly that reading. That is a confirmation of the close, and the one-line output form has no slot for a confirmation.
