# Evidence the user supplied for the no-show decision

Ten excerpts.

## E1. No-show trend and what changed

No-show rate: 11% January to March; 24% April to August. In April the clinic changed the sender of its automated text reminders from a local ten-digit number to the vendor's short code, saving $40 a month. Nothing else about the reminders changed.

## E2. Reminder delivery and a patient survey (July)

The vendor dashboard reports 97% of reminders delivered. The front desk phoned 60 patients who had missed an appointment in July: 41 said they never saw a reminder. Of those 41, 33 are on one of the county's two largest mobile carriers, whose phones by default divert short-code messages to a filtered folder. Among surveyed patients over 65, 70% never saw the reminder.

## E3. Two-week pilot of next-day calls (August)

On two days a week the front desk phoned every patient with a next-day appointment; the other three days ran as usual.

| | Called days | Uncalled days, same weeks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No-show rate | 9% | 25% |
| Patients reached or left a voicemail | 100% | n/a |
| Patients who rescheduled or cancelled on the call | 22% | n/a |

Slots freed on the call were refilled from the same-day phone list. Staff time: 3.4 hours per called day, across the three front-desk staff.

## E4. Messaging vendor options

Returning to a ten-digit sending number now requires carrier registration for application-to-person messaging; the current vendor and two others quote six to ten weeks pending approval. Email: 38% of patients have an email on file; among patients over 65, 19%.

## E5. Front-desk time study (two weeks)

Between 14:00 and 16:00 each day the desk is quiet; the three staff average two idle hours each in that window, about six person-hours a day. The pilot's calls were made in that window.

## E6. Patient portal

12% of patients have activated the portal; 4% of patients over 65. The portal already offers self-rescheduling and a same-day waitlist. The pilot's freed slots were refilled by phone, not by the waitlist.

## E7. Booking lead time

Since April the no-show rate is the same (23 to 25%) across every booking lead time from two days to eight weeks. Before April, appointments booked more than four weeks out had a higher rate (16%) than near-term ones (8%).

## E8. Medical director's memo (August)

"Releasing a slot because a patient did not reply to a message they may never have seen is unacceptable. Appointment length stays at the current standard; the spring safety incident arose from compressed scheduling."

## E9. Postal reminders

$0.73 per card; about 2,800 appointments a month; four to six days' delivery. About 30% of appointments change within six days of the visit.

## E10. Other figures

- Telehealth: 18% of visits are eligible; the no-show rate for telehealth visits since April is 22%.
- A 2025 education campaign (posters, leaflets) produced no measurable change.
- Repeat no-shows: 71% of no-shows since April are patients with no prior miss.
