# Decision case: the clinic's missed appointments

## Decision question

How should the Ridgeway Family Clinic reduce its appointment no-show rate, which has risen from eleven percent to twenty-four percent since the spring, before the flu-season rush begins in eight weeks?

## Background, as the user stated it

A primary-care clinic with four clinicians, about 140 appointments a day, and a front desk of three. Since April roughly one appointment in four is missed without notice. Each missed slot is a patient not seen, often a chronic-care follow-up, and lost revenue. The clinic sends automated text-message reminders through a vendor.

## User's candidates (marked as theirs, quoted exactly)

- **Call patients the day before their appointment** — "Have the front desk phone each patient with a next-day appointment to confirm." (user's)

## Hard constraints the user confirmed, with what each costs

1. No overbooking: the medical director ruled it out after a patient-safety incident. What it costs: double-booking to absorb no-shows is out.
2. No charges, deposits, or penalties for missed appointments; the clinic's payer contracts and charitable status forbid them. What it costs: financial deterrents are out.
3. No new staff or contractors before flu season; the front desk's existing hours are what there is. What it costs: options needing new labour are out.

## Values the user stated

- Patients seen, especially chronic-care follow-ups, over slot revenue.
- Do not make booking or attending harder for elderly patients.
- Keep the front desk's workload sustainable.

## Survivor count

About four.

# Field

Grouped by what each option changes: how patients are reminded, how the schedule works, and what patients are asked to do. Order within a group is not a quality order.

## Change how patients are reminded

**Call patients the day before their appointment** (user's) — "Have the front desk phone each patient with a next-day appointment to confirm."
Sets it apart: a human voice; the most labour-intensive reminder in the field.

**Switch the text-message reminders to a different vendor** — Move the automated reminders to another messaging provider.
Sets it apart: keeps reminders automatic; bets the current vendor is the weak link.

**Add email reminders** — Send an email reminder alongside the text message.
Sets it apart: a second channel at no labour cost; reaches only patients with an email on file.

**Add a second text-message reminder** — Send a reminder at three days out as well as the day before.
Sets it apart: doubles the chances a reminder is seen; a configuration change with the current vendor.

**Send reminders earlier** — Move the single reminder from the day before to three days before.
Sets it apart: gives patients time to rearrange; costs nothing.

**Mail appointment cards by post** — Send a printed reminder card for every appointment.
Sets it apart: reaches patients with no phone or email; slow and per-piece cost.

**Run a patient education campaign about missed appointments** — Posters, leaflets, and a note on the website about what a missed slot costs other patients.
Sets it apart: appeals to patients' consideration rather than their memory.

## Change how the schedule works

**Add a waitlist that fills cancelled slots automatically** — Let patients join a same-day waitlist through the patient portal so freed slots are refilled without staff effort.
Sets it apart: recovers the slot rather than preventing the miss.

**Offer an online self-rescheduling link** — Include a link in reminders so patients can move an appointment themselves.
Sets it apart: turns a would-be no-show into a reschedule; needs the patient to act online.

**Shorten the booking horizon to two weeks** — Stop booking appointments more than two weeks ahead.
Sets it apart: bets that far-ahead bookings are the ones forgotten; makes long-term planning harder.

**Reduce appointment length to add slots** — Shorten standard appointments so more slots exist and misses cost less.
Sets it apart: treats the loss rather than the miss; changes clinical time per patient.

**Extend clinic hours into the evening** — Open two evenings a week so patients who miss daytime slots have alternatives.
Sets it apart: adds convenience; needs staff on those evenings.

**Offer telehealth for follow-up visits** — Let eligible follow-ups happen by video.
Sets it apart: removes the trip; bets the trip is why patients miss.

## Change what patients are asked to do

**Require patients to confirm by reply or lose the slot** — Ask patients to reply to the reminder; release unconfirmed slots the morning of.
Sets it apart: converts silence into a freed slot; a patient who never saw the message loses their appointment.

**Send a policy letter after two missed appointments** — Write to patients who miss twice, explaining the clinic's expectations.
Sets it apart: addresses repeat offenders individually; a letter, not a penalty.
