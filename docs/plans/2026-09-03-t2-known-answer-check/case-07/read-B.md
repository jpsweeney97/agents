Best: **Call patients the day before their appointment**
Why: E1 and E2 locate the cause: the April switch to a short-code sender, whose messages the county's two largest carriers divert unseen, hitting patients over 65 hardest (70% never saw the reminder), so every option that only adds or retimes a text (second text, earlier reminder, self-rescheduling link) sends more messages into the same filtered folder. E3 is a direct two-week pilot of this option: 9% no-shows on called days against 25% on uncalled days in the same weeks, which restores the pre-April rate, reaches 100% of patients, and turned 22% of calls into reschedules or cancellations whose slots were refilled by phone. E5 shows the 3.4 staff-hours per called day fit inside the roughly six idle person-hours the desk already has between 14:00 and 16:00, so constraint 3 (no new staff) and the sustainable-workload value hold. E4 rules out the root-cause fix landing in time: a return to a ten-digit number needs carrier registration quoted at six to ten weeks by the current vendor and two others, against an eight-week deadline. E8 removes confirm-or-lose and shorter appointments, E7 removes the booking-horizon bet, and E10 removes telehealth (22% no-show), education (no effect in 2025), and the repeat-miss letter (71% of no-shows are first-time misses).
Runners-up, in order: **Add email reminders**, **Switch the text-message reminders to a different vendor**, **Mail appointment cards by post**
Unknowns that would change the choice: E5's idle window was measured before flu season, and if the rush consumes those afternoon hours the calls would no longer be free labour. E3 ran two days a week for two weeks, so whether the 9% holds at five days a week, and whether call fatigue sets in, is not shown. If any vendor could complete ten-digit registration inside the eight weeks, the vendor switch would fix the cause rather than work around it.
