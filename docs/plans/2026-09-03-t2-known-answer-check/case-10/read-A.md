Best: **Run the three reviews concurrently instead of in sequence**
Why: It is the only option that cuts the backlog while passing every hard constraint and every stated value: all three legally required reviews still happen by qualified reviewers, no people are added or contracted, no software is replaced, first-come-first-served order is unchanged, and no extra hours are loaded onto already stretched staff. With fourteen reviewers and a median that grew from nine to thirty-four days, elapsed time per application is dominated by waiting between sequential hand-offs, and running the three reviews at once collapses three queue waits into one. It is a routing change that can be in place well inside the ten-week window. The user's own candidate, overtime, adds real hours but directly conflicts with the morale value and risks tired-reviewer errors that the safety value ranks above speed. Hiring, outsourcing, plan-check services, and self-certification are all ruled out by the hard constraints, and the fast lane and batching break first-come-first-served.
Runners-up, in order: **Reassign reviewers from commercial permits**, **Change which applications get the supervisor sign-off**, **Standardize the review checklists**
Unknowns that would change the choice: If one review stage (structural, the scarcest expertise) is fully saturated on its own, concurrency shortens waits but cannot raise throughput, and reassignment from commercial or overtime becomes the better choice. Whether the commercial queue has slack and whether its reviewers are qualified for residential zoning, structural, and fire reviews is not stated. Whether the current permitting software can route the three reviews in parallel through configuration alone is not stated.
