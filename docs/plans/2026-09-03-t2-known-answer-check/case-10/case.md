# Decision case: the permit backlog

## Decision question

How should the city's building-permits office cut its backlog of pending residential permit applications, which has grown from about 300 to about 1,100 over the spring and summer, before the winter construction cutoff in ten weeks?

## Background, as the user stated it

A municipal permits office with fourteen plan reviewers. Median processing time for a residential permit has gone from nine days to thirty-four. State law sets a forty-five-day maximum, and the office is now breaching it on some applications. Contractors have complained to the council, and applicants are losing their construction season.

## User's candidates (marked as theirs, quoted exactly)

- **Authorize overtime for reviewers** — "Fund overtime for the review staff until the backlog clears." (user's)

## Hard constraints the user confirmed, with what each costs

1. No reduction in the legally required reviews: zoning, structural, and fire reviews must each be completed by a qualified reviewer for every application. What it costs: skipping a required review, or letting applicants self-certify one, is out.
2. No new staff or contractors before the next budget cycle; temporary staff and outside firms need a council vote that takes eight weeks. What it costs: adding people is out.
3. The permitting software cannot be replaced this year; the contract runs through next year. What it costs: a new system is out.

## Values the user stated

- Public safety and legal compliance over speed.
- Fairness to applicants: first come, first served.
- Reviewer morale; the staff are already stretched.

## Survivor count

About four.

# Field

Grouped by what each option changes: capacity, the workflow, intake, and communication. Order within a group is not a quality order.

## Capacity

**Authorize overtime for reviewers** (user's) — "Fund overtime for the review staff until the backlog clears."
Sets it apart: more hours from the people who know the work; a budget line rather than a process change.

**Hire temporary reviewers** — Bring in temporary qualified reviewers for the season.
Sets it apart: adds capacity without permanent headcount.

**Reassign reviewers from commercial permits** — Move staff from the commercial-permit team to residential for the season.
Sets it apart: capacity from inside the office; the commercial queue pays for it.

**Outsource structural review to an engineering firm** — Contract a firm to perform structural reviews.
Sets it apart: buys the scarcest expertise; a contract.

**Use a third-party plan-check service** — Route complete applications to an accredited plan-check service and have staff verify the results.
Sets it apart: outside throughput with inside sign-off.

## The workflow

**Create a fast lane for simple applications** — Route minor work (decks, fences, small additions) to an expedited path.
Sets it apart: clears the easy cases quickly; complex cases wait longer.

**Allow licensed contractors to self-certify** — Let licensed contractors certify compliance for defined work types in place of a staff review.
Sets it apart: removes a review for trusted applicants; the largest reduction in review work.

**Change which applications get the supervisor sign-off** — Route only some application types through the supervisor sign-off that follows the three reviews, rather than all of them.
Sets it apart: a routing rule; changes which applications a supervisor sees, not what reviewers do.

**Run the three reviews concurrently instead of in sequence** — Have zoning, structural, and fire review each application at the same time rather than one after another.
Sets it apart: the standard throughput fix for a sequential workflow; needs no new people.

**Batch similar applications for review** — Group applications of the same type so a reviewer works through them together.
Sets it apart: efficiency from repetition; changes the order applications are handled in.

**Train reviewers on the permitting software's shortcuts** — Run training on the software's batch and template features.
Sets it apart: cheap; helps only if reviewers are slowed by the tool.

## Intake

**Add pre-application checklists** — Publish detailed checklists so applications arrive complete.
Sets it apart: cuts rework from incomplete submissions; helps future applications, not the backlog.

**Improve the online submission portal** — Add validation to the portal so incomplete applications cannot be submitted.
Sets it apart: intake quality at the source; a software configuration project.

## Communication

**Standardize the review checklists** — Give every reviewer the same checklist per permit type so each review takes less judgment time.
Sets it apart: shortens each review a little; no change to who reviews or when.

**Ask the state for a deadline extension** — Request relief from the forty-five-day maximum for the season.
Sets it apart: removes the legal exposure rather than the backlog.
