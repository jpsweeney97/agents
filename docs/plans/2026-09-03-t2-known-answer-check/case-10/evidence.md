# Evidence the user supplied for the permit-backlog decision

Ten excerpts.

## E1. Queue by workflow step, today (about 1,100 pending residential applications)

| Step | Applications waiting | Median days at step (stage median) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake complete, awaiting assignment | 40 | 1 |
| Zoning review | 60 | 3 |
| Structural review | 90 | 5 |
| Fire review | 70 | 3 |
| Supervisor second sign-off | 780 | 21 |
| Issuance | 60 | 1 |

Zoning, structural, and fire stage medians are at their pre-March levels.

## E2. The March change

Director's memo, March 1: "Effective March 3, applications involving accessory dwelling units require a supervisor's second sign-off, per the new state rule." Workflow change ticket #311 (permitting software): "Add supervisor sign-off step after fire review." The rule as configured applies to all residential permit types; the ticket contains no accessory-dwelling-unit filter. Accessory dwelling units are 6% of residential applications.

## E3. Legal position

State law requires zoning, structural, and fire reviews for residential permits, each by a qualified reviewer. City attorney's note, August: "The supervisor second sign-off is a local discretionary step. For accessory dwelling units it implements the state rule; for other residential applications it is not required by any statute or ordinance and may be removed at the director's discretion."

## E4. Second sign-off outcomes, March to August

Non-accessory-dwelling applications signed off: about 1,400; returned for correction: 3, all labeling issues fixed the same day. Accessory dwelling unit applications signed off: about 90; returned: 11, all state-rule items.

## E5. Staffing and the July overtime pilot

Fourteen reviewers, two supervisors. Each supervisor spends about thirty hours a week on sign-offs. In the July overtime pilot, reviewer overtime cleared the review stages faster and the supervisor queue grew; supervisor overtime raised their sign-off throughput by 8%.

## E6. Software vendor

Changing the routing rule so the sign-off step applies only to accessory dwelling unit applications is a configuration change, one working day, within the existing contract.

## E7. Intake

Online portal since 2024; paper intake is 4% of applications; intake median one day; incomplete applications 12%, unchanged since 2025. The zoning, structural, and fire reviews have run concurrently, not in sequence, since the 2024 portal rollout. Review checklists per permit type were standardized in 2025.

## E8. Commercial permits

A separate team of six with its own sixty-day backlog.

## E9. Budget and law

Temporary staff, contractors, and outside services need a council vote (eight weeks). The forty-five-day maximum is state law with no local extension mechanism; the state has declined extension requests from three other cities this year.

## E10. Council note

Applications are processed first come, first served by policy; the council rejected a fast-lane proposal in 2025 on fairness grounds.
