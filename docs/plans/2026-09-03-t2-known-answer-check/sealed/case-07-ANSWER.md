## Winner

**Call patients the day before their appointment** (user's).

## Fixing facts (evidence only)

1. E1/E2: the rise coincides with the switch to a short code; 41 of 60 no-show patients never saw the reminder; the two largest carriers filter short codes; 70% of over-65s never saw it.
2. E3: the pilot of next-day calls cut no-shows to 9% on called days against 25% on uncalled days, reached 100%, and freed slots that were refilled.
3. E5: the calls take 3.4 staff-hours a day, inside the six idle person-hours the desk already has in the quiet window; no new staff (constraint 3), workload sustainable (value).
4. E4: the automated fixes are blocked for six to ten weeks (carrier registration) or reach few of the affected (email, 19% of over-65s).
Cost: no money; staff time from an idle window; eight weeks is enough because it is already running.

## Why every other candidate loses

- Vendor switch: E4, six to ten weeks; flu season in eight, and the short-code filtering is carrier-side.
- Email: E4, 38% overall, 19% of over-65s.
- Second text / earlier text: E2, the messages are filtered; more of them are filtered too.
- Postal cards: E9, $2k a month, and 30% of appointments change inside the delivery window.
- Education campaign: E10, no effect in 2025.
- Waitlist: E6, exists, 12% activation; recovers slots but does not stop misses.
- Self-rescheduling link: E6, the link rides in the filtered message and 4% of over-65s use the portal.
- Booking horizon: E7, no-shows now uniform across lead times.
- Shorter appointments: E8, the director forbids it.
- Evening hours: constraint 3.
- Telehealth: E10, 22% no-show, no better.
- Confirm-or-lose: E8, the director forbids it; E2, patients never saw the message.
- Policy letter: E10, 71% are first misses; and it edges toward a deterrent (constraint 2's spirit).
