# Decision case: the slow hiring process

## Decision question

How should Larkspur Software shorten its engineering hiring process, which now takes a median of 71 days from first contact to accepted offer and is losing candidates along the way, before the next planning cycle in three months?

## Background, as the user stated it

A forty-person software company hiring six engineers this half. The process has one part-time coordinator and six hiring managers. In the last quarter nine candidates reached the final stage and five were lost: they withdrew or took other offers. The interview loop has four stages and is the same for every candidate.

## User's candidates (marked as theirs, quoted exactly)

- **Engage a recruiting agency** — "Use an external agency to source and screen candidates so the team only sees finalists." (user's)

## Hard constraints the user confirmed, with what each costs

1. No increase to compensation bands or to the headcount budget this cycle. What it costs: paying more per offer is out, and anything funded from the headcount budget reduces the number of hires.
2. Each hiring manager's time on hiring is capped at four hours a week. What it costs: options that need more of the managers' hours are out.
3. The four-stage structured interview loop is a compliance requirement: every candidate goes through the same four stages. What it costs: shortening the process by removing a stage is out.

## Values the user stated

- Candidate quality over speed.
- The candidate's experience of the process; people talk.
- Fairness and consistency across candidates.

## Survivor count

About four.

# Field

Grouped by the part of the process each option changes: sourcing, the interview loop, deciding and offering, tools and staff. Order within a group is not a quality order.

## Sourcing

**Engage a recruiting agency** (user's) — "Use an external agency to source and screen candidates so the team only sees finalists."
Sets it apart: moves the top of the funnel outside the company; a fee per hire.

**Introduce a referral bonus** — Pay employees for referrals who are hired.
Sets it apart: the cheapest sourcing change; referred candidates tend to move through faster.

**Add a part-time sourcer** — Bring in a contractor to find and approach candidates directly.
Sets it apart: more candidates at the top; changes nothing after first contact.

**Rewrite the job posts** — Rework the postings for clarity and appeal.
Sets it apart: costs a few hours; helps only if the posts are what slows or deters candidates.

## The interview loop

**Batch the loop into a single onsite day** — Run all four stages back to back on one day per candidate instead of spread over weeks.
Sets it apart: collapses the loop's calendar time; asks a lot of everyone's day.

**Automate interview scheduling** — Use a scheduling tool so candidates pick slots from interviewers' live calendars.
Sets it apart: removes the back-and-forth from booking; a tool change.

**Shorten the take-home exercise** — Cut the take-home from its current length to about an hour.
Sets it apart: less to ask of candidates; a change to one stage's content.

**Train interviewers to submit feedback faster** — Set a same-day feedback rule and coach interviewers on writing it quickly.
Sets it apart: attacks any lag between an interview and its write-up.

**Add an early technical screen call** — Insert a short technical call before the loop to filter earlier.
Sets it apart: fewer candidates reach the expensive stages; adds a step for everyone.

## Deciding and offering

**Hold the hiring decision meeting within one working day of the final interview** — Put a standing decision slot on the calendar every day and require interviewers to attend or submit a written vote before it.
Sets it apart: a calendar rule; changes nothing about who is interviewed or how.

**Pre-approve offer packages before the final stage** — Have finance sign off the offer range for each candidate before the final interview rather than after.
Sets it apart: removes an approval from the end of the process.

**Send offers with a 48-hour expiry** — Give candidates two days to accept.
Sets it apart: forces a quick close; risks the candidate experience.

**Add a sign-on bonus** — Offer a one-time bonus on acceptance.
Sets it apart: makes the offer easier to say yes to; costs money per hire.

## Tools and staff

**Adopt an applicant-tracking system** — Move the pipeline into a dedicated system with stage tracking and reminders.
Sets it apart: visibility into where every candidate is; a setup project.

**Hire a full-time in-house recruiter** — Replace the part-time coordinator with a dedicated recruiter who owns the process end to end.
Sets it apart: a person whose job is to make the process move; a new role.
