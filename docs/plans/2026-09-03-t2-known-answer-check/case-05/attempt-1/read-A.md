Best: **Hold the hiring decision meeting within one working day of the final interview**
Why: The one hard number in the case is that five of nine finalists were lost after reaching the final stage, so the delay between the last interview and a decision is where candidates are actually leaving. A standing daily decision slot with a written-vote fallback attacks that delay directly, costs no money, adds no stage, and stays inside the four-hour manager cap because the vote can be written rather than attended. It changes nothing about who is interviewed or how, so candidate quality, fairness, and consistency are untouched, and a fast answer improves the candidate's experience. The sourcing options add candidates at the top without fixing where they are lost, the sign-on bonus and full-time recruiter run into the money and headcount constraint, the 48-hour expiry harms candidate experience, and the ATS and agency are setup or fee commitments that will not pay off within three months.
Runners-up, in order: **Pre-approve offer packages before the final stage**, **Automate interview scheduling**, **Train interviewers to submit feedback faster**
Unknowns that would change the choice: The case does not say how the 71 days split between stage-to-stage scheduling lag and post-final decision lag; if most of the time is between stages, automated scheduling becomes the better first lever. It also does not say whether the five lost finalists withdrew before or after receiving an offer; if they received prompt offers and still declined, the cause is the offer itself and this rule will not fix it. Whether a daily standing slot counts against the four-hour cap in practice is also unstated.
