Best: **Hold the hiring decision meeting within one working day of the final interview**
Why: E1 shows the wait from final interview to decision meeting is 34 of the 71 median days, roughly three times any other stage. E2 shows four of the five lost finalists withdrew during exactly that wait, citing silence and other offers, so this stage is also where candidate experience is being damaged. E3 shows the cause is calendar-booking, not feedback: the meeting is booked ad hoc and the first shared free hour is a median of 19 working days out, while feedback arrives within one day. A standing daily slot with a written-vote fallback costs no money (constraint 1), stays inside the four-hour manager cap since E9 shows managers already at 3.4 hours and the slot replaces an existing meeting (constraint 2), and leaves the four-stage loop untouched (constraint 3). The competing options miss the real delay: the agency route hit the same internal wait (E4), scheduling and tracking tools are already in place (E5), the take-home is off the critical path (E6), the funnel is healthy (E7), an onsite day breaks the manager cap (E9), and a 48-hour expiry would hurt candidates who accept when given a week (E8).
Runners-up, in order: **Pre-approve offer packages before the final stage**, **Hire a full-time in-house recruiter**, **Introduce a referral bonus**
Unknowns that would change the choice: Whether the hiring managers will accept a written vote in place of attending, since if all five people must still meet live the standing slot may not remove the 19-day scheduling problem. Whether the four-hour cap counts a daily standing slot even on days with no decision, which could push some managers over the cap. Whether a full-time recruiter would be funded from the headcount budget; if not, that option becomes a stronger second choice because it also owns the decision-meeting scheduling.
