Best: **Put the hiring decision meeting on a standing daily slot**
Why: E1 shows the final-interview-to-decision-meeting wait is 34 of the 71 median days, roughly half the whole process, and E3 shows the cause is ad hoc booking of an hour all five people share, a median of 19 working days out, not slow feedback (E3, E10). E2 shows four of the five lost finalists withdrew during exactly that wait, citing silence and assumed rejection, so this gap is both the delay and the candidate loss. A standing short slot with a written-vote alternative removes the five-way calendar search without touching the four-stage loop (constraint 3, E11), without new money (constraint 1), and within the manager cap since it replaces the existing hour-long meeting and E9 puts managers at 3.4 of their 4 hours. The rivals attack stages that are not the problem: the scheduling tool and applicant-tracking system already exist (E5), the take-home is not on the critical path (E6), the top of funnel is healthy with good finalist quality (E7), and the agency delivered finalists in 9 days yet the same internal wait followed while fees would cost about one headcount (E4). A single onsite day breaks the manager cap (E9), a sign-on bonus breaks constraint 1, a 48-hour expiry hurts candidate experience with no gain (E8), and an early screen counts against reported candidate-experience metrics (E11).
Runners-up, in order: **Pre-approve offer packages before the final stage**, **Hire a full-time in-house recruiter**, **Introduce a referral bonus**
Unknowns that would change the choice: Whether the compliance rule requires all four interviewers and the hiring manager to be present together for the decision, which E11 does not say; if written votes are not acceptable, the daily slot may not shrink the wait. Whether the daily slot's total weekly minutes stay under the 4-hour cap for the managers already near it (E9 gives only the median). Whether the five-person conflict is a calendar problem or a symptom of managers deprioritizing hiring, in which case a person who owns the process (the recruiter option) would rank higher.
