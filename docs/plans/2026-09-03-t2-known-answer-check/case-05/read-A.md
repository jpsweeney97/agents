Best: **Automate interview scheduling**
Why: The background points at the structural bottleneck: one part-time coordinator booking four stages across six managers who each have only four hours a week, which is how a loop ends up "spread over weeks" and reaches a 71-day median. Letting candidates self-book against live calendars removes that back-and-forth without touching any constraint: no compensation or headcount money, no extra manager hours, and all four compliance stages stay exactly as they are. It also serves the stated values rather than trading against them: quality is untouched, candidates get a faster and more respectful experience, and every candidate goes through the same booking path. It is a tool change that can be live within the three-month window, unlike the agency, sign-on bonus, and full-time recruiter (which spend money the constraints forbid), the 48-hour expiry (which damages candidate experience), or the batched onsite day (which asks more concentrated time from managers than the weekly cap comfortably allows and sits awkwardly with a take-home stage). The runners-up each remove a different lag in the same spirit, but scheduling is the lag the stated facts most directly implicate.
Runners-up, in order: **Put the hiring decision meeting on a standing daily slot**, **Pre-approve offer packages before the final stage**, **Batch the loop into a single onsite day**
Unknowns that would change the choice: The file does not say where the 71 days actually go; if most of the delay is after the final interview (deciding and approving the offer) rather than in booking the loop, the standing daily decision slot or pre-approved offers would beat scheduling automation. If the company already has a scheduling tool and the lag is interviewer feedback, the faster-feedback training would rank higher. If a full-day onsite can be run inside the four-hour manager cap and the take-home stage can sit outside that day, batching would deliver the largest single reduction.
