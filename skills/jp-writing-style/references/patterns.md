# JP Patterns — Rhetorical Moves, Diction, and Genre Adaptation

Load this while drafting, after reading `references/corpus.md`. Every pattern
below is anchored to a real line from the corpus. Use the *construction* the
example demonstrates; do not paste the example phrase verbatim, or it becomes a
recognizable tell. If a move would require asserting a fact the user did not
give you, stop — see the integrity gate in `SKILL.md`.

## Rhetorical moves (one canonical entry each)

Each move lists *when* to reach for it and a real anchor. These are the same
moves that make up the default movement in `SKILL.md`; this is the catalog,
not a separate template.

- **Open on a premise.** State the direction of the piece in the first
  sentence — a thesis, a diagnosis, or a statement of purpose. Optionally follow
  a formal opening with one short emphatic fragment, then return to formal prose.
  > "TerraCog's ambitious 'Project Aerial' was doomed from the start. Literally."
  > "I began my time at my university with the goal of laying the foundation for my dream career as a financial analyst."

- **Define the terms that carry weight.** When a goal or standard is doing real
  work, unpack it before reasoning from it. Pattern: *"X" is not Y; rather, it
  refers to Z.*
  > "'Everyone' is not an arbitrary group of people; rather, it refers to the project's key stakeholders…"

- **Diagnose the root cause.** Name the initiating failure, missing condition,
  wrong assumption, or ambiguity — not just the symptom.
  > "…doomed from the beginning because the project leaders … did not identify and interview all key stakeholders."
  > "the persistence of my problems indicated that the initial diagnosis was not the cause."

- **Trace consequences with concrete specifics.** Make downstream effects
  explicit, and ground them in named people, real quotes, and quantities.
  > "All of this ambiguity surrounding the scope of the project had the consequence of wasting time (1 year) and resources…"

- **Qualify, then sharpen.** Acknowledge the nuance before stating the real
  claim. Pattern: *I don't mean to say X, but rather Y.*
  > "I don't mean to say that they were ever easy, but the shift I experienced was that of manageable to unmanageable."

- **"Not only X, but also Y."** Pair the obvious point with the deeper one.
  > "…showed me not only that it was this condition … but also that I was still the smart and capable person I had always been."

- **Reassess with new clarity.** Begin with one interpretation, then revise it.
  > "As I rediscovered my potential, I realized my performance up until then was not indicative of my true capabilities."

- **Correct with a counterfactual.** Say what should have happened, or would
  happen given the chance. Pattern: *Given the chance to… I would…* / *Had this
  been the case…*
  > "Given the chance to take the lead on Project Aerial from day one, I would prioritize identifying and interviewing key stakeholders…"
  > "Had this been the case, I strongly believe that my academic record would have been consistent with my most recent performance…"

- **Resolve forward with earned confidence.** Close on what follows — a lesson,
  a corrective, or renewed direction — stated plainly and only where the facts
  support it.
  > "I am confident that this program will give me the chance I need to prove that my significantly improved performance is more than an anomaly…"

- **Emphatic fragment (signature, sparing).** A short, blunt fragment after a
  formal sentence, carrying real feeling. Rare and intentional — overuse kills
  it.
  > "Literally." · "Basically, I feel stuff really hard." · "I felt like myself again." · "Whether they're a project manager or not."

- **Moral or character close.** In leadership, responsibility, or growth
  contexts, land the point as a reflection of character.
  > "They are the result of living with integrity, which is something I believe everyone should strive to do. Whether they're a project manager or not."

## Diction

These are JP's actual words, drawn from the corpus — not a generic "sound
smart" list. Use them inside his constructions (the phrase bank above), not
sprinkled to inflate the register. Do not reach for an elevated word the corpus
never uses; when in doubt, check it against the samples.

foundation · trajectory · preliminary · crucial · permeate / permeated
throughout · paramount · cognizant · inefficacy · exacerbate / exacerbated ·
mitigate · utilization · ambiguity / ambiguous · legitimacy · stakeholders ·
scope · constraints · capabilities · contributions · determination · potential ·
integrity · compassion · discouraged · indicative · measurable · significant

Phrase constructions JP genuinely uses (reuse the shape, vary the words):
"laying the foundation", "the direction or trajectory", "permeate throughout",
"a significant degree of confidence", "not indicative of my true capabilities",
"the persistence of [the] problem", "the inefficacy of the [initial/prescribed]
…", "exacerbated the discouragement", "demonstrating respect by recognizing the
capabilities and contributions of …", "integrating my natural talent for … with
my lifelong interest in …".

## Genre adaptation

The skill targets first-person self-presentation. The entries below are the
in-scope genres, plus the analytical sample that grounds the voice.

- **Analytical case write-up — voice-grounding sample, not a target genre**
  (Sample 1): JP's voice was reverse-engineered partly from an analytical case
  write-up that turns into first-person reflection. Its moves — define the
  standard of success first; name specific actors, quotes, and constraints;
  quantify the consequence; close with a concrete counterfactual ("Given the
  chance… I would…") — transfer directly into self-presentation writing. Reuse
  the moves; the skill no longer writes case write-ups or other neutral
  exposition itself.

- **Personal statement / application** (Sample 2): the setback → insight →
  renewed-capability arc — but built strictly on the real timeline. Keep emotion
  sincere and interpreted; close with forward confidence tied to the specific
  program or role. This is the highest-stakes genre: invented hardship,
  diagnoses, credentials, or outcomes are fraud, so the integrity gate governs
  every beat. If the real story has no clean resolution, do not manufacture one.

- **Cover letter / autobiographical narrative**: open on a clear premise about
  who you are or why you fit; trace real experience into consequence and fit;
  hold the formal, reflective register; close on forward direction tied to the
  specific role or audience. Built strictly on real experience — the integrity
  gate governs here too.

- **Short responses** (application or interview answers): preserve the causal,
  reflective movement; compress the reasoning without deleting it; keep the
  formal, purposeful tone. Do not flatten into bullets unless asked.
