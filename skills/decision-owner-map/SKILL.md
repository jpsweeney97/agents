# Decision-Owner Map

A decision without a named owner still gets made — by drift, with nobody accountable. This skill converts a floating decision into a routable packet: what exactly is being decided, whose call it is, what they need to make it well, and what happens when they do — or don't. The router's authority ends at the route: mapping a decision is never making it.

## The moves

1. **Pin the decision in decidable form.** A question an owner could answer in a sentence, with its real options and its deadline or triggering event. "Figure out the partner strategy" is not decidable; "do we advance Partner X to contract drafting this quarter, yes or no?" is. If the ask is still shapeless, say what shaping it needs and stop — mapping a non-decision produces ceremony.
2. **Split bundles.** If different parts of the question would be answered by different authorities, it is several decisions wearing one name. "Should we sign this partner?" may bundle a diligence outcome, a commercial-terms call, and a technical-feasibility call — three owners, three packets, often three different clocks. Split first; map each. The bundle is the most common reason a decision stalls: it has no owner because no one person could own all of it.
3. **Name one accountable owner, with the evidence.** The single person or role whose authority this decision falls under — from a charter, a program framework, an explicit delegation, or precedent (who decided the last one of these). One owner per decision; a committee is a smell — name the chair or the tie-breaker. Cite the basis; mark inference as inference. If ownership is genuinely unclear or contested, that is a finding, not a blocker: the packet routes the *ownership question* to the escalation point instead. Never default ownership to whoever asked, whoever is loudest, or yourself.
4. **Assemble what the owner needs to decide well.** The options with their honest consequences — not steered; the information in hand, with where it came from; the information missing, with who holds it; and the adjacent decisions already made that constrain this one. A starved owner decides badly and slowly; most of routing's value is delivering the decision *fed*.
5. **Cast the supporting roles, minimally.** Who must be consulted before (their input could change the decision) and who must be informed after (their work changes with it) — each name carrying a one-clause why. Consult lists grow by comfort, not need: adding a name is always safer for the router than leaving one off, and every added name is latency — which is exactly why the list needs the why-per-name discipline.
6. **Set the clock and name the drift outcome.** When this must be decided (date or trigger), who unsticks it if the owner stalls (usually the owner's manager or the governing forum) — and what outcome drift produces if nobody decides. Name the drift outcome explicitly: "undecided by March 1 means we miss the launch window" turns an unowned decision from hygiene into stakes. Not deciding is a decision; the packet makes it a visible one.
7. **Pre-wire the conversion.** When the decision lands, what actions fire and who acts. A packet ends with "decision → then what," so the answer becomes coordinated action instead of a meeting memory.

## The packet

One per decision, post-split, compact enough to paste where the routing happens:

```markdown
**Decision:** <the question in decidable form, with options>
**Owner:** <name/role> — basis: <charter / framework / delegation / precedent, or "contested — escalate ownership to <X>">
**Decide by:** <date or trigger> · **If undecided:** <the drift outcome, named>
**Owner needs:** <options + consequences; info in hand (sourced); info missing + who holds it; constraining prior decisions>
**Consult before:** <name — why> · **Inform after:** <names>
**On decision:** <actions that fire, and who acts>
```

## The close

The packet is the deliverable; it routes, and the skill stops there. No verdict on which option should win — that is the owner's call (or a recommendation lane's, if the owner asks for one). No ownership by fiat — a proposed owner carries its basis, and contested stays labeled contested. Close with one honest line naming the packet's weakest point — usually the ownership basis, or a consequence nobody has checked.
