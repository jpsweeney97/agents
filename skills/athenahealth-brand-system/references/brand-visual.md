# athenahealth visual system

Use this reference for visual design decisions. The system should feel simple, structured, recognizable, confident, and human.

## Design foundation

- Positioning: “Curing complexity to simplify the practice of care.”
- Make it simple for people to focus on what matters.
- Use coordinated, restrained choices. Do not add visual elements without a communicative purpose.
- Let content determine which elements are needed; not every piece must use every element.

## Typography

- Primary typeface: Source Sans 3. Always use it for the main typographic system.
- Secondary typeface: PT Serif. Reserve it for subheadings and numerical callouts; it is optional.
- Left-align all text except calls to action when the template specifies otherwise.
- Hierarchy: eyebrow/label → Head 1 → Head 2 → Head 3 → body → CTA. Stats, callouts, and inline links sit outside that sequence.
- Head 1: Source Sans 3 Bold; use large; minimum 22 px digital or 26 pt print.
- Head 2: Source Sans 3 Regular; use large; minimum 22 px digital or 26 pt print.
- Head 3: PT Serif Regular; use smaller; maximum 20 px digital or 24 pt print.
- Eyebrow and inline link: Source Sans 3 Bold; maximum 20 px or 24 pt.
- Body: Source Sans 3 Regular; maximum 20 px or 24 pt.
- Callout: Source Sans 3 SemiBold; maximum 20 px or 24 pt.
- Establish a clear descending hierarchy; do not use every level when the content does not need it.

## Color behavior

Read [color-palette.md](color-palette.md) for exact values and approved tints.

- Hero Purple is the defining color, but it need not dominate every execution.
- Hero Purple, cream, and white should lead. Honey and midnight support them.
- Use Honey sparingly for calls to action and callouts; never as a background.
- Use secondary colors mainly in data visualization or event themes. Never let them overpower the primary palette; do not use them as body-copy or general background colors.
- Use only approved text/background pairings. Avoid low contrast.
- Use approved gradients sparingly for digital or experiential/interactive work and limited presentation cases. Never place gradients inside letterforms.
- Reserve red for genuinely critical states, such as patient-safety or severe business-risk warnings.

## Logo system

- Use only approved artwork. Never reconstruct or alter any logo or lockup component.
- Primary logo: full-color purple logo. If contrast prevents legibility, use full-color white; then all-white. Use all-purple only as a last resort for one-color applications.
- Primary logo minimum height: 12 px or 0.15 in.
- Clearspace: at least the height of the `h` in `health` on every side. More is preferred.
- Preferred placement: top left with equal top and left margins. Footer or end-card placements are exceptions driven by context or the official template.
- Align nearby text and objects to the left edge of the logo leaf, not the wordmark text.
- Never crop, stretch, recolor, place over busy photography, place in a holding shape, or combine logo variants.

### Curing Complexity lockup

- Prefer the lockup for brand-story and in-market campaign moments.
- For evergreen, service-description, compliance, training, text-heavy, or internally focused materials, use the primary logo.
- In multi-page documents or decks, the lockup may lead on first use and the primary logo may appear subsequently.
- Minimum height: 30 px or 0.45 in. Apply the same clearspace principle.

### Restricted variants

- Stacked logo: only for social avatars and unusually constrained applications; minimum 72 px or 1 in. Other use requires brand permission.
- Brand symbol: only for social avatars, app icons, favicons, and limited promotional items. Use only on white, cream, or Hero Purple. Other use requires brand permission.

## Grid and layout

- Use a 4-, 8-, or 12-column grid, depending on complexity and scale.
- Use multiples of four to create balanced, structured layouts.
- Keep top, bottom, left, and right margins equal.
- Maintain a consistent relationship between outer margins and column gutters.
- Typical use: four columns for simple ads or print layouts, eight columns for 16:9 presentations, and twelve columns for dense digital forms.
- Organize content with an obvious hierarchy and generous whitespace.

## Holding shape

- Use curved-edge holding shapes as content windows or information organizers, drawing from the circular forms of the logo.
- Corner radius = shortest side ÷ 10, rounded to the nearest whole unit.
- When multiple shapes appear in one composition, derive the shared radius from the smallest shape.
- A content-showcase holding shape should occupy at least 25% of the composition and be anchored to at least one edge or corner. Never float it arbitrarily in the middle.
- A cropped holding shape may exit only one side and must retain two visible rounded corners.
- Keep equal outer margins and align all shapes to the grid.
- Information-division layouts may use 2–6 shapes. Use common heights or widths and linear alignment anchored to a margin.
- Each shape owns its content; do not let content bleed between shapes.

## Photography

- Style: “In the room where it happens.” Use authentic, photojournalistic moments of care, work, and human connection.
- Prefer candid, specific interactions and thoughtful compositions such as over-the-shoulder views or foreground context.
- Keep imagery simple, natural, warm, inviting, diverse, medically accurate, and emotionally honest.
- Avoid staged stock-photo behavior, unrealistic retouching, cold or washed-out lighting, distracting backgrounds or clothing, and defaulting to smiling subjects.
- Represent diversity across race and ethnicity, age and life stage, gender identity and roles, family configurations, abilities, body types, beliefs, and socioeconomic background without stereotypes.
- Show people with disabilities in active, empowered roles. Focus on people and connection, not conditions.
- Use athenahealth brand elements in scenes sparingly; do not imply that athenahealth itself provides clinical care.
- Do not apply purple overlays or filters. Keep retouching minimal and do not change identifying features or body types.
- Crop only when needed; do not cut off heads or key features.
- Ensure each image supports the message. Use the holding shape as a content window when useful; full bleed is acceptable when immersion is the goal.
- Do not use AI-generated portraits as employee headshots or present generated imagery as approved brand photography.

## Iconography

- Use only approved library icons. New icons require brand-team approval.
- Guiding icons: decorative, navigational, or marketing signposts; 40–100 px (0.5–1.25 in); use on white backgrounds.
- Functional icons: task-oriented product/interface cues; 15–30 px (0.2–0.4 in).
- Use icons to support a message, never as the principal heroic visual.
- Never recolor, redraw, or swap guiding and functional roles.

## Motion

- Motion should be precise, elegant, deliberate, purposeful, simple, and direct.
- Keep animation intensity consistent and use easing as a settling motion.
- Avoid bounces, cartoony overshoots, secondary embellishment, jarring changes, or abrupt endings.
- Use official logo animations only. Do not recreate them.
