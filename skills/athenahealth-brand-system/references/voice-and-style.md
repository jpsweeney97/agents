# athenahealth voice and editorial style

## Voice foundation

Write as a trusted guide: experienced, calm, confident, approachable, and focused on clearing obstacles so others can succeed.

Balance three attributes:

- Courageous: bold, honest, optimistic, proactive, and solutions-focused. Name complexity without blaming or dwelling on it. Do not overpromise, belittle, or act arrogant.
- Credible: specific, evidence-backed, accountable, and clear. Use data and examples. Avoid vague claims, jargon, internal shorthand, condescension, and over-explanation.
- Compassionate: human, conversational, relatable, and audience-aware. Use familiar language and meet people where they are. Avoid sentimentality, assumptions, robotic phrasing, and verbosity.

The voice should feel human, approachable, informative, specific, confident, trustworthy, humble, clear, optimistic, honest, proactive, engaging, bold, and accountable. It should not feel robotic, superior, dominating, salesy, cocky, technical for its own sake, passive, goofy, timid, or fake.

## Writing approach

- Lead with audience benefit and what becomes simpler or more achievable.
- Prefer active voice, concrete verbs, short sentences, and clear transitions.
- Reframe problems toward solutions while remaining realistic.
- Use tactful wit only when it clarifies complexity; never at the audience's expense.
- Use inclusive, specific descriptions. Avoid stereotypes and ableist phrasing. When individual preference is unknown, follow current AP guidance and use respectful person-first language where appropriate.
- Use the Oxford comma.
- Spell out `and` except when space is constrained and in the approved phrase `practice & revenue cycle management`.
- Avoid exclamation points. Let the language convey urgency or enthusiasm.
- Use em dashes with spaces around them.
- Keep bullets short, begin them with capitals, use parallel syntax, and punctuate complete sentences.

## Capitalization and headline punctuation

- Use sentence case throughout.
- Add terminal punctuation to complete thoughts used as hero messages or content-slide headlines, including ad headlines, web hero cards, social tiles, PowerPoint content slides, long-form video statements, and internal communication headlines.
- Omit terminal punctuation from navigation, labels, section headers, subheads, thought-leadership headlines, PowerPoint title/divider slides, video titles/dividers, and other labeling contexts. Retain question marks when appropriate.

## Company and audience terminology

- `athenahealth`: always lowercase. Use the full name in public-facing communication. Use `athena` internally only after introducing `athenahealth`.
- `athenahealth, Inc.`: reserve for legal references to the company.
- `athenista`: lowercase; mostly internal or recruiting use.
- Prefer `customers` over `clients`, but address people as `you` or by their role when possible.
- Prefer `clinicians` for broad medical-professional audiences. When space allows, use `physicians and other clinicians` on first reference.
- Avoid `provider` when addressing medical professionals, except when the audience prefers it, such as behavioral health, or when using established industry/product terminology.
- Prefer `EHR` over `EMR`. Spell out electronic health record on first reference when the audience may need it.
- `healthcare` and `whitepaper` are each one word.
- Use `payer`, not `payor`, unless matching an organization's name.
- Use `Ob-Gyn` and `orthopedics` unless matching a named organization or event.
- Avoid acronyms unless widely recognized; spell them out on first reference. Do not apostrophize plural acronyms.

## Solution hierarchy

- Solution: an integrated package of products and services, such as athenaOne.
- Product: a core software/technology component within a solution.
- Capability: a collection of related features that delivers an overall purpose and value.
- Feature: a specific software element that performs a distinct task.
- Enhancement: an improvement to an existing capability or feature.
- Add-on: an optional purchased service, capability, or feature that extends athenaOne.
- Platform: the underlying technical infrastructure and framework. Do not confuse it with Platform Services.
- Functionality: preferred modifier for product behavior.

## AI terminology

- `AI-native`: describe athenaOne and its three products when the content specifically focuses on AI integration. Do not attach it automatically to every mention.
- `AI-powered`: describe a specific capability or feature that uses AI. Do not describe athenaOne or its overall products as AI-powered.
- `native to athenaOne`: acceptable for athenaOne's AI capabilities as a whole.
- `AI-generated`: preferred phrase for content created or surfaced by AI. Avoid `AI-driven`, `AI-enabled`, and `AI-enhanced` when an approved term fits.
- `athenaOne's Advanced Intelligence Layer`: use the full contextual phrase on first reference.
- `athenaOne's AI-native encounter`: use this context; it describes the opt-in clinical visit and documentation experience, not every AI capability.
- `athenaAmbient™`: add the mark on first external reference; in messaging, describe it as `athenahealth's ambient model`.
- `Sage™`: on first external reference, write `Sage™, athenaOne's AI assistant`. Inside athenaOne, use `Sage`.
- Use the AI-native icon only as approved. It is not a substitute for the athenahealth brand symbol.

## Numbers, dates, and marks

- In prose, spell out whole numbers zero through nine unless showing a range or impact metric.
- Use numerals and the `%` symbol for percentages.
- For impact metrics, round down to the two highest places and add `+` when appropriate: 156,321 → `150,000+`.
- Use `K`, `M`, and `B` for thousand, million, and billion when abbreviating; never use `MM` for million.
- Use AP-style dates: `Wednesday, Sept. 25`; no ordinal suffixes.
- Use `a.m.` and `p.m.`; simplify U.S. time zones to `ET`, `CT`, `MT`, or `PT` where appropriate.
- Write telephone numbers with periods: `617.402.0000`.
- Use postal abbreviations for U.S. states.
- On first external reference, apply the required marks: `athenaOne®`, `athenaPatient®`, `athenaPayer®`, `athenaIDX™`, and `epocrates®`.
- Never use the retired term `athenaNet`.

## Boilerplate

Do not improvise corporate boilerplate when an approved description is required. Use the current official language from the source page listed in [assets-and-sources.md](assets-and-sources.md), or ask the user to confirm the intended approved version.
