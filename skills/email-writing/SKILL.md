# Email Writing

Draft professional emails in JP's default email style: warm but plain, clear without becoming stiff, and human without sounding padded.

This skill owns the text of the email draft. It does not send email, mutate a mailbox, archive threads, label messages, or manage inbox state unless a separate mail-management tool or skill is explicitly invoked and authorized.

## Default Scope

Use this style for professional outreach, scheduling, follow-ups, thread replies, and similar work correspondence.

Do not turn this into a general voice system. If the user is asking for a resume, cover letter, application answer, interview prep, or other career artifact, use the artifact's owning skill first. Use this skill only for the actual email note around that work.

## Before Drafting

Ask one or two clarifying questions before drafting when key details are missing and the answer would materially change the email, such as:

- who the recipient is and what relationship they have to JP
- the concrete purpose of the email
- any date, time, deadline, or scheduling constraint
- what JP wants the recipient to do next
- any sensitive context that would change the level of directness

If only minor details are missing, draft with clear placeholders and note the assumptions after the draft.

## Output Shape

Default to one complete draft, not a menu of full variants.

Include:

1. A plain subject line.
2. The email body.
3. A short note outside the draft explaining the main style choice or offering one or two alternate phrasings.

Keep the note short. It is there to help JP steer the draft, not to analyze the writing.

## Subject Lines

Let the subject line do the practical work. Prefer plain labels over clever, polished, or sentence-like subjects.

Good defaults:

- `Following up on [topic]`
- `Scheduling [meeting/interview]`
- `Quick question about [topic]`
- `Checking in on [topic]`
- `Meeting details for [date/topic]`

Avoid subject lines that oversell the message, carry too much emotion, or hide the purpose.

## Body Structure

Open with a short relationship or context note. The body does not need to make the ask in the first sentence because the subject line already names the purpose.

Then include the practical details, useful context, or request. A little extra context is fine when it makes the email feel grounded and human. Do not clip routine emails down to the bare minimum unless the user asks for a very short draft.

Use `Best, JP` as the default sign-off.

## Tone

Aim for warm but plain.

The email should sound like a real person being easy to work with. It should not sound like JP is performing professionalism.

Allowed when natural:

- `I hope you're doing well`
- `just wanted to`
- `excited` or `thrilled`
- an occasional exclamation point
- a direct thank-you when there is a real thing to thank someone for

Avoid:

- over-polished or formal phrasing
- stacked praise
- repeated gratitude
- generic compliments
- placating, patronizing, or brown-nosing warmth
- filler that exists only to make the email sound friendly

Warmth should be natural and specific. It should not add extra praise or repeated gratitude just to soften the message.

## Asks, Follow-Ups, And Urgency

For first-time outreach where JP wants something from someone, keep the ask softer and more conversational. Prefer wording like `If you're open to it, I'd be glad to...` or `Would it be possible to...` over a hard call to action.

For follow-ups, be clear without sounding clipped or transactional. It is fine to say JP is still interested, available, or happy to send more information when that is true.

When urgency matters, state the deadline plainly. Do not bury the deadline in apology, vague pressure, or extra formality.

## Calibration Examples

Too polished/formal:

> Thank you for reaching out and for your flexibility. Tuesday at 2:00 PM works well for me. I look forward to speaking with you then.

Closer to JP's email style:

> Tuesday at 2 works well for me. Thanks for coordinating.

Too padded:

> Thank you so much again for taking the time to connect, and I really appreciate all of your help and flexibility here.

Closer to JP's email style:

> Thanks again for making the time. I'm looking forward to talking.

Routine follow-up:

> Just wanted to follow up here. Happy to send anything else that would be useful.

Plain deadline:

> I need to send this by Friday afternoon, so it would be helpful to have your notes by Thursday if that works.
